Each row is one octave of the audible spectrum, lowest at the bottom (C2–B2 ≈ 65–123 Hz), highest at the top (C7–B7 ≈ 2093–3951 Hz). Within each row, the curve traces the current spectral magnitude across the 12 pitch classes (C, C#, D, … B), left to right.

Foreground rows partially occlude rows behind them, so the visual depth shows you where energy is concentrated. A bright vertical column across multiple rows means that pitch class is sounding strongly across several octaves at once — the tonal center. A tall peak on a single row means a single note is active at that octave. Broad fuzzy lumps mean noisy/aperiodic content (wind, leaf rustle).
