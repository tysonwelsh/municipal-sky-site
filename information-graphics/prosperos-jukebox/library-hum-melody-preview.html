~ four ways to shape the random ~

Every approach below uses the same Living-Voice synthesis as the production hum — identical timbre, vowel walk, jitter/ shimmer/vibrato. The only thing that changes between cards is how the next pitch is chosen. Phrase intervals are tightened to ~6–10s here so you can evaluate each approach in seconds rather than waiting through production's 25–50s rests.

0. Baseline (current production)

What ships today: a single uniform Markov walk over C Dorian (C D E♭ F G). No phrase shaping, no ending bias. Included for A/B comparison.

1 Markov matrixC Dorianno shape

1. Mood-specific Markov matrices

Each phrase picks one of five named pitch-transition tables: settled (hovers around 2–3 pitches, stepwise), restless (jumps to extremes), descending (next note biased lower), climbing (biased higher), wandering (uniform — the baseline matrix). The Markov is biased differently for each, so the same 5 pitches yield audibly different characters. Most direct analog to your harpsichord's 5-gesture system.

5 matricesone per phrasesame scale

2. Melodic contour templates

Each phrase picks an abstract pitch silhouette — arch (rise then fall), valley (fall then rise), descent (high to low), climb (low to high), hover (stay around one pitch with neighbors). The contour is sampled at each note's position in the phrase to set a target pitch; the actual pick is the target ±0–1 with weighted random. Every phrase has a perceivable shape rather than wandering.

5 shapescontour-anchored picksmost visually “melodic”

3. Mode rotation

Each phrase picks one of four modes built on C, all in the male hum range: Dorian (C D E♭ F G — current, moody), Phrygian (C D♭ E♭ F G — the flat-2 is dark and exotic), Lydian (C D E F♯ G — sharp 4 floats and lifts), and Mixolydian (C D E F G — natural 3, brighter than Dorian). Biggest impact on emotional color; changes the harmonic relationship with the drone.

4 modeschanges scale notesstrongest mood shift

4. Tonal-pull resolution

Baseline Markov walk, but the final note of every phrase is forced toward the tonic (C) or dominant (G) instead of being random. Phrases feel like they arrive somewhere intentional rather than just stopping. Subtle but musical — gives the hum a sense of punctuation.
