Who Was Rabelais?

François Rabelais

c. 1483/1494 – 1553 · French Renaissance humanist, physician, and satirist

  • A Franciscan friar who left the cloister to study medicine in Lyon and Montpellier.
  • Practiced as a physician while writing under the anagrammatic pseudonym Alcofribas Nasier.
  • Author of Gargantua and Pantagruel: a bawdy, encyclopedic, riotously satirical five-book cycle (1532–1564).
  • Repeatedly condemned by the Sorbonne for obscenity and heresy; protected by powerful patrons including King François I.

Book 2, Chapter 30 of Pantagruel sends the scholar Epistemon on a brief tour of Hades, where the great men of antiquity have been reduced to menial trades. The chapter is the data source for everything that follows.

Who Was Diogenes? (An Outlier IRL)

Diogenes the Cynic

c. 412 – 323 BCE · Ancient Greek philosopher

  • Sent into exile after a scandal over the debasement of the currency.
  • Lived in a wine barrel in the streets of Athens.
  • Reportedly told Alexander the Great to “stand out of my sunlight.”
  • Mocked social convention; masturbated in public.

Diogenes was a philosopher who flouted social convention. In life, he could reasonably be described as a low-life, a deadbeat, a bum.

Sources Cited

Data Sources

  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), May 2024. bls.gov/oes.
  • U.S. Department of Labor. O*NET-SOC database (work context, interests, and occupation descriptions). onetonline.org.
  • Rabelais, François. Gargantua and Pantagruel, Book 2, Ch. 30. Urquhart (1693) translation, via Project Gutenberg.

Works Cited

  • Bakhtin, Mikhail. Rabelais and His World. Translated by Hélène Iswolsky. Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1984.

Methodology

  • “Lot in life” entries for each underworld figure were generated by a large language model and reviewed for accuracy.
  • Each figure’s SOC classification was assigned by Claude based on the labor Rabelais ascribes to them in the chapter.
