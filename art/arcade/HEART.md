# HEART.md — what this place is about

Read this before `WORLD.md`. `WORLD.md` is the rulebook: what things are called,
what they cost, what never changes. This page is about why the place
exists and how it should feel. It was distilled from an owner interview
on 2026-09-26 and is deliberately impressionistic: use it to make taste
calls when the rulebook is silent.

---

## The one sentence

**A crummy nickel arcade that re-enchants the world, without letting you
forget it's a crummy nickel arcade.**

## Re-enchantment with a teenager's eye-roll

These places were magic when you were a kid. Even then, you could tell they
were a bit seedy, a bit broken, and there mainly to take your parents' money.
Both things were true at once, and holding both is the whole register.

- The magical realism is **real and never corny**. No utopian glow, no
  wonder-for-its-own-sake, no "the magic was inside you all along."
- The cynicism is **real and never the point**. The machines cheat, the
  carpet is sticky and the prizes are junk. The world is allowed to roll
  its eyes at itself.
- Underneath both is something **earnest**. The joke is a shell, and a
  crack in it should show something tender. Most of the time the world
  plays it cool; once in a while it has to mean it.

## The possum is the method

A possum plays dead until the danger passes. Irony is a way of playing
dead: staying cool so you never have to say what you want. The whole
arcade does this. Its jokes, its junk and its broken bulbs keep the player
at arm's length.

The arc of the larger game, and the small arc inside each cabinet when it
can manage one, is **the possum getting up**: the moment the place stops
being ironic and says something plainly. Those moments must be rare,
small and unannounced, or they turn back into a bit. One true line on a
fortune card is worth more than a cutscene.

## The player

**An outsider passing through, lost at night.** They aren't from the holler
and don't know its history, which is the right distance for meeting it.
When they leave, they take **a way out** and **something they learned about
themselves**, with the courage to say it out loud. That should be embodied
in a physical object, never stated. (What the object is: open.)

## What's under the floor

The mine under the arcade is the **unconscious**: rumoured, then
discoverable, then deeper than it should be. The two poles of the descent:

- **The hope at the bottom:** gold, or something that could save the town,
  or a way to redeem the past. **A restart**: do it all over again. It
  carries all the weight Appalachia and the South carry.
- **The fear at the bottom:** nothing. The abyss. The fact that time only
  runs forward.

**Never resolve these into each other cheaply.** The deeper levels of any
machine can hint at both. Gold should glint, the dark should be really
dark, and neither wins outright.

## Scrip and tokens: the joke on ourselves

Company scrip only counts inside the company store. The arcade runs on
tokens, and so, as it happens, does the machine that helped build it: this
place is partly made with an AI that people pay for **by the token**. The
world knows this about itself. Pink tickets, points and made things may be
worthless outside the building, or they may be what you traded your
nights for.

Handle this lightly: a wink, a double meaning on a plaque, a redemption
counter that is a little too honest. **Never a lecture.** The arcade
doesn't moralise; it just runs the company store and lets you notice.

## The junk drawer

The emotional model for prizes, inventory and "lost things" is a child's
**junk drawer**: the top drawer of the dresser, where pocket things went to
live, with scribbles on the bottom. Nonsense items with enormous private
value. A fortune-telling fish from the neighbours across the street
(the red cellophane one that curls on your palm) belongs in it.

So:
- Prizes are **specific, slightly useless and loved.** Not loot. Not
  stats. A thing you'd keep.
- The inventory should feel like a drawer, not a grid.
- (The site already has a Junk Drawer piece. It's the same instinct, and
  cross-references are welcome.)

## The machine at the end of the hall

At closing, every cabinet goes dark but one. It **hums like a vending
machine**. It's an **oracle** in the fortune-teller tradition (Zoltar, the
penny-arcade card printer, the cellophane fish), but it doesn't tell your
fortune. **It tells you something about yourself**, by asking and drawing
it out, the way an interview does. It's kitschy and low-rent, and it still
works on you.

This is a cabinet waiting to be built, and probably the emotional
centre of the building. Treat it as **the** oracle: other machines can
point toward it but never duplicate it.

## The sensorium

Default palette, soundscape and air for every cabinet and every room:

- **Sound:** coins hitting a plastic bucket. The hum of electricity.
  The lonesome whistle of a distant train.
- **Smell** (to be evoked, not rendered): **stale cigarette smoke.** Put
  it in the haze, the yellowed placards, the burn mark on the cabinet
  lip, the nicotine tint on the backlit glass.
- **Colour:** **dark purple, the purple-black of night.** The room is that
  colour. Everything else is lit against it.

## Lean into / never do

**Lean into:** the tender thing inside the joke; specific objects over
generic ones; the institutional voice (plaques, legends, exhibit cards)
saying slightly wrong things; the sense that the place knows you a little;
rewards you'd keep in a drawer; outsider's-eye views of the holler that
are affectionate, not condescending.

**Never:** corny wonder; a moral stated out loud; mocking Appalachia or
the people in it (the weight of its history is real; the arcade sits
on it, it doesn't sneer at it); irony with nothing underneath; a
"meaningful" moment that announces itself.

## Per-cabinet echoes (so far)

- **HOLLER ROLLER:** the possum presides and plays dead. The machine that
  cheats with a grin: a pure crummy-arcade surface, with the eggs as
  small cracks in it.
- **SCRIP CREEK:** the company store in one glass box. Tokens in, tokens
  out, the animals take their cut.
- **MOTHER LODE:** the descent. The first cabinet about what's underneath.
  Its lost things are the junk drawer's, buried; its jackpot is the hope
  (gold, the vein); its dark sections are the fear. Its rare tier, still
  deferred, is a natural place for the first hint of **a restart**.
