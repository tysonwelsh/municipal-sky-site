## Site notes

### www.filmarchiv.at — FILMARCHIV ON (Austria, German)
- **There is no server-side search, and you do not need one.** `/de/suche?q=` and `/de/search?q=` both 404; `?q=` on the section URL is ignored. FILMARCHIV ON is a single page with the **entire catalogue inlined**: one `GET https://www.filmarchiv.at/de/filmarchiv-on/` (≈ 535 KB) returns **all 561 videos**, and `/de/filmarchiv-on/historische-filmdokumente` returns the same 560. Fetch once, parse, search offline. That is the whole VOD tier, not a page of it.
- Parse shape: split the HTML on `<a href="/de/filmarchiv-on/video/f_…">`; each card carries `<span>YEAR</span>`, `<span>MM:SS</span>` and a `<title-div>`. Carousel duplicates drop the title, so merge by id across both pages. Six lines of regex; my index (561 rows: id, year, duration, title) is in the scout scratchpad as `fa-index.json`.
- Catalogue shape, for anyone coming back: 1896–1994, weighted to 1910–1960 Vienna. About 40 Nazi-era items (1938 "Anschluss" material — handle deliberately). Big seams beyond this round: **Austria Wochenschau**, *Werbefilm* (including a remarkable run of Hans Albala avant-garde advertising), *Amateurfilm*, and a "Vor N Jahren" anniversary series that keeps growing.
- Media: the `FilmArchiv` yt-dlp extractor reads item URLs directly. `yt-dlp -j --no-playlist` on #4 returned `extractor: FilmArchiv`, **6 formats**, HLS ladder with a separate audio track. **`duration` comes back `None`** — take the duration from the catalogue card instead, which is reliable.
- Item pages carry a real German curatorial paragraph plus `Originaltitel / Land / Jahr / Regie / Produktionsfirma / Musik / Quelle / Restaurierte Fassung`. **The Originaltitel is frequently nothing like the site title**, and is usually the better reel name: site "Maria Lassnigs Trickfilm-Studio" → *TRICK FILME – Vom Zeichentrick zur Computergraphik*; "Die Stadt der Zukunft" → *Stadt am Morgen*; "Mit Helmut Qualtinger auf den Mars" → *Fahrt zum Mars*; "Richtig telefonieren!" → *Der Selbstanschluss-Fernsprecher*.
- Licence: every item page ends `Restaurierte Fassung: Filmarchiv Austria © <year>`. The restoration is claimed even where the underlying film is PD by age, so **Tier B throughout** — ≤ 6 windows, never a whole scene, music windows ≤ 10 s. One item to look at again: #32-in-reserve, the 1956 telephone-exchange report, whose `Quelle` is "File von WStLA, mit freundlicher Genehmigung von Wiener Stadt- und Landesarchiv" — a public archive, not the restoration.
- Rate limits: well behaved. 26 item pages at 2 s apart with no throttling; a browser UA is advisable. Nothing in robots.txt obstructed `/de/filmarchiv-on/`.

### openbeelden.nl (Netherlands, Dutch)
- **The site is the slow one and it will eat your budget.** Search is `GET /media?q=<term>` (the form posts; GET works) and takes **70–130 seconds per query**. Worse, running two jobs against the host at once makes searches return *nothing at all* — curl exits with no bytes written, which reads as a 000, not as an error. One query at a time, several seconds between, and do not fetch item pages concurrently with searching.
- **Item pages are fast — 12–15 s.** So the efficient shape is: a small number of searches to harvest ids, then fetch every promising item page directly. Results pages cap at 30 hits with no pagination I could find; an Atom alternate exists (`/feeds/atom/?search=<term>`) but timed out on me.
- Item pages are the best of the three sites. Each gives title, Dutch synopsis, **a full `SHOTS:` list in the cataloguer's own words** (sometimes with durations, e.g. the PHOHI item's `01'03"` / `01'17"`; the Willy Mullens silents carry complete timecoded shot lists), `Publicatiedatum`, `Lengte` (mm:ss), `Maker`, `Bijdragen` (Philip Bloemendal is the commentator on most 1970s items), tags — **and an explicit download block naming every rendition with its file size**: MP4 360×288 and 720×576, WEBM, two OGVs, a source MP4, plus an `.m3u8`. Direct URLs are `https://openbeelden.nl/files/<a>/<b>/<id>.<name>.mp4`, handed to you verbatim in the share block. **No yt-dlp needed anywhere on this site.**
- Licence is printed on the page, per item, and there are two good ones: the Polygoon core reads **"Creative Commons – Naamsvermelding-Gelijk delen" = CC BY-SA 3.0 NL → Tier A**, and a run of late-1930s Polygoon Hollands Nieuws items reads **"Public Domain Mark" → Tier A outright** (#27 and several reserves). Attribution string to carry: "Polygoon Hollands Nieuws (producent) / Nederlands Instituut voor Beeld en Geluid (beheerder)". The other portals on the same host (VPRO, NIMk, EYE, Natuurbeelden) have their own terms — check each.
- Dutch search terms, by yield: **`toekomst`** is the single richest query in the beat (30 hits, and it reaches the whole Polygoon technology seam — it is a curator tag, not just a word); `computer` (9, incl. *Computerkunst*, *Computer tapijten*); `satelliet` (4, and it found *Nederland hoort de kunstmaan*); `wereldtentoonstelling` (15); `Philips` (30); `kernenergie` (4); `raket` (5); `trekking`/`Staatsloterij` (both return the one draw film); `atoom` (1 — it returns *Evoluon*). **Do not spend a query on `elektronisch brein`** — it returns the Italian *cervello* dance series and nothing else. `elektronische muziek` returns the NIMk art tail, not studio footage: **Raaijmakers, Dissevelt and the Natlab are not on Open Beelden under those words**, which corrects one of the round's theme leads for this site.
- Beyond this round: the Polygoon newsreel core here is the same material as the pool's `polygoon-signalen-1933-1974`, but **at the source, Tier A, and with per-item files**. Anything the pool wants from Dutch newsreel should be taken from here rather than from a mirror.

### www.nb.no / api.nb.no (Norway, Norwegian)
- **Correct the source register on one point.** *Filmavisen* the newsreel is **not** in the catalogue API. `title:Filmavisen` returns the trade *magazine* (mediatype `tidsskrift`) and a book; the wartime newsreel survives only as individually catalogued **segments** under *Norsk Films revy* (11 items, 1941, NS propaganda), and the parent issue has no video body. The register's "950 newsreels, free where rights allow" should be read as aspirational. What is actually there is better described below.
- What *is* there and free: aggregating `mediatype` over the whole catalogue gives `film: 11 796`, of which **`contentClasses:public` = 1 872** — that is the free, streamable film tier, and it is mostly Norwegian advertising films, instructional and agricultural film, 1910s–20s Kristiania actuality, and industrial documentary. Also `publicdomain: 30`, `CCBYNC: 57`, `CCBYNCND: 17`, **`CCBY: 5`**, and four **oil-company collections** — `EKOFISK: 96`, `FRIGG: 52`, `DRAUGEN: 21`, `VALHALL: 14` — which are 1970s–90s North Sea industrial film: control rooms, seismic visualisations, corporate video newsreels, decommissioning. `fjernsyn` is only 21 items; the NRK broadcast deposit is not reachable from this API.
- Query shape that works: `https://api.nb.no/catalog/v1/items?q=*&size=100&page=N&filter=mediatype%3Afilm+AND+contentClasses%3Apublic`. Booleans in `filter` work; `aggs=mediatype,contentClasses,license,viewability,year` is how you find the seams. **Free-text `q` is nearly useless here** — it searches titles and catalogue text only, and `datamaskin`, `elektronhjerne`, `kybernetikk`, `atomkraft`, `romfart`, `fjernsyn`, `lotteri`, `automatisering` all return **0** against `mediatype:film`. Page the whole 1 872 and filter the titles offline instead; that is how every Norwegian row above was found.
- **The media URL lives in the IIIF v3 manifest**, not on the item page (which is an Angular SPA the generic extractor cannot touch): `GET https://api.nb.no/catalog/v3/iiif/<id>/manifest` → `items[0].items[0].items[0].body.id` = `https://wow.nb.no/vod/URN:NBN:no-nb_digifilm_<n>_<date>/playlist.m3u8`. The manifest also carries the **rights URI** — this is the only place `publicdomain/mark/1.0` and `licenses/by/4.0` actually appear — the `Omfang` duration, and a long Norwegian **`summary` that is a real shot description**, often detailed enough to write moments from without auditioning. **Where `body` is absent the item is catalogued but not digitised**; check this before queueing anything found in a title list (it is what killed the Elektrisk Bureau telephone film).
- Licence reading: `accessInfo.viewability: ALL` plus `accessAllowedFrom: EVERYWHERE` means NB publishes it free to the world, but `license` is usually still `copyrighted` → **Tier B**. Tier A is only the `publicdomain` (30) and `ccby` (5) items, and those 35 are worth going through in full — several are 1910s Kristiania street, exhibition and aviation film. CC BY-NC and BY-NC-ND items I have treated as **Tier B**, since the round's Tier A definition names only PD, CC-BY/SA and open government.
- Rate limits: by far the cheapest of the three sites. Twelve pages of 100 at 0.4 s apart, then ~35 manifests, with no throttling and no failures.
