# `africa` — video queue, reels round 4
Scout: Opus 5, 2026-09-14. Sites: cvet.org.za (the beat's only Pile A site).
Languages: English, isiXhosa, Afrikaans (Cape Town, 1983–1996).

**Method.** The site's own search is dead — `results.php` on the static mirror returns
a byte-identical 10 520-byte page for every keyword and contains no result links. So the
catalogue was reconstructed from the eleven genre browse pages plus `featured` and
`shorts` (173 full videos + 21 short clips = 194 records), and every one of the 173 item
pages was read one at a time at 2.2 s intervals — the only way to search a site whose
search is broken. 97 of the 173 carry **timecoded shot lists** in the description; those
timings are the `moments` below and are the archive's own, not guesses.

**The honest finding on this round's theme.** CVET holds **nothing** on priority 1
(aleatoric / chance / computer music) and **nothing** on priority 2 (computers,
technology history). Measured across all 172 descriptions: `computer` 0, `fahfee` 0,
`lottery` 0, `random` 0, `dice` 0, `synthesiser` 0, `studio` 0, `satellite` 0,
`telephone exchange` 0. That is not a gap in the scouting; it is what a community video
collective in a township under a State of Emergency pointed its camera at. The nearest
things to priority 1 are Abdullah Ibrahim (Dollar Brand) at a piano and three choral /
drama evenings; the nearest to priority 2 is CVET's own **pilot news broadcast**.

Where CVET *is* rich is **priority 4 — the future as people imagined it** (19 items: a
country arguing its constitution out loud, "the road ahead", "past, present & future",
"a new way of life", post-apartheid economy workshops), and **priority 3** thinly
(one health-screening film). Priority 5 is empty. So the "one third on priorities 1–2"
target cannot be met from this site and is not met here; the queue is weighted to 4, to
media-artifact items (6), and to the raw strangeness the station wants. Ranked accordingly.

## Site notes

**cvet.org.za — Community Video Education Trust, Cape Town**

- **What it is, exactly.** 173 full-length videos (about 62 hours by the published
  durations) plus 21 short clips, all shot by a Cape Town community video collective
  between 1983 and 1996, with a handful of finished films as late as 1996. Genres, with
  counts I confirmed: Speeches 57, Meetings 51, Interviews 42, Demonstrations 35,
  Funerals 12, Celebrations 12, Drama 10, Other 9, Finished films 3, Seminars 3,
  Vigils 2 (items carry multiple genres). 47 items are flagged `featured` by the archive
  and that flag is a decent quality prior.
- **The search does not work — this is the single most important operational fact.**
  `search.php/` renders, but its `results.php/` target on this static mirror returns the
  same 10 520-byte page for every keyword, with the PHP that should print results sitting
  in the source as an unexecuted HTML comment. Any scout who trusts it will conclude the
  site is empty. **Browse by genre instead**; the genre pages are complete, unpaginated,
  and between them cover the whole catalogue.
- **URL shapes.** Trailing slashes are mandatory — `search.php` 302s, `search.php/`
  serves. Listing: `https://cvet.org.za/browse.php/type=genre&genre=<Genre>/sort=title/`
  (also `type=featured` and `type=shorts`; `sort` accepts `title`, `dateasc`, `datedesc`).
  Item: `https://cvet.org.za/displayvideo.php/vid=207-784-<n>/`, shorts at
  `displayclip.php/vid=…`. The `<n>` is not contiguous — it runs 1 to ~419 with large
  gaps — so enumerate from the browse pages, never by counting up.
- **How the media resolves (for the cutter).** Each item page hard-codes a **flat
  progressive MP4**, no HLS, no DRM, no token:
  `https://cvet.org.za/recordFiles/<vid>/<tapeid>-H.264LAN.mp4` — e.g.
  `…/recordFiles/207-784-287/31x009-H.264LAN.mp4`. `yt-dlp -s` resolves all five probed
  items through the **generic** extractor as format id `0`. The `<tapeid>` (`31x009`,
  `11x022`) is the physical tape number and is not derivable from the vid — scrape it
  from the item page. **Five items have no file at all**, their pages printing
  `KoraEmbedMedia lookup [<vid>] with cid [fileuploadmp4] returned invalid media path`
  where the date should be: 207-784-215, -238, -277, -317, -318. Check for that string
  before planning a cut.
- **Where the timings come from.** 97 of the 173 item pages publish a timecoded shot list
  in the description, in `[mm:ss]` or `[mm:ss-mm:ss]` form, written by the archivists —
  "Hani addresses the audience about jobs, housing, overhauling education [03:50-06:15]".
  Item 207-784-123 goes further and publishes a seven-chapter table of contents. Every
  `m:ss` in the queue above is the archive's own; everything marked `timing: audition`
  is a description with no published timing. The item pages also carry **Filmmaker**,
  **People**, **Primary Language(s)** and place/date fields, and the People field names
  who is on screen, often with their later job title attached.
- **Rate limits and etiquette.** No `robots.txt` exists (the host returns a JSON
  `ResourceNotFound` for it, which also reveals the site is served from
  `/msumatrix/public/staticsites/cvet.org.za/` — a frozen static mirror on Michigan
  State's Matrix infrastructure, not the fragile PHP host the source register feared).
  I fetched 173 item pages sequentially at 2.2 s intervals plus 13 listing pages and five
  `yt-dlp -s` probes; no throttling, no errors, every response 200. It is nonetheless a
  dead archive nobody maintains — keep it to one request at a time and do not re-crawl,
  since **`cvet-items.json` in this round's scratchpad already holds every item page's
  parsed metadata** and answers most questions without touching the site again.
- **Licence, read from the page.** `copyright.php/` states: all video content is
  copyright CVET unless otherwise indicated, and *"Copyrighted materials may be reused
  only with appropriate attribution and only for non-commercial, educational use. For
  other uses please contact CVET."* That is an explicit standing permission for
  non-commercial educational reuse with attribution — but it is not a named open licence
  and it carries an NC restriction, so by this round's rule it is **Tier B**: fair-use
  posture, ≤ 6 windows per reel, never a whole song or scene, music windows ≤ 10 s. It is
  the strongest Tier B on the beat, though, and CVET publish a working postal address and
  `cvet@mail.ngo.za` — if any single source on this round is worth a permission letter,
  it is this one, because the terms they already publish are most of the way there. One
  caveat: vid=207-784-217 states outright that segments in it were recorded by **NBC,
  Reuters and BBC**; those are third-party rights inside a CVET tape and must not be cut.
- **What else is there, beyond this round's theme** (the brief asked me to register it).
  CVET is, first, the most complete surviving film record of **Cape Town's townships
  under the State of Emergency**: Crossroads and KTC being burned and bulldozed
  (vid=207-784-39, -37, -352, -34, -35, -394), the Gugulethu Seven funeral in full
  (-262, -202, -139, -160, -137), the Trojan Horse killings and their aftermath
  (-243, -256, -259, -244), the mass funerals with the army standing at the graveside
  (-21, -23, -24, -193, -212, -301). Second, it holds **the founding of the UDF**,
  including the Mitchell's Plain launch and the pre-launch press conference
  (-358, -321, -338, -266, -1, -53). Third, an unusual amount of **Muslim Cape Town** —
  Imam Gassan Solomon at the mosque arguing from the Qur'an against the Tricameral
  Parliament, Islamic funerals at Park Stadium (-321, -206, -70, -244, -193). Fourth,
  a substantial **women's and labour** strand: the Black Sash play, Liz Abrahams and the
  Food and Canning Workers Union, the Crossroads Women's Committee, the SACTWU Human
  Chain Day, the women of Lavender Hill on District Six (-226, -325, -376, -222, -182,
  -219, -228). Fifth — and this is the part a later audio round should note — the site
  is full of **singing**: 35 of the 172 descriptions mention singing or freedom songs,
  eight mention toyi-toyi, and it is nearly always a crowd rather than a stage.
- **Nothing was added from a linked site.** The only outbound links are the two Michigan
  State mirrors of this same collection, which the round's rules exclude as held abroad.
  The register's other 25 African rows remain unreadable or off-limits, so this queue is
  one site deep by the shape of the web, not by the scouting.
