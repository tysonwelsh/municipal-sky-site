# The Russophone and post-Soviet world — native sources register (`russophone`)
Scout: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-14. Languages: Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, Kazakh, Uzbek, Georgian, Armenian, Azerbaijani, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian. Territories: Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Central Asia, the Caucasus, the Baltics, Moldova.

**Read this first.** Two structural facts shape this whole beat, and the video round
will waste days if it does not know them:

1. **A large share of the post-Soviet state web refuses US traffic outright.** Not
   geo-blocked-with-a-message — refused at the socket. Confirmed today from this
   machine: `culture.ru` ECONNREFUSED, `kfdz.kz` ECONNREFUSED, `archive.tvr.by`
   read-timeout on both yt-dlp and WebFetch, `1tv.ge` + `filmfond.az` +
   `tsdaea.archives.gov.ua` HTTP 403 (Cloudflare). Several more have broken TLS
   (`rgakfd.ru` unverified chain, `archive.nku.org.ua` expired cert,
   `nostalgiatv.ru` self-signed). **`readable` below records what happened from a
   US egress; it is not a verdict on the site.** The rows marked `egress` in
   `etiquette` need re-probing from elsewhere before anyone concludes they're dead.
2. **The Baltics are the readable half of this beat and Russia is the unreadable
   half.** ERR, LRT and LSM all have working yt-dlp extractors, free public
   players, and deep 1960s–1990s holdings. If the video round wants reels out of
   this beat *this month*, it should start in Tallinn, Vilnius and Riga, not Moscow.

## Top 5 — scout these for videos first

1. **https://arhiiv.err.ee/** — the only site in the beat that is *simultaneously* free, native, deep in the 1950s–90s, and read perfectly by an existing extractor. Probe returned HLS + DASH first try. Start here; it will produce reels the same day.
2. **https://www.e-kinas.lt/** — ~1 000 Lithuanian documentary works **1919–1961**, free, published by the state archive. The highest deep-cut score attached to a genuinely open collection anywhere in this register. Needs a browser-grade session to get past Cloudflare; worth it.
3. **https://1tv.ge/gpbarchive/for-everyone/** — Georgian state television film from **1956**, 35 mm and 16 mm, from the pre-videotape era, published by the rights holder under "Archive for Everyone". Unread today only because of a Cloudflare 403. Highest upside-per-hour in the beat.
4. **https://www.redzidzirdilatviju.lv/** — 1 100+ watchable films out of 7 700 records, 1910–2017, including a 600-film amateur/home-movie fund. Type 7 barely exists anywhere else in this locale, and 8 mm Latvian streets are as close to "a face looking into the camera" as this beat gets.
5. **https://mediateka.suspilne.media/** — raw, unedited Ukrainian broadcast tape from the 1950s on, ~1 500 items and growing, published nowhere else. **Blocked on an owner decision:** viewing requires a free account, and this round's rules forbid logins. *Runner-up worth naming:* **https://vk.com/video** — not a curated source, but the largest reservoir of never-re-uploaded post-Soviet tape in existence, and yt-dlp reads it. It rewards a human with search terms, not an automated pass.

## Search notes

**What worked, per language.**
- **Russian.** `заставки`, `уход с эфира`, `настроечная таблица`, `реклама 90-х` reliably land on staroetv.su and its forum; `кинохроника` + `киножурнал` land on net-film and РГАКФД; `архив` + the institution's name is the only way to separate a real archive from a streaming aggregator. The phrase **`опубликованные кинодокументы`** is what net-film uses for "actually watchable", as opposed to catalogued.
- **Ukrainian.** `кінохроніка`, `архів`, `медіатека`, `оцифровані` work. Search `Суспільне Медіатека` by name — generic searches drown in news articles *about* the archive rather than the archive.
- **Belarusian/Russian (BY).** `архіў кінафотафонадакументаў` finds БДАКФФД; `архив Белтелерадиокомпании` finds the `archive.tvr.by` subdomain, which is invisible from `tvr.by` itself.
- **Baltic.** The single highest-yield terms in the whole beat: **`arhiiv`** (ee), **`arhīvs`** + **`zelta arhīvs`** (lv), **`archyvas`** + **`paveldas`** (lt). `paveldas` ("heritage") is the word LRT files its old material under and it is not obvious from outside.
- **Georgian.** `არქივი` + `კინოჟურნალი`; the Georgian National Archives split their catalogue onto a separate host (`kinokatalogi.archive.gov.ge`) that the main site only links to once.
- **Kazakh.** `мұрағат` (Kazakh for archive) finds far more than the Russian `архив` does on .kz domains.
- **Armenian/Azerbaijani.** `պահոց` (vault) beats `արխիվ` on 1tv.am; `Dövlət Film Fondu` and the series name **`Mozalan`** are the productive Azerbaijani terms.

**What a video scout must know before going in.**
- **Egress is the binding constraint, not availability.** Six sites in this register refused or timed out from a US IP today, and three more served broken TLS. Before concluding any `.ru`, `.kz`, `.by` or `.ge` site is dead, re-probe from somewhere else. I have marked every such row `egress` in the `etiquette` column.
- **Cloudflare is on the good stuff.** e-kinas.lt, 1tv.ge and filmfond.az all challenge bare fetches. yt-dlp's own error names the fix (`--extractor-args "generic:impersonate"`); this round did not use it, and a video round should decide deliberately whether to.
- **Several of these sites are JS-rendered and return an empty page to a plain fetch** — redzidzirdilatviju.lv and kinokatalogi.archive.gov.ge both did. Their holdings are real; the fetch method was wrong.
- **The institution/viewing-layer split is the defining trap of this beat.** Over and over: a genuine native archive exists, and its public player is YouTube. I rejected every such viewing layer by rule but registered the parent where the parent holds anything of its own. Expect the video round to keep hitting this.
- **Two named series are worth chasing by name alone**, because no aggregate search will surface them: **"Mozalan"** (the Azerbaijani satirical newsreel, filmfond.az) and **"Sovietscaia Moldavia"** (231 Moldovan newsreels, not yet online). Both are exactly the register's idea of a caught signal.
- **Holes in this beat, stated plainly:** Uzbekistan and the rest of Central Asia beyond Kazakhstan have **no archival video online** that I could reach. Moldova has a digitisation programme but no published home yet. Armenia and Azerbaijan publish almost entirely to YouTube. Belarus publishes a catalogue and keeps the films in the reading room. If the owner wants those territories in the pool, they will need a request or a partner, not a fetch.
- **Audio (type 10, deferred) — one line as instructed:** `staroeradio.ru` and `retro-archive.ru` are both live, native, and large. retro-archive.ru is specifically digitising the **Soviet State House of Radio Broadcasting and Sound Recording** phonogram fund, and gtrf.ru holds the radio master fund. Worth a scout when the audio tier opens.
