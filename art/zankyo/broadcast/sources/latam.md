## Search notes

**What worked, per language.**
- Spanish: the productive nouns are institutional, not descriptive. `cineteca` + country, `filmoteca` + country, `archivo audiovisual` + country, `acervo` + `en línea`, `patrimonio audiovisual`. Adding `ver en línea` or `catálogo` separates the archives that publish from the ones that only exist. `noticiero` is the single highest-yield content word in this beat — nearly every national archive catalogues its newsreels under it.
- Brazilian Portuguese: `acervo`, `cinejornal`, `vinhetas antigas`, `propagandas antigas`, `encerramento`. `acervo` + institution gets further than `arquivo`, which collides with file-storage results.
- Broadcast-ephemera words that found things: `carta de ajuste`, `cierre de transmisión`, `tanda comercial`, `comerciales`, `cortinilla`. The Dominican AGN is the only state archive found that catalogues **comerciales** as a holding type in its own right — that row is worth a video scout's whole day.
- Indigenous-language searching: the broadcasters are found through the *state* institution, not by searching the language. `Ñuqanchik` (Quechua) and `Jiwasanaka` (Aymara) lead to IRTP; `archivo etnográfico audiovisual` leads to INPI; `cine indígena` + `comunicadores` leads to CEFREC/CAIB in Bolivia. Searching in Quechua or Guaraní directly returned language-learning sites, not archives.

**Technical field notes for a video scout.**
- `yt-dlp` knows almost nothing here. Of 26 rows only Globo (and the RTVC/Señal Colombia/Caracol/Canal1/BioBioChile family, none of which are the *archive* sites) have extractors. Twenty-four rows are on hosts the tool has never seen. Expect to read players by hand.
- The one clean hit: `senalmemoria.co/piezas/…` resolves under the **generic** extractor. Start any tooling work there.
- Hosts that refuse a plain fetcher: `agn.gob.do` (Cloudflare challenge — needs `--extractor-args "generic:impersonate"`), `irtpplay.gob.pe` (403 to a bare UA), `inpi.gob.mx` (broken TLS chain). None of these are geo-blocks.
- Hosts unreachable from a US IP altogether: `catalogo.senalmemoria.co` (connect timeout), `cinetecavirtual.uchile.cl` (HTTP-only, read timeout), `cinematecanacionalcce.com` (connect timeout), `repositorio.inah.gob.mx` (connect timeout). Retry from a Latin American exit before writing any of them off.
- Geo-fenced by design: `retinalatina.org` serves most titles only inside Latin America and the Caribbean.
- Where a landing page could not be made to yield an item URL, the probe was run against the landing page and the `readable` column says so. Landing-page "Unsupported URL" means generic found no media in the HTML — i.e. a JS-driven player — not that the site has no video.

**Dead ends, so nobody repeats them.**
- Haiti has no native online audiovisual archive. The national archives (dloc-hosted, US) are paper; RTNH publishes nothing historical; the substantial Haitian broadcast collection is Radio Haiti at Duke — audio, US-hosted, out of scope twice over.
- Venezuela: the Cinemateca Nacional's 85 000 reels are real and entirely offline; its only web presence is a YouTube channel.
- Paraguay: the Archivo Nacional de Asunción holds digitised film 1905–1938 but publishes no player; `consulta-en-linea` is a document catalogue. SNT/Radio Nacional broadcast in Guaraní but keep no public archive. Nothing registerable found.
- Puerto Rico: the Fundación Luis Muñoz Marín's Archivo de Imágenes en Movimiento (filmoteca since 1987, plus the Emmy-winning WIPR programme *Archivo s.20* built from it) is a genuine holding, but luismunozmarin.org publishes catalogue text only, no media. Worth a letter; not a row.
- Mexican commercial television (Televisa/XEW) keeps no public archive; what survives publicly is on collector YouTube channels. Fotográfica.mx holds Televisa *stills*, not moving image.
- The Museo del Cine in Buenos Aires (90 000 reels) lost its online catalogue when `acceder.gob.ar` went dark — check whether it has moved under `buenosaires.gob.ar` before the video round.

**One audio archive tripped over, noted per the brief:** `staroeradio`-equivalent for this beat is Mexico's **Fonoteca Nacional** (fonotecanacional.cultura.gob.mx) — XEW radio 1944–2011, catalogued and consultable. Type 10, deferred, but it is the biggest sound archive in the region and worth the audio pass when it comes.
