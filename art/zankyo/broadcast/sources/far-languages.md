Coordination notes: **KNR (Greenlandic)** is registered here — the germanic-nordic brief also names it; cartographer should keep one row. **Rai Ladinia / Rai Südtirol** and **CCMA / TVG** touch romance-west; registered here because the Ladin, Catalan and Galician *language* holdings are this beat's. **Spielberg Jewish Film Archive** touches mena; registered here for its Yiddish holdings only.

## Top 5 — scout these for videos first

1. **https://www.isuma.tv/** — a real native video host (own player, HLS in the page), Inuktut, community-run since 1990, with IBC and Igloolik NITV material from the 1980s–90s. Best match in the beat for the vibe *and* technically reachable. Use a long socket timeout; the CDN is slow.
2. **https://uluulu.recollectcms.com/** — the only site in this beat whose representative page **yt-dlp actually resolved a format on**. Hawaiʻi's official state moving-image archive, full of 1960s–90s island television, on its own Recollect player.
3. **https://gwylio-gwrando.llyfrgell.cymru/** — 13,000 Welsh broadcasts watchable from home out of 377,000 catalogued, back to the 1920s, including the whole ITV Cymru library and S4C from 1982. The largest untouched minority-language vault in the register. The Cloudflare 403 is the obstacle to solve, not a reason to skip it.
4. **https://artxiboa.eitb.eus/eu** + **https://pasouoquepasou.crtvg.gal/** — two Iberian minority-language broadcasters that opened their own archives rather than posting them to YouTube. ETB1 from 1982 and TVG from 1985: idents, news openings and continuity in Basque and Galician, from a standing start.
5. **https://www.cinematheque-bretagne.bzh/** — 6,546 films free online, amateur and professional, 1920s–1990s Brittany, with a Breton-language programme inside the institution. The "kitchen and the parade" tier, and nothing of it has been re-uploaded.

Runner-up worth a look the moment a mainland-China fetch path exists: **http://www.vtibet.cn/** — the pool has 14 Tibetan reels and not one from Tibetan television's own house.

## Search notes

**What worked, per language.** The pattern that paid off everywhere was *the language's own word for archive, next to the broadcaster's or the library's name* — `archif` (cy), `cartlann` (ga), `tasglann` (gd), `arkiiva` (se), `artxiboa` (eu), `arxiu` (ca), `arquivo`/`pasou o que pasou` (gl), `argyf` (fy), `archiw` (hsb), `archiv` + `rumantsch`, `pūranga` (mi), `ᐊᒡᕕᐊᓖᑦ` / `Isuma` (iu), `архив` (mn), `ཡིག་ཚགས` and `བརྙན་འཕྲིན` (bo), `档案` + `点播` (zh for the PRC minority channels). Searching the *broadcaster acronym plus the native archive word* beat searching the language name every time.

**The structural finding of this beat.** Minority-language archives split cleanly into two kinds, and the split decides everything:
- **A national library or state archive digitised the broadcaster's tape** (Wales, Greenland, Hawaiʻi, Aotearoa, Okinawa). These have real 1930s–1990s depth, run their own player, and are unknown to yt-dlp. This is where the deep cuts are.
- **The minority-language channel is young** (S4C 1982, ETB1 1982, TV3 1983, TVG 1985, TG4 1996, Māori TV 2004, Brezhoweb 2006, Uvagut 2021). Their whole archive starts late — but *the first years of a channel that started from nothing* are exactly the register the station wants: a test card and an ident in a language that had never had one.

**Three languages are audio, not video, and the round will miss them if nobody says so.** Scottish Gaelic (Tobar an Dualchais, 50,000+ recordings from the 1930s), Conamara Irish (cartlann.ie), and Navajo (KTNN/KNDN) have deep native archives that are entirely sound. CBC North's Indigenous Language Archives project is digitising radio in ten languages from the 1950s. **When type 10 opens, start there** — it is the richest audio tier in the whole scout round and none of it is on an Anglophone host.

**Dead ends and traps.**
- *Vimeo is the minority-language default.* Euskadiko Filmategia and ʻŌiwi TV both put their public video on Vimeo. The institution is native; the host is not. Register the catalogue, reject the Vimeo.
- *WAFs are the real gate, not licensing.* Four of the best sites (Welsh clip player, iMuseum, Cinémathèque de Bretagne, Tibet TV) returned 403 to an ordinary fetch — Cloudflare, F5/ASM, plain UA filtering. None of them are paywalled or DRM'd. A video scout needs an impersonation-capable fetch before it needs permission.
- *Mainland PRC minority channels (vtibet, XJTV, NMTV) 403 or hang from abroad* — vtibet pulls a dependency from `www.zhufengyunxz.cn` that never answers. CURATE.md's Bilibili 412 workaround is the nearest field note; a Tibetan/Uyghur/Mongolian fetch will need the same class of trick.
- *Aggregators point home, as the plan said.* Nuohtti (32 European institutions' Sámi holdings) and the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek entry for the Sorbische Kulturarchiv were the fastest way to find where footage physically is. Neither hosts video; both are maps.
- *Two sites 404 on their own archive index* — MNB (mnb.mn/archive/search/...) and CCMA's collection 90. Navigate from the site root; do not trust a URL a search engine hands you for these.

**The ethical gate is stricter than the licence gate in two places.** Ngā Taonga's Māori taonga carry kaitiakitanga conditions — reuse means talking to the iwi or whānau, not reading a terms page. IsumaTV and IBC operate community protocols on ceremonial material. Both are Tier B legally and something stronger than Tier B in practice; a video scout should approach them through their access teams. The Ainu-language archive states its no-scraping rule in plain Japanese on the page.

**One flag for the owner, not resolvable by me.** XJTV's Uyghur-language channels (and to a lesser degree vtibet and NMTV) are PRC state broadcasters in territories under documented repression. Technically they are exactly the deep-cut native source this round is for: Uyghur-language idents and news openings exist nowhere else. Whether the station should receive a signal from a state broadcaster in that position is the owner's call, and I have registered it rather than deciding it.
