## Top 5 — scout these for videos first
1. **https://cvet.org.za/** — the only site on this beat that is already readable (`yt-dlp -s`
   resolved a format), 90 hours of 1985–94 Cape Town community video in Xhosa, Afrikaans and
   English, public player, nothing re-uploaded. Start here; it is the whole reason this beat
   is not empty.
2. **https://arquivos.rtp.pt/colecoes/africa-colonial/** — 120+ items 1938–1974 of the
   Lusophone-African broadcast record, native player, owner-sanctioned. Lourenço Marques,
   Angola 67/70, Mozambican independence night. Needs a page-level HLS pull, not an extractor.
3. **https://www.tchiweka.org/** — an African liberation archive run from *inside* Africa with
   film and audio on its own portal, in Portuguese. Deep-cut 5, and small enough that a polite
   email would probably open it.
4. **https://www.sudanmemory.org/** — the Sudanese National Film Archive digitised: 1940s–60s
   Khartoum newsreel, a texture nothing in the pool has. Needs a browser-grade visit (it 403s
   automated fetchers) before anything is planned.
5. **https://ifcinema.institutfrancais.com/fr/catalog/afr** — 600+ titles of African cinema
   explicitly cleared royalty-free for non-commercial screening. The only large Tier-A block of
   African film anywhere, even if the platform is Paris-run and needs an institutional account.

Runners-up worth an email rather than a fetch: **archives.gov.zw** (250 digitised hours of
Central African Film Unit newsreel, 1948–63) and **ubc.go.ug** (the UNESCO-listed Uganda
National Media Archive, 1947–86).

## Search notes

**The one finding that matters.** yt-dlp's 1 752 extractors contain **zero** sub-Saharan African
hosts. That is not an oversight in the tool — it reflects the web. Across ~40 broadcasters and
archives checked, the sub-Saharan pattern is: the broadcaster has a website, the website has a
video section, and the video section is a wall of YouTube embeds (RTB Burkina and NTA Nigeria,
both verified by fetch). Africa did not build native video platforms; it moved straight to
YouTube and Facebook. So "native source" on this beat almost never means "a native video host";
it means **a native *archive* with its own player**, and those are rare and small.

**Where the material actually is, in four tiers.**
1. *Readable now, natively hosted:* one site — cvet.org.za. That is the whole tier.
2. *Native player, needs a page-level HLS pull rather than an extractor:* arquivos.rtp.pt
   (Lusophone Africa), tchiweka.org (Angola), genocidearchiverwanda.org.rw (Rwanda),
   crtv.cm/replaytv (Cameroon), sudanmemory.org (Sudan, once past its bot rule).
3. *Digitised but not published — an email, not a fetch:* Zimbabwe's Central African Film Unit
   (≈250 h digitised), the Uganda National Media Archive (1947–86, UNESCO-listed), Nigeria's
   NFVSA Jos (20 000 works), South Africa's NFVSA Pretoria, Namibia's National Archives
   (≈450 films incl. SWABC), Kenya National Archives (13 M items digitised, paid portal
   announced), Ghana Film Archive, the Cinémathèque Africaine de Ouagadougou, the recovered
   Cinémathèque nationale du Sénégal. **This is by far the largest tier**, and it is the
   difference between this beat and the European ones.
4. *Held abroad:* Tervuren (Congo), colonialfilm.org.uk (British Africa), MSU/Matrix (South
   Africa), Criterion/MoMA (Sudan), Durham (Sudan). Rejected by rule, listed so nobody re-finds them.

**Terms that worked, per language.**
- *Portuguese* was the most productive language on the whole beat: `arquivo` + `acervo` +
  `audiovisual` + a country name reliably finds real portals — `África colonial portuguesa`,
  `luta de libertação`, `Kuxa Kanema`, `Associação Tchiweka`. Portuguese-African institutions
  publish; anglophone and francophone African ones mostly do not.
- *French*: `archives` alone returns broadcaster replay pages, which are worthless. What works
  is `cinémathèque` + a city (`Ouagadougou`, `Dakar`, `Bamako`), and `actualités` for newsreel.
  `fin des programmes` / `anciennes publicités` returned nothing native at all.
- *Afrikaans*: `toetsbeeld`, `ou advertensies`, `argief`, `uitsaai` return Wikipedia, Netwerk24
  listicles, and Springbok Radio (audio, deferred). **There is no Afrikaans TV-nostalgia
  collector site with its own video** — the community lives in Facebook groups and on YouTube.
  The Afrikaans/Oshiwambo tape that exists is in Pretoria (NFVSA) and Windhoek (NAN), off-line.
- *Swahili*: `nyaraka`, `kumbukumbu`, `hifadhi` reach the national archives (nyaraka.go.tz),
  which hold **no moving image**. TVZ Zanzibar — sub-Saharan Africa's first colour station,
  1973 — has a broadcaster site that returns HTTP 500. Swahili television history is
  effectively unindexed.
- *Amharic* (`መዝገብ ቤት`, `የቀድሞ ማስታወቂያ`), *Hausa* (`taskar`, `tsofaffin`), *Yoruba*
  (`ilé ìpamọ́`, `ìpolówó àtijọ́`), *Somali*, *Tigrinya*: returned no native archive site at all.
  The only Hausa-language platform of any size is AREWA24's OTT (2014→, subscription).
- *English* as an African language was worth searching in South Africa only, where the
  university and NGO archives (CVET, Mayibuye, DISA, SAHA) are the real tier.

**Dead ends worth not repeating.** Three pan-African VOD platforms that every article still
cites — africafilms.tv, afrocinestream.com, africafilmlibrary.com — have all let their domains
lapse. African VOD churn is brutal; verify DNS before writing anything down. Several archive
sites (sudanmemory.org, disa.ukzn.ac.za, archive.nelsonmandela.org) return **403 to datacentre
IPs** — they are alive and public, just hostile to automated fetchers; a browser visit is the
right next step, and scraping them would be exactly the wrong one.
cinemathequeafricaine.org has a **broken TLS chain**; archives.gov.zw is frequently down.
Budget for flaky infrastructure, and never retry in a loop.

**Radio (type 10, deferred) noted in one line as instructed:** the single richest audio holding
found was Radio Mogadishu's 200 000+ tapes (70 years, UNESCO-backed digitisation, <30 % done);
Senegal, Zambia (ZNBC) and Uganda (UBC) all have substantial sound reels in the same
institutions listed above, so the audio pass can re-use this register's contacts wholesale.

**For the video scout, before going in.** Nothing on this beat is a bulk-fetch target except
cvet.org.za. Everything else is either (a) a page-level HLS pull from a small server that will
notice, or (b) an institution to write to. Three of the top five are run by fewer than ten
people. Etiquette here is not a formality — it is the only way any of this material gets out.
