THE SOURCE

Henry Dreyfuss's Honeywell T87 "Round" (1953) put the whole mechanism behind glass: a wide gold-anodised ring is itself the setpoint dial, turning to bring one of its own printed numbers under a fixed window; behind it a flat bimetal coil unwinds with room temperature, carrying a small glass vial with a bead of mercury at its free end. As the coil turns, the vial tilts until the bead rolls across and bridges two wire contacts — no spring, no click, just gravity and a bead of metal closing a heating circuit.

The tells kept here: the ring's own printed 50–90 scale turning past a stationary window, rather than a pointer moving over a fixed dial; the flat spiral of blue-grey bimetal with a copper edge; the glass vial riding tangent to the coil's free end, its bead rolling to the contact end with a small delay and a settling wobble when the room is colder than the setpoint; a soft amber glow at the base while it is "calling"; the clear cover disc with its own small room-thermometer scale and red pointer, removable with the cover attribute to show the works; the brass anticipator slider at the coil's centre.

Left out: the wall plate and mounting screws, the true two-thermometer (heat/cool) variant, and the exact Dreyfuss typeface — substituted with a close condensed sans.
