THE SOURCE

To the right of the two manuals on a Hammond B-3 (and its church-going twin the C-3) sit six long phenolic tilt tablets in a walnut rail: two black ones for the vibrato and four ivory ones for the percussion. They are not switches that stand up — they are see-saws. Each one is engraved with a different legend on each half, and the half you push down is the half you are turning off; the half left standing is the state the organ is in. VIBRATO SWELL over OFF, PERCUSSION ON over NORMAL, DECAY FAST over SLOW, HARMONIC THIRD over SECOND.

The tells kept here: the see-saw geometry is real — the tablet is a plane rotated ±12° about its middle and projected from the console's viewing angle, so the raised end lifts, the depressed end sinks below the rail's surface and is cut off by the near lip of the slot, and the whole tablet lengthens or shortens on screen exactly as a tilted plank does. The far ends are a little narrower than the near ends (the console's tilt). The gloss is Hammond gloss: one long soft highlight running down the raised half and a sharp bright line along the bevelled near end. The legends are engraved in two short lines per half, in a Gothic condensed, filled white on the black tablets and black on the ivory. The ivory has yellowed at the near ends where hands rest. Walnut rail, a ~2 mm gap and a dark slot round every tablet, and the ivory key-ends of the upper manual peeking past the left edge of the first one.

Left out: the drawbars and the preset keys, the felt bushing you can see if you lift a tablet out, and the wiring bus underneath. Click either half to push it down, drag across the row with the button held to flip a run of them, Space or Enter toggles.
