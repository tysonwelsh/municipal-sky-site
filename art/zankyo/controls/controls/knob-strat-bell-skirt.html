THE SOURCE

The Stratocaster knob has not changed since 1954: a small white bell of moulded plastic with a flared skirt, a concave waist and a flat top a good deal smaller than the skirt. The numerals 1 to 10 are printed on the slope of the skirt, and VOLUME or TONE across the top. There is no pointer; you read the number that faces you. The knob sits on a three-ply pickguard whose bevelled edge shows the black middle ply as a hairline.

The tells kept here: the bell silhouette drawn as a solid of revolution seen from nearly overhead, so the front skirt is a little taller than the back; each numeral is placed on the cone by angle and projected with its own affine matrix, so it stands upright at the front, lies on its side at 3 and 9, and is squashed and inverted at the back; concentric turning rings and one soft highlight on the top; the numerals at 5, 6 and 7 rubbed thin by a decade of thumbs; the skirt yellowed warmer than the top, with a finish="fresh" attribute for a new knob; a faint wear mark on the pickguard at 12 that serves as the index; the pickguard's bevel with the black ply, and one of its screws. Drag is vertical (up is clockwise), the wheel steps, arrows step and Shift-arrows step by ten.

Left out: the split-shaft pot's D-flat inside the knob, and the mould's parting line on the skirt.
