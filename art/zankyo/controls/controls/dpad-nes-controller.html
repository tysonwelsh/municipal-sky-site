THE SOURCE

The NES-004 control pad put a single moulded cross where two arcade sticks used to be. The cross is a matte black ABS plus with square ends, a low arrow ridge moulded into each arm and a shallow dish at the middle; it sits about a millimetre below the surface in a round charcoal recess, rocking on a pivot under the dish so that only one arm — or two, for a diagonal — can go down at a time. The brick around it is two-tone grey with a hairline parting line where the two shells meet, and the right-hand half is a glossy black overlay with fine silver rules, two rubber pills and two red domes.

The tells kept here: the four equal arms with a dished, never-moving centre; the moulded arrow on each arm, lit on the edge that faces the light and dark on the edge that does not; the press that tilts the cross about its pivot — the pressed arm drops and darkens, the opposite arm rises and catches a grey highlight, and a diagonal tilts two arms by half; the recess ring shadowed hardest at the top-left because the light comes from there and the cross lives in a hole; matte black that takes a broad grey highlight and never a white one. In full the overlay's silver rules, the pills in their lighter grey well with SELECT and START printed beneath, and the red domes in their black squares with the red A / B legends. Pointer, arrow keys (two at once give a diagonal) and the wheel all drive it; ways="4" throws the diagonals away.

Left out: the cable and its strain relief beyond a stub, the screw bosses on the back, the exact Nintendo wordmarks (a generic legend stands in), and the rubber membrane contacts under the cross.
