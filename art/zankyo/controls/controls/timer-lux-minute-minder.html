THE SOURCE

The Lux Minute Minder is the white drum on the kitchen counter: a painted steel can whose top half you twist clockwise to wind the clockwork, printed 0 to 60 around a sloped shoulder, a chrome ring where the halves meet, and a red pointer on the fixed base. It creeps back anticlockwise as it runs and rings a bell at zero. Lux Clock of Waterbury made them from the 1930s; Robertshaw kept making them into the 1970s.

The tells kept here: the numerals are on the moving part and the red index on the base stays still; the numerals and minute ticks sit on the sloped shoulder and are foreshortened twice, by the cylinder (they crowd at the sides) and by the slope; the chrome rim is a hard, bright band; the painted steel has one soft highlight from above-left and a band of reflected light on the far right; a chip on the base's bottom edge shows steel. Dragging follows the angle around the top; the wheel and arrows step a minute; the countdown runs in requestAnimationFrame with a tick every second and stops when the element leaves the page; at zero the drum shakes and emits ring. A paused attribute holds it.

Left out: the "LUX" script on the top, the wind-up ratchet click, the bell's sound.
