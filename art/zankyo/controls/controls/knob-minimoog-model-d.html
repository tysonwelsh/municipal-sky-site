THE SOURCE

The Minimoog Model D knob is the plainest knob on the most famous panel in synthesis: a skirtless black cylinder, a little wider at its base, with a shallow cone for a top and a single white line laid in a moulded groove that runs from the centre of the cone over the edge and a few millimetres down the side. Nothing is printed on the knob. The scale — 0 2 4 6 8 10 with a tick at every unit over 300°, or −5 to +5 for the cutoff — and the bold legend above it are silk-screened in white on the matte black aluminium panel, and between the knob and the panel you can see the bright nickel nut and lock washer that hold the pot.

The tells kept here: the taper (the side shows as a ring around the cone from above); the cone, shaded round the clock so it reads as a cone and not a disc, with a crisp highlight on its top-left rim — black on black, the knob is nothing but its rim and its line; the line continuing over the edge; the hex nut and split washer peeking out below the base; the every-unit ticks ending at 7 and 5 o'clock; the legend in a bold Helvetica-like grotesk; a faint greasy ring where fingers land, off-centre toward below-right; and the walnut cheek at the edge of the demo. The centre attribute switches the printed scale to −5…0…+5 and the value range with it. Drag is vertical (up is clockwise), the wheel steps, arrows step 0.1 and Shift-arrows step 1.

Left out: the D-shaft's flat, the pot's bushing, and the panel's brushed grain under the paint.
