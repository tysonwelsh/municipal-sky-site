THE SOURCE

The Veeder-Root revolution counter out of Hartford is the little cast-zinc brick bolted to the end of every jackshaft, press and winder from the 1920s to the 1970s: a sand-cast case with rounded corners, a slot cut in the front with a glass insert set back behind a raised rim, five ivory numeral wheels, two mounting ears, and a knurled reset knob on a shaft out of the right-hand side.

The tells kept here: the wheels are drawn as projected cylinders — every figure sits at an angle on the drum (36° per numeral), is placed at y = R·sin θ and squashed by cos θ, so it flattens about 6 % as it leaves the window; the units wheel rolls continuously while each higher wheel only moves during the last tenth of the wheel below it, which makes a row of nines carry like a wave; hair-thin spacer seams between the wheels; the raised window rim lit on its top bevel and shadowed under; the drum curvature darkening the top and bottom of every cell; a faint sand-cast grain over the zinc and a matte sheen, never chrome; the knob's ~24 knurls placed by the same projection so they crowd at its top and bottom edge. An increment takes about 180 ms with a ratchet overshoot; a long jump spins at twelve digits a second and decelerates; a reset rolls every wheel back to zero together in 400 ms while the knob turns 30° and springs back.

Left out: the rear shaft and its worm, the "Veeder-Root" script (the cast wordmark here is generic), and the pawl click you would hear rather than see.
