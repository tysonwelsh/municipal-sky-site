THE SOURCE

A GWR- or LNER-style builder's nameplate: brass sand-cast in one piece with the letters and the frame standing proud, the raised faces then filed and polished bright while the painted ground stays matte, and the whole plate screwed to the boiler cladding with four slotted brass screws.

The tells kept here: the letters and the mitred border are one continuous polished brass surface, lit top-left with a bright top face, a dark cast side showing along the bottom-right of every stroke and a soft shadow it throws onto the paint; the ground is a matte, faintly brush-marked enamel, darker at the edges the polishing rag never reached, with a small corner chip that has always exposed the raw brass underneath — cast plates take knocks; four brass screws with slots at random angles set into the border. ground swaps red for the black or green GWR variants also seen. With wear the paint scuffs further near the letters and green verdigris settles into their corners.

Left out: the running number roundel and the shed-code smokebox plate that usually ride alongside it, and the plate's very slight convex curve to follow the boiler.
