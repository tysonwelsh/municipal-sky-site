THE SOURCE

The engine controls on a Cessna 172 are two plungers side by side on the lower panel, coloured by regulation: the black phenolic THROTTLE knob, pushed in for full power, and the red anodised MIXTURE knob, a vernier — twist it for fine leaning, press the small button in its centre to slide it freely. Each rides a chrome shaft through a black friction collar; the shaft only shows when the knob is pulled out, and the knob lands back against the collar when pushed home.

The tells kept here: the pair — black plunger, red vernier — with the panel legends engraved above the holes in a Futura-like face; THROTTLE curving around the top of the black face with PUSH beneath; the mixture's knurled skirt with about forty knurls placed by angle so they crowd at the sides, and the black centre button that depresses while you drag; the chrome shaft with a hard horizon, visible between knob and collar only when pulled; the black knurled friction collar, a little grubby at its base; a shallow ring of wear on the paint around each hole; one light above-left, so the knob's rim catches a highlight there and the shadows fall down and right. Pulling the knob down the screen reduces, with a 40 ms friction ease; the wheel over the mixture turns the vernier and moves it 1 % per notch; arrows step 1 %, Shift-arrows 10 %, Home is full in, End full out.

Left out: the carburettor-heat knob that sits beside them, the throttle's friction-lock adjustment and the placard text below the panel.
