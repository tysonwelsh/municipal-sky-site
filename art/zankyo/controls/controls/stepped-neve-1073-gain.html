THE SOURCE

The Neve 1073 is the mic/line amplifier module of the 1970 Neve consoles, and the red knob at the top of its grey-blue panel is the coarse gain: a 23-way rotary switch behind a tall, waisted, moulded "Marconi" knob with a white pointer nose moulded into its skirt. The panel around it is engraved and filled white with two arcs of dB positions, the MIC range on the outer arc and the LINE range on the inner one, each with a bracket, and OFF at the bottom-left. The same knob comes in grey and blue on the module's other switches.

The tells kept here: the waisted silhouette read from above as a domed red cap over a dark concave waist over a wide flared skirt, with a long shadow because the knob is tall; the white pointer nose on the skirt; the two concentric arcs of Barlow numerals with their LINE and MIC brackets and a black index line at OFF; the panel's grey-blue with faint horizontal brush marks and two scuffs where the fingers land; a hard detent snap of about 80 ms with a two-degree overshoot. Drag is vertical (up is clockwise), the wheel and the arrows click one position, Shift-arrows ten, Home is OFF, End is -80, and a click on a printed value jumps to it. The colour attribute gives the grey and blue siblings; value is the printed position (a "-20" resolves to the MIC arc; use index for the unambiguous position).

Left out: the rest of the module (the fine trim, the EQ knobs, the phase button), the engraved serial, the knob's set-screw hole.
