THE SOURCE

The Nakamichi 600 of 1976 put its two level meters behind a pair of narrow horizontal slots cut straight into the sloping black front, one above the other, marked only L and R. There is no meter face to look at: what moves is the white tip of a pointer rising out of the lip from underneath, and what you read is a scale printed on the top edge of the slot, white on black, with a single warm lamp behind the pair washing up from the bottom of each opening.

The tells kept here: only the tip shows — the pointer's body stays hidden below the bottom lip; the scale runs −40 −20 −10 −5 −3 0 +3 +5 with the numerals crowding to the left, because deflection follows voltage rather than decibels, and turns red from 0; the lamp's glow leaks up from the bottom of the slot, unevenly, and dies out before it reaches the numerals; the lip is lit along its top edge and shadowed along the bottom; the whole panel scrap is drawn with the deck's slight tilt (a −4° shear) so it reads as a sloped front rather than a flat rectangle; the pointer rides an arc of a very long radius, so it rotates a couple of degrees end to end and stands about a pixel taller at mid-scale. Both slots have VU ballistics — 300 ms to 99 % of a step, a per cent of overshoot — and the lamp does not flicker.

Left out: the deck around the slots (this is a panel scrap, not a front), the peak LED of the later 680, and the tape-type legend that sits below the meters on the real machine.
