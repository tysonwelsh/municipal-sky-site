THE SOURCE

Sifam of Torquay made the AL29 and its relations with no bezel at all: the case is a solid block of clear cast acrylic with softly rounded front edges, and the white scale card floats a few millimetres behind its front face. They went into Quad-era test gear, into BBC and Marconi racks, and into anything whose designer wanted the instrument to look like a piece of the panel rather than a fitting bolted through it.

The tells kept here: no bezel — the card is read through the slab, with the block's rounded top-left edges carrying two bright refracted highlights and its bottom-right edge a dark one with a thin caustic; the four corner bolts and their washers seen inside the block, very slightly out of focus; the card's soft shadow on the dark panel behind it; upright numerals on a 90° arc with a tick every two units and a long one every ten; a hairline blued-steel needle with a tiny curved spade near its tip, on a chrome-cased jewel just above the bottom edge; the chrome zero-adjust screw at bottom centre of the front face with its slot at an idle angle; small polite lettering and a class mark 1.5 in the corner. The block throws a soft shadow down and right; the matte card does not.

Left out: the rear terminal block and the moulded-in mounting studs, and the maker's name, which is replaced by a plain legend.
