THE SOURCE

The RCA DR2000 is a light bulb with seven filaments in it. A black ceramic plate inside a nine-pin miniature valve envelope carries seven short tungsten wires stretched between support posts; drive one at about 4.5 V and it glows the yellow-white of a torch bulb. It was the cheap answer to the Nixie — low voltage, no driver transistors — and it looks nothing like an LED.

The tells kept here: the segments are wires, not bars — hairlines with a visible sag toward the middle of the digit and bright end-posts where they clamp to the plate. The whole figure-eight scaffold is always there in dark grey on the black plate, lit or not. Coming on, a filament ramps from dull red through orange to yellow-white over about 180 ms; going off it dies away through orange-red over 350 ms, so a counting display always has one segment fading behind the new number. The envelope is a miniature valve: domed top with the evacuation tip, a mica support disc, a tall specular streak down the glass, and a black base wafer with the pin circle. The warm attribute runs the filaments under-driven, and filter="red" drops the Heathkit red window in front, which sinks the plate into blackness and turns the filaments orange-red.

Left out: the getter patch, the pin numbers moulded into the wafer, and the faint hum a real bank of these makes when the drive is multiplexed.
