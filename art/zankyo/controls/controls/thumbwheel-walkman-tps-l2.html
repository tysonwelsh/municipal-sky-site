THE SOURCE

The Sony Walkman TPS-L2 of 1979 is blue and silver with an orange button, and its volume is not a knob but two black serrated wheels, one per channel, set into the silver top edge. Each wheel lives inside the body and only a sliver of its rim pokes up through a slot; you roll it with a thumb moving front to back. This is that top edge seen from above: the slots, the two rims, VOL with L and R silk-screened in white between them, and below it a slice of the blue front with the orange HOTLINE button.

The tells kept here: only an arc of each wheel shows, black against the silver, and its sixty serrations are placed by angle on a 17.5 px rim (y = R·sin θ, width and brightness by cos θ), so they crowd toward both ends of the slot and scroll past as the value changes; the slot's inner shadow at the top and left with a lit lip at the bottom and right; the white index tick at the slot's edge and the faint white dot on the rim that passes it at zero; the brushed silver edge over the anodised blue; the wheels the only black thing on the body. Drag along the slot (1 px = 0.05, up is louder), wheel over it, arrows and Shift-arrows, Home/End; 300° of travel with hard stops. channels="2" gives the twin and emits { value: [l, r] }; link gangs them so either wheel drives both.

Left out: the TONE switch and the headphone jacks further along the edge; the second, white "SONY" print; the cassette door.
