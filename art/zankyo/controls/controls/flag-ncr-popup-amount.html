THE SOURCE

On a National Cash Register of the Class 1000 / Class 21 generation the amount is not printed, it is lifted: strike a key and a tall, narrow, ivory-enamelled steel flag with a black numeral is thrown up on a lever behind the bevelled glass of the indication window, so the customer on the other side of the counter can see what was rung. Among the ivory flags is a red one — NO SALE, TOTAL, CHARGE — and the whole row falls back into its slots with a clack before the next amount goes up.

The tells kept: the chromed frame with rounded corners, a hard horizon in the plating and a knurled finial at each end; the bevelled glass with its diagonal reflection and the frame's soft shadow falling on the flags; the black japanned interior, the slot plate the flags rise through, and the lever stem visible under each raised flag; flags that rise in 120 ms, hit the stop and bounce 3 px, and drop in 90 ms before a new amount; the row not perfectly aligned — every flag rests a pixel high or low and half a degree off square; the painted $ and decimal point on the slot plate; positions with no digit showing only the tips of their lowered flags.

Left out: the keyboard, the drawer and the printed detail strip below; the NCR crest and scrollwork on the register top (only the scrap of japanned casting that carries the window is here); the clack itself, which is the one tell a screen cannot keep.
