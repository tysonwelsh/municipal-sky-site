GEN BUS 1

THE SOURCE

The Klixon 7274 and its lookalikes from Wood Electric and Mechanical Products are the little black push-pull breakers that fill a general-aviation breaker panel in rows of eight or more: a round black button standing a few millimetres proud with its amperage printed on the face, on a chrome bushing and hex nut. When the thermal element trips it snaps out on its own, exposing a white nylon collar on the stem that was hidden inside the bushing — a whole row reads at a glance because one white band stands out among black buttons.

The tells kept here: the white collar that only shows once the button is out; the amperage printed white on the button's face; a chrome hex nut and a hint of bare bushing thread above it once popped; a row of eight under a single white outline with a bus name and a stencil legend under each breaker; one breaker in the row wearing a wider black collar (collared) that is not meant to be reset in flight, so pushing it back in is refused. Click or drag a breaker out to trip it manually, click or press it to reset (unless collared), and the built-in tripped attribute pops it with a 40 ms snap and a one-pixel bounce.

Left out: the rear wire lugs, the printed date-code on the button's underside.
