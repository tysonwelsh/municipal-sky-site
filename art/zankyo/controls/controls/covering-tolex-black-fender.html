THE SOURCE

Fender's black tolex (1963–) and its 1950s brown/blonde predecessors: a vinyl-coated cotton fabric, embossed with a fine pebbled "elephant" grain, glued over the pine cabinet with the corners folded and stapled, then capped with a stamped nickel corner protector. The same idea covers Marshall's black levant, Ampeg's printed blue-diamond and Orange's orange.

The tells kept here: the pebble is built from two layered noise fields lit from above-left, so it sparkles in tiny highlights rather than showing a reflected band; a folded, stapled corner under a stamped, slightly domed chrome L-bracket with three screws at odd angles, casting its own small shadow onto the vinyl; the blue-diamond variant as a printed grid softened by the grain underneath; a vertical seam where two sheets meet, one edge sitting a hair proud; yellow ambers the blonde variant and wear adds grey scuffs and a small torn corner exposing the wood beneath.

Left out: the piping trim along the edges, the individual weave of the backing cloth under the vinyl skin, and Ampeg's full sparkle-diamond repeat (approximated as a soft crosshatch).
