THE SOURCE

Under the dial of a 1950s Delco or Motorola car radio sits a row of chunky chromed rectangular presets in a chrome bezel: pull one toward you to set it to the station you've just tuned by hand, push it back in and it stays there, and pushing any preset in pops all the others out together with a little mechanical jolt.

The tells kept here: the caps as reflective chrome with a hard horizon line, dark below and bright above, and a small hot spot on the dome; the black insert bar across each face carrying a moulded numeral between two bright chrome hairlines; the pressed cap reading not darker but simply lower, its slot showing more black above it; the linked pop when a new one is pressed; and the dial pointer above slewing to the new station over 300 ms. Dragging a cap upward pulls it into a "set" stage beyond normal-out and fires a set event; pushing it back in selects it.

Left out: the tone and volume knobs either side, the AM dial's full frequency scale, and the radio's chassis behind the bezel.
