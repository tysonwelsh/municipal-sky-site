THE SOURCE

The Otis "Series 1" car station of the late 1970s put a round black Lexan button into a brushed stainless plate and ringed it with a translucent acrylic halo lit from behind by a small incandescent lamp. Press it and the call registers: the ring comes up warm over about a tenth of a second and stays lit until the controller clears it at the floor. It is a two-way control — an input and a lamp on the same 32 mm circle.

The tells kept here: the halo is a ring of light around the button, dull grey when unlit and evenly bright with a hot inner edge when lit, throwing a soft bloom and a colour cast onto the steel; the numeral is moulded proud, not printed, so it has a lit top bevel and a hairline shadow under it; the button is a low cylinder standing about 2 mm out of the plate with a tight contact shadow; the plate is brushed with a horizontal grain, a broad soft highlight across it and a chamfered hole that goes dark at the top-left and catches light at the bottom-right; a grey ring of grime sits in the gap where thumbs never reach, while the top of the numeral is polished. Press travels 1.5 px with a solid stop, the lamp warms over 120 ms and cools over 250 ms. The left plate carries the tactile braille cell for its floor.

Left out: the fixing screws at the top and bottom of a real car station (the element is one button on its pitch, so a host can stack a column), and the lamp's slight flicker.
