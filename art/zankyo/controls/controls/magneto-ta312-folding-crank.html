THE SOURCE

The TA-312/PT field telephone rides in a canvas case with an olive-drab moulded box, a black handset in spring clips, and, on the box's right end, a folding hand generator: a zinc-plated steel crank folded flush against the case until it is wanted, with a black phenolic knob on its end and a chrome hub cap where it pivots.

The tells kept here: the crank folded flat and tucked against the box, and a swing out to a working angle before it will turn; once deployed the knob describes a fixed circle around the hub while its own highlight stays put — it rotates on its own pin, not with the arm; the chrome hub cap with a slotted screw, the one bright thing on the moulded olive; the box's satin grain and a data plate rivetted to it; the paint worn pale at the corner a strap has rubbed for forty years. A turn and a half inside a second and a half rings the (unseen) exchange, as the real magneto does.

Left out: the true two-hinge fold of the real arm (modelled here as one swing to stow or deploy), the handset, the CB/LB selector and the buzzer knob — this build is the crank alone, as the brief asks for.
