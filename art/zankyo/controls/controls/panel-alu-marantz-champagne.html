THE SOURCE

The 1970s Japanese silver face: a 4 mm aluminium plate, brushed dead straight along its width and clear-anodised with a warm champagne tint, carrying tiny dark-blue Helvetica legends and two engraved hairlines filled black. Marantz's 2270 is the warmest of them; Pioneer and Kenwood ran cooler and neutral, which is what finish="silver" gives you.

The tells kept here: the grain is horizontal and continuous across the whole width — built from an anisotropic turbulence field plus three coprime hairline gradients so it never moirés at any scale; a single soft reflected band across the upper third that follows the grain, brighter at the left, and that slides ±6 px as the pointer crosses the plate, the way a real brushed surface moves its sheen; the anodise warm rather than neutral; legends tiny, wide-tracked and dark blue with a half-pixel halo under each glyph so the ink sits proud of the metal; two engraved rules with a bright lower lip so they read cut, not printed; a 1 px bright chamfer at the top and the whiter raw aluminium of the 4 mm cut edge along the bottom. With wear the legend under the centre knob thins back to bare grain, and the anodise polishes where thumbs went.

Left out: the maker's script logo (a generic wordmark stands in), the gyro-touch flywheel cut-out, and the rack handles' countersinks.
