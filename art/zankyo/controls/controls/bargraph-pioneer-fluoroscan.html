THE SOURCE

Fluoroscan was Pioneer's name for the vacuum-fluorescent bar meters it put across the CT-F950 and CT-F1250 cassette decks and the SA-9800 amplifier in the late seventies: two rows of short, wide phosphor segments on a glass plate behind a smoked acrylic window let into the brushed silver fascia, with the dB figures printed white on the tube's own black mask so they seem to float in the dark.

The tells kept here: the glow is phosphor, not LED — blue-green, soft-edged, with the fine grid of the tube's control wires laid over it, and the top three segments the same fuzzy glow in amber rather than a hard red; unlit segments survive as barely-there ghosts, so the ladder is readable at silence; the smoked window swallows the mask so the bars float, with one long horizontal reflection picked up from the fascia's brushed grain; the extended Eurostile legend, wide-letterspaced, on the silver below. The ballistics are a 20 ms rise and a 600 ms fall per 20 dB, a segment fades up in 40 ms and its phosphor takes about 30 ms to die away, and the whole tube shimmers by about one per cent.

Left out: the deck's memory and Dolby lamps at the right of the real window, and the tube's pin stems and getter flash, which are hidden behind the mask on the real thing too.
