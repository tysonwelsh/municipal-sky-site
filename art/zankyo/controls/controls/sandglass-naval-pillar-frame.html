THE SOURCE

The half-hour glass ran the Royal Navy's day: the boy of the watch turned it and struck the bell, eight turns to a watch. The instrument is two hand-blown bulbs joined at a narrow waist, the join bound in waxed cord, the pair held between two round end plates by four turned pillars of dark oak — beads and rings cut on a pole lathe — with brass pins through the plates.

The tells kept here: the glass is hand-blown, so the two bulbs are not quite the same and there is a seed bubble in the shoulder of the upper one; the cord is a real wrap of five turns, lit on top; the sand in the upper bulb sinks as a funnel with a dimple that deepens as the level falls, and the pile in the lower bulb grows as a cone at the angle of repose until it drowns in its own level; a single thread of sand between them with loose grains in it; long vertical highlights on both bulbs, and the sand lit from the left and shadowed to the right. Turning the glass rotates the whole frame through 180° in half a second, and the thread starts again after a beat — the sand that was below is now above.

Left out: the paper seal over the neck, the maker's ivory label, and the gimballed binnacle case some of these lived in.
