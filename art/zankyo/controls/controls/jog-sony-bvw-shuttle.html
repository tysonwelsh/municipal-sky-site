THE SOURCE

The SEARCH dial on the Sony BVW-75 Betacam SP editing recorder (1986) and its PVW-2800 successor: two concentric controls on a dark grey panel. Inside, a 50 mm black ABS jog knob with the shallowest dish and one off-centre finger dimple, free-spinning with detent clicks, one frame per click. Around it, an 80 mm rubber-rimmed shuttle ring that turns ±135° in seven detents each side and points, with a raised lug at 12 o'clock, at a silk-screened speed scale — ×10 ×5 ×2 ×1 STILL ×1 ×2 ×5 ×10. Two small square lamps say which mode the deck is in: JOG green, SHUTTLE amber.

The tells kept here: the dimple that goes round with the jog and rests at 2 o'clock; the lug as the ring's pointer with its bright top edge, and a shadow it casts on the ring; the ring flat with a matte rubber edge, the jog dished with its top-left inner wall in shadow and its lower-right lip lit; the mode lamps — touching the jog lights JOG, moving the ring lights SHUTTLE; the shiny patch on the jog's rim at half past four where a thumb lands. The jog has inertia (about half a second of spin-down, ticking detents as it goes); the ring snaps between detents and, with spring, eases back to STILL in 180 ms when released.

Left out: the surrounding transport keys, the ring's fine knurl, the panel's screen-printed part number.
