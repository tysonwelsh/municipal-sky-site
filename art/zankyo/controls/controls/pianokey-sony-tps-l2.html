THE SOURCE

The Sony TPS-L2 of 1979 — the first Walkman — carried its transport on four chrome-plated piano keys set in a black slot along the top edge of a blue-and-silver box, with the stamped black symbols small enough that you found them by feel. The one warm colour on the whole object is the orange HOT LINE button on the blue band, which cut the music and opened the microphone so two people sharing the two headphone jacks could talk.

The tells kept here: chrome with a hard horizon — bright sky on the upper half, a sharp line, dark ground reflection below, then a bright bottom lip; a latched key that sits 6 px lower than its neighbours and has a deeper black gap above it, popping back up with a spring when STOP is pressed; stamped black symbols, crisp and small; the satin silver body with a fine horizontal grain and one broad soft highlight; the Walkman-blue painted band with its hairline seam; the matte orange button, domed, the only warm thing on the panel; and the wear on PLAY, whose chrome has gone dull grey where a thumb has landed ten thousand times.

Left out: the second headphone jack and the cassette window that would sit above the keys, the REC/PAUSE slider, and the mechanical clack (this is a silent control).
