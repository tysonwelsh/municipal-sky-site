THE SOURCE

The Marantz 2270 and its 2230 / 2325 brothers put a hand-span of smoked glass across the front of the receiver. Behind it sit incandescent lamps with blue filter caps; the scale is printed on the back of the glass in white, and a red pointer on a string drive slides across it. Switched off, the window is a black slab and the numerals are gone. Switched on, it is the most recognisable blue in hi-fi.

The tells kept here: the glass is genuinely black when off — the print is there, at a hair above the glass's own value, and only the lamps reveal it; four pools of light along the top edge, so the numerals under a lamp are brighter than the ones between and the bottom of the window falls away to a deep blue; the pools run warm in the core and blue at the edge, because it is a hot filament behind a blue cap; a single fine horizontal reflection from the room, which does not follow the shape of anything behind the glass; the pointer as a thin red line the full height of the window that casts no light of its own and arrives with the string's lag and a pixel of overshoot; the FM scale even and linear with a compressed AM row below it; and a separate STEREO window at the left end that lights red.

Left out: the gyro-touch flywheel and its knob (another control), the signal-strength and centre-tune meters that sit to the right of the glass on the real receiver, and the FM MUTING / MODE legends along the fascia below.
