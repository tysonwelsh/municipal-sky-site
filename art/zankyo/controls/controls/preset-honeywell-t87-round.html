THE SOURCE

Henry Dreyfuss drew The Round for Honeywell in 1953 and it is still on walls today: a convex dome of spun, gold-anodised aluminium (later white or chrome), an outer ring you turn with two fingers, a curved window at the top where a cylindrical drum of black numerals scrolls past a fixed red pointer to set the temperature, and a small thermometer window below showing the room's actual reading — which does not move with the ring.

The tells kept here: the dome's radial brushed highlight, bright at ten o'clock and dark at four, sweeping as one piece with the ring; the set-point numerals foreshortening as they near the window's edges, the way text does wrapped around a cylinder; the thermometer needle's slow, independent ballistic; a heat-on dot that lights when the setting is above the room. Turning the ring is an angle-follow drag, like a tuning dial, plus wheel and arrows.

Left out: the wall sub-base and its low-voltage terminals, the mercury switch inside, and the interior this cover sits over (another build's job).
