THE SOURCE

The tweed-era Fender control plate bolted flat to the top of a Deluxe, Bassman or Twin chassis: a bright chrome-plated steel strip with a numbered dial ring silk-screened around each pot's shaft hole and the control's name printed underneath in a bold black gothic. It reads from across a stage as one bright bar with a dark band running through it — the way any flat chrome catches a room.

The tells kept here: the "horizon" — a dark band across the middle with pale sky above and a warm ground below, the classic way a flat chrome plate reads regardless of its size — with a soft smeared blur of the room caught in the reflection and a hard bright glint that drifts sideways as the plate is tilted; a clockwise ring of 1–12 silk-screened around each hole, sized and spaced to the number of names in value; bold black legends with no halo, floating right in the reflection; rounded 6 px corners with a thin bright-then-dark plated edge; four small nickel screws. finish="brushed" swaps the horizon for a satin hairline sheen with one soft band. With wear, dark pits with bright rims cluster near the screws and the centre legend scratches through to bare steel.

Left out: the chassis lip and eyelet board beneath the plate, the jack and speaker-cable cutouts.
