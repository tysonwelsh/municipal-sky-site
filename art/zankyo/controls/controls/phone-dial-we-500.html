THE SOURCE

The Western Electric 500 is the telephone: Dreyfuss's 1949 shape, black ABS, and on its face the dial that was its one clever idea — a clear acrylic finger-wheel so the numerals could be printed on the plate outside the holes, where the finger does not hide them. Ten holes, a chrome hook of a finger stop at about four o'clock, and in the middle a typed number card under a clear disc held by a bright ring. Pull a hole round to the stop, let go, and a governor lets the wheel purr back at ten pulses a second: a 0 takes just over a second.

The tells kept here: numerals in a Bell-Gothic-like condensed face on the ring outside the holes, with the letters ABC…WXY beneath them and OPERATOR under the 0; the wheel as an 18 % white tint with one broad room-shaped reflection, the plate's print ghosted a hair through the acrylic, and each hole drawn as a see-through window with a shadow on its upper-left wall and a refracting bright edge lower-right; the chrome finger stop as a hooked tab with a hard horizon; the card ring, card and clear disc; a bright thumb-worn patch on the plate by the 5 and 6. Drag any hole clockwise (mouse or touch) — it stops at the hook; on release the wheel returns at a governed 300°/s, clicking a pulse event per hole passed, bounces at rest, and then commits the digit with change; dialed accumulates the number. Number keys dial with the animation; arrows dial the next and previous digit, Home dials 1 and End dials 0, Backspace erases the last digit dialed.

Left out: the click of the finger stop, the dial's sound, the rest of the telephone.
