THE SOURCE

The Toshiba T3100 (1986) was one of the first "luggable" DOS machines to swap a CRT for a gas-plasma panel: a 640×400 orange text/graphics screen behind a bronze-tinted filter, built into a heavy grey clamshell. It has no backlight of its own to worry about and no scanlines — the plasma cells fire instantly and each character sits on a fixed 8×16 cell grid, giving DOS its crisp, glowing monospace look.

The tells kept here: a deep, slightly redder-than-amber orange on a warm black field, drawn with a two-pass glow (a soft blurred under-layer plus a crisp core) so solid text blocks bloom a little without losing edges; a fine 8-pixel grid etched faintly across the glass; the bronze filter tint and a soft highlight along the bezel's top edge; a hard 2 Hz cursor blink with no fade; and a canned DOS session ("C:\>DIR", a FORMAT prompt, a box-drawn menu) typed in at 60 characters a second when the control is auto-demoed. `crop` shows a smaller window of the 80×25 page for a closer, larger view.

Left out: a true 8×16 bitmap glyph table (a monospace Google Font stands in — real CGA box-drawing glyphs are approximated with plain ASCII `+`/`-`/`|` so the grid never breaks on a missing glyph), the clamshell's keyboard and latch, and the vent grille's exact slot count.
