THE SOURCE

The Airpath C2300 and its Kollsman cousin is the little black can clamped to the windscreen post of every light aeroplane: a fluid-filled bowl holding a vertical drum card — white marks on a black cylinder — read through a small rectangular window against a fixed white lubber line. It is called the whiskey compass for the fluid, and it is the only instrument in the aeroplane that is always telling the truth and always telling it late.

The tells kept here: the card is a drum, so the figures are placed by angle (x = R·sin θ, width and brightness ∝ cos θ) and crowd toward the window's edges until they vanish into the cylinder's shadow. It reads backwards to a map — E sits to the left of N, because you are looking at the far side of the card. A figure every 30° with the zero dropped (3, 6, 12, 15…), N E S W at the cardinals, a long tick at every 10° and a short one at every 5°. The window is small and the body is fat: a rounded black can with a bevelled frame, two brass compensator screws on the top face marked N‑S and E‑W, a bent-tin mounting strap, and the typewritten correction card in its own tin frame underneath. A bubble sits at the top of the fluid and a glint runs along the window's top edge. The card is liquid-damped: it leads the turn, overswings about 8°, takes two decaying swings to settle, and sways a degree when nothing is happening. card="cyrillic" swaps N E S W for С В Ю З.

Left out: the rear of the can and its cable, the deviation caused by the aeroplane's own iron (the correction card's figures are decorative), and the acceleration errors — a real whiskey compass also lies when you speed up on an easterly heading.
