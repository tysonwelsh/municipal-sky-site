THE SOURCE

A Great Western lower-quadrant home signal: a pressed-steel arm painted signal red with a white band near its tip, pivoted on a cast bracket near the top of a white timber post with a black band at its foot and a cast finial on top. The GWR kept the arm dropping down to show clear long after other railways went to the upper quadrant, and the balance weight at the foot of the post pulls it back up to danger the moment the wire goes slack — the fail-safe that the whole of British signalling is built on.

The tells kept here: the arm falls 45° to clear and overshoots four degrees before it settles, and snaps back up to danger in half the time with a hard stop and a shudder; the spectacle plate swings with it so that the oil lamp behind shows red through the red glass at danger and green through the blue glass at clear — blue glass plus a yellow flame is how a Victorian railway made green; the white band on the red arm; the white post, black band and finial; the balance lever and its weight turning at the foot with the operating rod rising alongside the post. night darkens everything to a silhouette and leaves only the aspect burning; distant swaps in the yellow fishtail arm with its black chevron and a yellow aspect.

Left out: the lamp's daily trip up the ladder — there is no ladder, no lamp iron and no wire run, and the post is a plain section rather than a full GWR wooden post with its cap and bolts.
