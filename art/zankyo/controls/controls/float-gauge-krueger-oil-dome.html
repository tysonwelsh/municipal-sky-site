THE SOURCE

The Krueger Sentry gauge is the little clear dome screwed into the top of a domestic heating-oil tank: a float on a rod inside the tank lifts a bright red disc up a printed card marked F ¾ ½ ¼ E, and the oil man in the basement reads the level from six feet away by where the red sits. Nothing is electrical; the whole instrument is a rod, a disc, a card and a plastic dome on a threaded cap.

The tells kept here: the red disc as the only saturated thing in the picture, read against big black letters on the card behind it; the dome yellowed with age, clouded at the crown and crazed with a cobweb of fine scratches, carrying two vertical highlight streaks that belong to the room and not to the card; the card itself set a degree or two askew in its slot and bleached at the top where the cellar light falls; the black threaded cap on its hex base with a ring of dirt where it meets the tank; the brass rod catching one line of light. The disc has mass — it eases to a new level over about two seconds and wobbles on the rod as it stops, tilting a little with the wobble.

Left out: the float and the rod below the cap inside the tank, the gallon table stamped on the tank's side, and the maker's decal (replaced by a plain word).
