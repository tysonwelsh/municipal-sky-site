THE SOURCE

The RCA Theremin was sold as a piece of furniture: a walnut cabinet with a cloth speaker grille, standing on turned legs, with a vertical chrome pitch antenna rising from one end and a horizontal chrome volume loop projecting from the other. Leon Theremin's instrument is played without touching it at all — the two antennas sense the capacitance of a hand at a distance, one antenna raising pitch as a hand nears, the other lowering volume as a hand nears its loop.

The tells kept here: two plain polished chrome tubes with a hard specular highlight; the response as distance, not contact, with a soft hand cursor near each antenna that lags the pointer a little; the optional faint contour rings showing the sensing field, tighter near the rod because the pitch response is exponential; the cabinet as furniture, complete with grille cloth and a pilot lamp. The whole element is tall because the rod has to be.

Left out: the internal vacuum-tube oscillators, the volume loop's true RF near-field shape (approximated here as simple radial distance), and the Moog Etherwave's smaller box (not modelled as a separate variant).
