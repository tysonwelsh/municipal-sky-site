THE SOURCE

The IBM Model M of 1985 put a buckling spring under a two-piece pearl PBT keycap: press it and the spring bows sideways about two millimetres down, throwing the hammer against the membrane with a crack you can hear two rooms away, and the cap drops the rest of its travel in one go. The legends are dye-sublimated — soaked into the plastic rather than printed on it — so they are grey, slightly soft at the edge, and they never wear off.

The tells kept here: the cap as a truncated pyramid with thick walls and a cylindrical dish across the top, dark at the left of the dish and bright toward the right where the curve turns back into the light; the hairline seam around the skirt where the shell meets its base, sitting two pixels proud of the case; the grey legend at the top-left of the dish for letters and centred for words; pearl for the alphas and pebble grey for the modifiers; the fine matte PBT grain; the polished, slightly warmer top of a much-used cap (Enter, here); and the case's hairline-textured pebble grey with the dark base plate showing in the gap between caps. The travel is two-stage — two pixels of load, then a snap through the remaining two — and activate fires at the snap, not at the bottom.

Left out: the case's three status LEDs and its badge, the barrel plate under the caps, and the flip-out feet. The row attribute tilts the top face (4°, 2°, 0°, −2°, −4° from the number row down) but all three caps here are from the home row, so they stand level.
