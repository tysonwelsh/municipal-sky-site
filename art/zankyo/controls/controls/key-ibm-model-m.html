THE SOURCE

The IBM Model M of 1985 put a buckling spring under a two-piece pearl PBT keycap: press it and the spring bows sideways about two millimetres down, throwing a hammer at the membrane with a crack you can hear two rooms away, and the cap drops the rest of its travel in one go. The legends are dye-sublimated — soaked into the plastic rather than printed on it — so they are grey, slightly soft at the edge, and they never wear off.

The tells kept here: the cap drawn as a real truncated pyramid — four separate walls, the left one lit, the right one in shade, the back one bright, the front one tall and dropping into the shadow the top face casts on it; a cylindrical dish across that top face, dark at the left and turning bright where the curve comes back up into the light; the hairline seam around the skirt where the shell meets its base; the grey legend at the top-left of the dish for letters and centred for words; pearl for the alphas and pebble grey for the modifiers; the fine matte PBT grain on every face; the polish on a much-used cap (Enter, here); and the pebble-grey hairline-textured case with the dark base plate showing in the gap between caps. The travel is two-stage — two pixels of load, then a snap through the remaining two — and activate fires at the snap, not at the bottom.

Left out: the case's three status LEDs and its badge, the barrel plate under the caps, and the flip-out feet. The row attribute tilts the top face (4°, 2°, 0°, −2°, −4° from the number row down) by trading front wall for back wall; all three caps here are from the home row, so they stand level.
