THE SOURCE

The Wittner "Maelzel" 803/811 is the walnut pyramid on a million piano lids: a wound clockwork escapement driving an inverted pendulum — the rod is pivoted just above the base, a lead bob hangs below it out of sight, and a brass weight slides up the rod against a printed card. Higher weight, slower beat. This is the instrument with its front door taken off, which is how you actually use it.

The tells kept here: the upside-down pendulum with its pivot at the bottom and the bob hidden behind the base; the brass weight with the little window showing the number it sits on; the escapement's dead stop at each extreme (the rod holds for 30 ms before it comes back — that is the tick); a constant swing while only the period changes; the scale's numbers unevenly spaced, wide apart at 40 and crowding to nothing at 208, because the tempo goes as the reciprocal of the weight's height; the Italian tempo names set in italic up the right of the card; the bell lever with its 0 2 3 4 6 and the little brass bell it flicks; the brass winding key out of the right-hand side; oiled walnut with the grain running up the case and a satin sheen from above-left.

Left out / changed: the front door itself, the clockwork behind the card, and the amplitude — the entry asks for ±35°, which at 140 px wide would throw the rod clean out of the case, so the swing is drawn at ±10° and the rod is clipped by the window frame at the extremes exactly as the real frame hides it.
