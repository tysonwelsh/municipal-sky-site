THE SOURCE

The Weston Model 862 is the round Standard Volume Indicator screwed into the meter panel of every American broadcast console from 1939 onward — a 3½-inch black phenolic flange, flat glass, and behind it the Type A card in Weston buff, a warm ivory-tan that turns amber when the console's lamp is behind it. Two scales share one tick line: the dB row above, running −20 −10 −7 −5 −3 −2 −1 0 +1 +2 +3, and the percentage row below, 0 to 100 evenly spaced. The dB numerals crowd to the left because deflection follows voltage, not decibels: 0 VU sits at 100 % and about seven-tenths of the way along the arc, and full scale is +3 VU.

The tells kept here: both arcs on the one tick line, logarithmic above and linear below; the red section from 0 to +3 with red ticks, red numerals and a red bar joining the two rows; the plain black knife pointer with no spade and a chrome pivot cap low on the card; the shallow arc with the pivot far below the scale; the buff card lit from behind by a soft, uneven, low-centre pool with the paper's grain still showing through it; "VU" in its ruled box under the arc's centre; the flange's hairline polished rim and three small screws at 120°. The pointer has true VU ballistics — a damped second-order approach that reaches 99 % of a step in 300 ms and overshoots by about 1 %, the same falling as rising — and it stops when the element leaves the page.

Left out: the Weston script logo (a plain wordmark stands in), the rear terminal posts and the zero-adjust screw of the panel-mounting variant, and the slight parallax between pointer and card.
