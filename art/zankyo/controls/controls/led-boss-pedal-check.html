THE SOURCE

Every Boss compact pedal since the 1977 OD-1 carries the same lamp: one 5 mm diffused red LED pushed through a black nylon clip bezel in the sloping top of the enamelled diecast box, just above the treadle, with CHECK screened beside it. It is a battery light as much as a bypass light — Boss's instruction is to watch it and change the 9 V when it goes dim. This one sits in DS-1 orange.

The tells kept here: the dome is diffused, so unlit it is a dull red plastic disc and never black; the bezel is flat matte nylon with a fine mould seam at six o'clock and only a whisper of a rim highlight at the top-left; lit, the dome glows evenly from one hot spot at eleven o'clock with a darker ring at its base where the bezel shades it, and the halo stains the orange enamel a hotter red; the cast box's fine orange-peel texture runs under everything, with a boot scuff and one chipped edge where the enamel has been kicked off. Switching is instant, as an LED is, with a single frame of bloom; battery scales the brightness from 1 down to 0.35 without shifting the hue.

Left out: the pedal's screened name plate, the treadle and its rubber pad, the thumbscrew — this is a crop of the box around the lamp, which is what a host page wants to paste next to something else.
