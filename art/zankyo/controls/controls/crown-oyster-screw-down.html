THE SOURCE

The Oyster screw-down crown, and the two crown guards the Submariner grew in 1959, is the most copied silhouette in watchmaking: a fat knurled crown seated on a threaded tube at three o'clock, hugged above and below by two horns of case metal. Screwed in it is flush and sealed; unscrew it and it springs to the winding position, then pulls out one click for the date and another for the time. This is the view looking down at the three o'clock edge of the case, with the black bezel insert and its serrated edge at the far left.

The tells kept here: the guards as the silhouette, polished with one hard reflection and a scratch on the upper one; the brushed case with a horizontal grain and a broad soft highlight; the knurl seen edge-on, ridges placed by angle so they crowd at the top and bottom of the crown and scroll as it turns; each pull moves the crown out 1.2 mm and shows more of the bright tube; the domed end with one highlight and a tiny stamped dot; a hairline seal shadow where the crown meets the tube. Drag up and down or wheel to turn (events carry delta in clicks); click, Space or ] pulls the next position; past three it screws back down with three turns; [ pushes in a step; Home screws down; End pulls to time.

Left out: the Triplock gasket rings under the crown, the coronet (a plain dot instead), the lugs.
