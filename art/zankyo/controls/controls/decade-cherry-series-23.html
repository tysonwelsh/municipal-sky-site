THE SOURCE

The Cherry Series 23 and its Digitran and EECO cousins are the black thumbwheel modules on every 1970s counter, frequency synthesiser and NC machine console: a 10 × 33 mm front with a numeral in a window, a small push-button above and below with a moulded triangle, and the wheel's serrated rim showing in a slot beside the window so the impatient can roll it directly. Each module is one decade; they are butted into a bank with a brushed aluminium plate and a screw at each end, and each wheel wraps on its own, because a decade switch has no carry.

The tells kept here: the numerals live on a drum, placed by angle (y = R·sin θ, squashed by cos θ, dimmed as they turn away), so the next and previous digits show half-cut above and below the current one; the two pushers with their lighter top edge and moulded ▲ / ▼, which sink when clicked; the serrated edge in its own slot, ribs crowding toward the top and bottom, scrolling with the wheel; the modules butted with no gap and a thin white line printed at each edge; the aluminium end plates with grain and slotted screws; a ~110 ms roll with a small bounce into the detent. Click a pusher, drag the window or the edge (13 px per digit), wheel, arrows, Home/End for 0 and 9, type a digit and the focus moves on to the next wheel; Left/Right walk the bank.

Left out: the BCD/complement output codes and their rear pins; the optional red decimal-point dot on a pusher; the mounting bracket behind the panel.
