THE SOURCE

The Sony TPS-L2 of 1979 — the first Walkman — is a steel-blue painted aluminium box with brushed silver caps at the top and bottom edges, and nearly the whole front is the cassette door: a blue panel with a clear polystyrene window in it, hinged along its bottom, held by a small chrome latch button at the top edge. Press the latch and the door springs a few degrees; push it back and it clicks flat.

The tells kept here: that blue — a steel blue, not navy, not sky — with the silver ends and their little chrome screws; the cassette sitting label-out in the well with its two hub holes, the supply pack fatter than the take-up; the toothed hubs turning when playing, the take-up faster than the supply; the silver keyline round the window and the window's one long soft reflection at the upper left, which does not follow the shape of the cassette behind it; the door hinged at the bottom, so open, its top comes toward you and the leaf spring and the well's back wall show above it; a polished thumb-scuff on the blue just under the latch.

Left out: the brand, the model plate, the headphone jacks and the twin volume wheels on the top edge (those are their own control), and the orange HOTLINE button on the side.
