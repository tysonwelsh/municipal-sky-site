THE SOURCE

On the Polaroid SX-70 of 1972 the shutter is a 9 mm red button let into the brushed chrome front plate, immediately beside the tan Porvair panel that Edwin Land insisted should feel like a book binding. It is the only colour on the camera: everything else is chrome, tan and black. The 1977 OneStep moved the same button onto a white plastic body with the Polaroid rainbow at its edge.

The tells kept here: a small hot red dome in a field of chrome, its highlight tight and its underside lit warm by the leather beside it; the chrome's grain running vertically with one broad soft highlight crossing it; the button sunk in a round hole with a black plastic surround, the inner shadow at the top-left and the lit lip at the bottom-right; the leather's soft grain meeting the chrome at a crisp edge; the engraved exposure scale below. Press travels 1.5 px, goes in over 40 ms and comes back over 90, and fires the shutter as a single frame of white.

Left out: the lens housing and its focus wheel, the Polaroid wordmark on the chrome, and the lighten/darken wheel itself — only its engraved scale is here.
