THE SOURCE

The Simpson 260 is the American bench multimeter, made in Chicago from 1936 into the 2000s and hardly changed in looks: a cream-tan Bakelite face with the meter above and, below it, the range switch — a round black phenolic hub with a long moulded bar across it, one end drawn to a sharp nose, a raised spine down its length. The twelve ranges are printed on the face in a ring around it, the resistance and DC ranges in black and the AC in red, each position marked by a short radial line. There is no OFF: the ring is continuous, and the switch clicks straight from 500 mA round to R×1.

The tells kept here: the bar longer than the hub is wide, its two flanks shaded by the real angle so the lit side changes as it turns, with the bright edge along the spine; the domed plain hub with no set-screw and a dark shadow under it; the cream face with its faint swirl and warmer centre, a grime ring around the hub from decades of thumbs; the legends set tangentially above and below and radially at the sides, "Archivo Narrow" for the plain gothic print, with red for the AC ranges (ac, default 2.5V) and the group headings OHMS / AC VOLTS / DC VOLTS / DC MA around the outside (groups); twelve detents of 30°, and a loud snap that overshoots about two degrees and settles. Drag up or down to click through positions (up is clockwise), click a range to jump to it, wheel or arrows step, Shift-arrows step by three, Home/End go to the ends of the list. options is overridable; the value is the option string.

Left out: the function switch and the jacks beside it, the ohms-adjust knob, the meter itself.
