THE SOURCE

The Marantz 22-series receivers of the early seventies wear a long black-glass dial that lights up a cold, even blue when the set is on: white FM and AM scales printed on the glass, a thin red pointer, two small blue-lit meters at the left, all set in a brushed-aluminium fascia. Tuning is by the Gyro-Touch flywheel, a heavy horizontal wheel whose knurled rim shows through a slot at the right of the glass; flick it and it spins on, the pointer sliding a beat behind.

The tells kept here: the blue wash brightest around the pointer, with a bloom that spills past the bezel onto the aluminium; the scale numerals printed on the glass, so they carry a lit halo and drop a small shadow onto the diffuser behind them; the AM scale in tens (55 to 160) with its logarithmic crowding at the low end; the red hairline pointer with a hot tip and a faint halo; the flywheel rim drawn as a cylinder, its knurl ridges placed by angle and crowding toward the ends of the slot, spinning as the pointer moves and carrying on with inertia after a flick (~900 ms decay); the tuning and signal meters as static lit windows; the fascia's horizontal grain, and the black bezel line around the glass. The wheel takes horizontal drag, a mouse wheel and the arrow keys; band="AM" re-maps the pointer to the lower scale.

Left out: the Marantz script and model number on the fascia, the stereo lamp, the live meter needles, and the fascia's other controls.
