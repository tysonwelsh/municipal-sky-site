THE SOURCE

The pair of big Smiths instruments in the middle of a Jaguar E-Type binnacle — an RV-series tachometer and an SN-series speedometer, five inches across, matte black dials, white printing, and a chrome bezel so fat it is the first thing you see. Between the bezel and the domed glass is a thin black rubber seal. The tachometer carries a small inset clock at six o'clock; the speedometer carries the five-drum odometer with a red trip digit in the same place.

The tells kept here: the figures run 0 to 60 in hundreds over 270 degrees from about seven o'clock, every one of them upright to the page and slightly condensed; the red is a thin painted line inside the tick ring from 5,500 to 6,000, not a wedge; the bezel is twelve pixels of chrome with a hard horizon — sky above the middle, ground below — a bright top-left highlight and a dark lower right; the needle is a white blade with its own shadow, rising from a big black boss with a chrome ring and a dome highlight; "RPM ×100" in two lines above the boss and "MADE IN ENGLAND" in tiny caps below it; the black is not flat but lifts a little toward the centre under the domed glass, which carries one soft elliptical glint in the upper left. The needle has magnetic-drag lag: about 400 ms with a two per cent overshoot, and a half-degree flutter whenever the engine is under a thousand.

Left out: the Smiths wordmark and the chronometric tick of the earlier instruments; the odometer drums do not roll between digits (they are set, not animated); the setting attribute is called dial rather than role so that the host can keep role="meter".
