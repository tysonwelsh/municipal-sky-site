THE SOURCE

The Revox A77 carries its counter on the head block, between the reels: a small rectangular chrome frame no bigger than a postage stamp, standing about a millimetre proud of the brushed aluminium plate, with four black drums and white figures behind it, and a shallow black button in a chrome collar beside it that zeroes the whole thing with a click you can feel through the deck.

The tells kept here: the frame is thin — two pixels — with two-pixel corner radii, and its chrome has a hard horizon, sky above and plate below, rather than a soft sheen; the plate's brush runs horizontally and catches one broad highlight across the grain; the drums are white-on-black and read very small and precise, with a one-pixel gap and the frame's inner shadow between them and the chrome; every figure is placed on its cylinder by angle and squashed by cos θ, so the units drum rolls through the window instead of sliding; on rewind it spins fast and smears — a second copy of the drums at thirty per cent, three pixels above; the reset button is unmistakably a button and not a knob, a shallow dome with one highlight up and to the left; a small engraved COUNTER below the frame; and the whole assembly drops a one-pixel shadow onto the plate. Reset spins the drums to 0000 in 300 ms and the button goes down two pixels for eighty.

Left out: the mechanical linkage to the supply reel, the maker's name (the legend here is generic), and the tape path either side of the block.
