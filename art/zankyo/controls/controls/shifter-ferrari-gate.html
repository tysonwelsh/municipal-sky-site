THE SOURCE

From the 250 to the F355, a Ferrari's gear lever stands in an open chrome gate: a polished plate screwed to the transmission tunnel with the H cut clean through it, so the black of the tunnel shows in the slots. The pattern is a dogleg — first is back and to the left, on its own, with 2–3 in the middle slot and 4–5 on the right, and reverse forward beside first — and every change is two straight legs with a stop between them: out to the crossbar, across, then down into the slot. It is the only gear lever anybody has ever wanted to hear.

The tells kept here: the plate seen foreshortened from the driver's seat with the stem rising toward you; chrome with a hard horizon across it, sky above and ground below; slots cut through, with the lit lip on their near side and the shadowed wall on the far side; four countersunk chrome screws; the black Bakelite ball with one hot highlight and the gate pattern engraved on its crown in white; the leather boot showing as a dark rim under the plate's edge. A change runs along the crossbar and then down the slot, about 80 ms a leg, and lands with a small metallic shudder.

Left out: the badge on the crown of the knob, the lock-out collar for reverse on later cars, and the tunnel's stitching, which sits below the frame.
