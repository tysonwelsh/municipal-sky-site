THE SOURCE

The Boss compact pedal — the OD-1 in 1977, then the DS-1, CE-2 and dozens more — put a whole effect in a small die-cast box a guitarist could work with one shoe: a footswitch that is not a button but a hinged treadle, the full-width plate rocking down on a front hinge, with a ribbed rubber pad set into it so a wet or worn boot still finds grip. A red LED above the treadle shows the effect is latched in, and the colour of the enamel says which pedal it is before you can read the name.

The tells kept here: the treadle as a hinged plate, not a button, rocking about its front edge with a shadowed crease at the hinge; the twelve-rib rubber pad; the LED lighting on the press, the way Boss wired it, not the release; the coloured die-cast body with a soft orange-peel sheen and a rounded top edge; the black screen-printed name band; the front thumbscrew for the battery door and the two jack nuts on the sides; a faint scuff on the pad where a shoe lands.

Left out: the input/output jacks themselves, the battery compartment, the true three-position bypass-relay click; the three knobs up top are drawn as panel decoration, not live controls.
