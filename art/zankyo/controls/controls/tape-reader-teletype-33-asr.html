THE SOURCE

The Teletype Model 33 ASR's reader sat to the left of the keyboard: a light-grey steel housing with a hinged clear lid over a toothed sprocket wheel, reading 1-inch buff paper tape punched with up to eight holes per column plus a smaller, off-centre sprocket hole. A four-position lever — OFF, START, FREE, STOP — ran the tape, released it for hand-feeding, or held it still; the punch beside it chattered fresh tape into a chad box.

The tells kept here: the tape's eight data rows with the sprocket hole sitting between rows three and four, not in the middle; the sprocket's teeth advancing the tape one column per character as it runs; the acrylic lid, which opens on click to show the sprocket bare; the little heap of chad; the lever's own track and labels. Running scrolls the tape and turns the sprocket in step, emitting one event per character.

Left out: the companion punch mechanism's own paper path and the keyboard it sat beside; fanfold takeup is only suggested by the tape curling off both edges.
