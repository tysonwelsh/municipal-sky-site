THE SOURCE

The DSKY ("dis-key," Display and Keyboard) was the astronaut's only window into the Apollo Guidance Computer: a grey painted-aluminium panel the size of a paperback, a 19-key keypad of VERB, NOUN, the ten digits, the two signs, CLR, PRO, KEY REL, ENTR and RSET, and above it a block of amber-and-white status lamps beside a green electroluminescent display of three five-digit signed registers under PROG / VERB / NOUN. Every command to the computer was typed as a "Verb Noun" pair — Verb 16 Noun 36 to display the mission time — and confirmed with ENTR.

The tells kept here: the keys as dark squares with a legend that glows rather than prints, dropping 2 px and brightening on press; the registers as seven-segment digits built from rounded, slightly italic strokes, an electroluminescent green with a soft bleed at the edges; VERB and NOUN flashing together when the computer wants an answer; the status lamps as flat, hotspot-free rectangles, lit amber or white on black; the COMP ACTY square in the display's corner. Keyboard: digits, V/N for VERB/NOUN, Enter for ENTR, Esc for CLR, arrows move between keys.

Left out: the alarm lamps' full 14-name set (a representative twelve are wired), the physical rear connector, and the AGC's actual verb/noun program logic — this reproduces the panel, not the computer.
