THE SOURCE

On a blackface Fender — a Deluxe Reverb, a Twin, a Bassman — the pilot lamp is a one-inch faceted acrylic jewel screwed into a fluted chrome bezel, with a plain #47 bayonet bulb behind it. It stands well proud of the black tolex-covered panel, next to the power switch, and it is the one bright thing on the whole front of the amplifier.

The tells kept here: the dome is cut in three rings of facets — eight trapezoids outside, eight more inside them, eight triangles meeting at the centre — each one a flat plane with its own shading, so the lit lamp reads as a starburst and not a smooth glow. The brightest facets are the ones nearest the filament, which sits low and to the left, never in the middle of the dome; the upper facets run cooler and dimmer. Unlit, the jewel is deep wine red and you can see the pale bulb glass and its little hairpin filament through the centre facets. The bezel's twenty-four flutes are shaded one by one against a single light above-left, it casts a crescent shadow down and right onto the panel, and when the lamp is on the flutes nearest the jewel pick up a pink tint and a soft red halo spreads onto the panel. The acrylic is yellowed and has two hairline crazing cracks on the outer facets, and one white specular glint sits at eleven o'clock whether the lamp is lit or not. It is an incandescent: 140 ms to come up, 320 ms to die, going orange on the way down.

Left out: the threaded barrel and spring clip behind the panel, the bulb's bayonet pins, and the amp's own script logo. color also accepts blue and green, and flicker adds the 8 Hz shimmer of a tired lamp.
