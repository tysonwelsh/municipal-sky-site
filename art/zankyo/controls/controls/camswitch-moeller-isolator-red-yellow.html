THE SOURCE

The Klöckner-Moeller T0 cam switch is the little brown-box rotary that sits on the side of every German machine tool, lathe cabinet and compressor. In its main-switch dress it wears the IEC colours: a fat red moulded handle on a bright yellow plate, printed 0 at ten o'clock and I at twelve, and a padlock hole through the handle's base that only lines up with the collar's lug when the switch is at 0 — so the fitter can hang his own lock on it and work on the machine knowing nobody can energise it.

The tells kept here: red on yellow and nothing else coloured, on a grey enclosure; the tapered pistol grip with the raised white pointer stripe; the handle standing well proud, its shadow thrown down and to the right across the plate and turning with it; the four cross-head screws and the raised rim of the legend plate; the small black collar the handle turns in, with the padlock lug at 0; the printed legend in a grotesque along the bottom edge. The detent is real: each step takes 120 ms and lands with a small jolt. locked hangs a brass padlock through the hole and the handle will not move. The control variant is the same casting in black on a grey-white plate with 1·2·3.

Left out: the enclosure's cable glands and the stack of cam contacts behind the panel; the rating plate on the box's side; the "Klöckner-Moeller" wordmark, which is a live trademark.
