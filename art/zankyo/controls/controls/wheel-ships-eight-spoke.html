THE SOURCE

A clipper- and steam-era ship's wheel: a segmented, oiled-teak rim in eight scarfed sections joined by brass ferrules, turned mahogany spokes passing through the rim into baluster handles beyond it, and a domed brass hub carrying the maker's plate. The king spoke — brass-sleeved here — points straight up when the rudder is amidships, so a helmsman can feel the wheel's position without looking.

The tells kept here: the spokes running through the rim into turned, foreshortened handles that swell and neck like lathed wood; eight scarf joints, each with a brass ferrule band; the domed hub with a horizon highlight and an engraved maker's plate curving around the boss; grain streaks following the rim's own curve; darker "hand-oil" shading at the handle swells and a paler forward face on the rim. The wheel takes several turns lock-to-lock: dragging the rim or a handle turns it by the pointer's angle around the hub (accumulating past 360°, not by a single snapped angle), the rudder value derived from the gearing set by turns; the ends thud gently. Arrows step 5° of rudder; the wheel steps too; return adds a slow 2 s spring home; endless removes the lock stops for a valve-wheel-style demo.

Left out: the pedestal, the engine-room telegraph, the rope Turk's-head texture (a plain brass sleeve stands in for it), the exact scarf-joint carpentry.
