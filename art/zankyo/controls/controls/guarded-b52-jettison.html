THE SOURCE

On a B-52's pilot and bombardier panels the pushes that cannot be pressed by accident live under red-anodised aluminium flip-guards: a machined lid with a rolled free edge and a finger notch, on a bracket screwed to the panel, sprung so it snaps shut on its own, stencilled with the same word that is silk-screened on the black wrinkle panel below it. JETTISON is the one everybody remembers. The push underneath is an ordinary Cutler-Hammer red dome in a black rim — deliberately ordinary, because the guard is the safety, not the button.

The tells kept here: the anodised red with a directional brushing grain running across the lid and one broad highlight band lying over it; the rolled front edge and the notch you hook a gloved finger into; the stencil, cut with two bridges through the closed counter of the O so the island stays attached, as a real stencil brush demands; the bare-aluminium hinge pin between two red knuckles on a screwed bracket; the second JETTISON silk-screened on the panel; the wrinkle paint as a fine crinkle with a chip at the lower-left of the mounting hole showing bare metal. The lid flips in 160 ms and bounces once against its stop; closing snaps. The push travels 2 px and stops hard, and activate only fires with the guard open.

Left out, and one deliberate change: the guard's coil spring, and the hinge is drawn at the top of the lid rather than the bottom, so that the open guard stands clear above the push the way it does in the cockpit photographs instead of falling across the panel below it.
