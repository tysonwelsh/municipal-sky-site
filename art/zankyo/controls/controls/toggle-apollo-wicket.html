THE SOURCE

The Apollo Command Module's Main Display Console is a grey aluminium wall of bat toggles, and almost every one of them stands between a pair of wicket guards — two short rails bolted proud of the panel, taller than the bat's throw, so that a pressure-suited glove, a drifting checklist or a floating pen cannot catch the handle sideways. The switches themselves are Micro Switch / Grace parts: a stubby chrome bat with a ball tip on a black anodised bushing, clamped by a big flat twelve-point nut over a serrated lock washer.

The tells kept here: the two rails, drawn first because they are the first thing you see; the short bat with its spherical tip, which in the up position brings the ball to just under the top of the rails; the twelve-point nut with its chamfer facets alternating light and dark around the light source, and the lock washer's teeth peeking out from under its edge; the black-filled engraved legend on mid-grey paint, the Apollo way round, never white on black; the keyway notch at twelve o'clock and the black crescent where the panel hole is a hair larger than the bushing; the lathe rings on the bushing face; a soft shadow from the rails down and right, a harder, smaller one from the bat. Because the bat leans toward you rather than across the panel, it foreshortens: at the centre stop of the three-position switch you see the ball sitting on the bushing, a little larger for being nearer.

Left out: the console's module outlines and the circuit-breaker rows around it, the numbered switch index printed beside each group, and the rear wiring lugs. The legends are generic Apollo subsystem words rather than any one panel's exact set.
