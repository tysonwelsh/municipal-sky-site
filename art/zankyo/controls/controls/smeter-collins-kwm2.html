THE SOURCE

Collins receivers and transceivers — the 75A-4, the KWM-2, the 51S-1 — carry a small square signal-strength meter let into the grey-green wrinkle paint the factory called St. James Gray. It is a two-inch movement behind a square black phenolic flange and a slightly domed glass, and the card behind it is lit from the dial lamps so the whole face sits in a warm pool of light.

The tells kept here: the scale changes colour at nine — black figures 1 3 5 7 9 over the left, red +20 +40 +60 compressed over the right, with a fat red tick standing at S9 itself; the 90° arc on a square face with the pivot low and a small black boss; the knife-edge pointer with its counterweight cross peeking below the boss; four fine screws in the flange and a slotted zero-adjust under the window; the wrinkle panel's fine random cell texture with two chrome hex screws at the meter's corners; "S-UNITS" and "DB OVER S9" set tiny under their halves of the arc. The movement is fast — about 150 ms up, 400 ms back down as the AGC lets go — and at rest the needle breathes a little on band noise.

Left out: the meter's rear terminals and the zero-set potentiometer behind the panel; the manufacturer's script on the card, which is replaced by a plain caption.
