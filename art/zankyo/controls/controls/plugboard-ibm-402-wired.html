THE SOURCE

The IBM 402 / 407 tabulator's control panel is a black phenolic board a machine-room clerk could lift out and swap: hundreds of brass hub holes in labelled rows, and between them, plugged by hand, hundreds of short self-contacting wires in yellow, red, blue and black — the colour telling a technician the wire's length at a glance.

The tells kept here: the sheer density of the wiring — wires arcing up and across each other, each with a bright chrome tip standing proud in its hub; the colour coding by distance (short yellow, longer red, longer still blue, longest black); the tight grid of brass rings on black phenolic with silk-screened column numerals; the board as a slab in a satin-steel frame. Click a hub, then a second hub, to run a wire; click an existing wire to pull it; arrow keys move a cursor ring and Space picks or completes.

Left out: the row group labels (READING, PRINT, COUNTER ENTRY …), the carrying handle, and the true 40×66 board — this is a manageable slice, as the entry allows.
