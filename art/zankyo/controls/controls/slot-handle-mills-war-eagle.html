THE SOURCE

The Mills Novelty "War Eagle" of 1931 is a cast-aluminium one-armed bandit painted blue and gold, its reels behind glass and, bolted off the right edge of the case on a chrome bracket, the pull handle: a chrome-plated steel rod ending in a black phenolite ball, cocking the reel spring as it swings down through about sixty degrees.

The tells kept here: the chrome rod's hard highlight-and-shadow pair running its length, like a horizon line bent around a cylinder; the ball as a true sphere with a small hot spot at ten o'clock and a faint cast of the cabinet's blue in its lower half; the chrome worn dull to grey in the band where a hand grips below the ball; a sliver of the cabinet's blue-and-gold cast edge and the chrome mounting bracket the lever pivots from. The pull has weight — it lags the pointer and stiffens as it nears the bottom — and the return snaps back over 400 ms with a small bounce at the top. It fires activate only if the pull reaches the bottom; let go early and it just springs back.

Left out: the cast-eagle front, the reel windows and the jackpot glass — this build is the handle alone, as the brief asks for.
