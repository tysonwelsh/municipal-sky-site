THE SOURCE

Cockpit annunciator and placard panels from Grimes, Boeing and Douglas were built the same way from the 1940s on: a clear acrylic sheet painted satin black on the face, with the legend and index lines engraved right through the paint to the white backing. Lamps fed into the sheet's edge light the whole piece by total internal reflection, so only the engraved cuts and the sheet's own edge ever glow.

The tells kept here: unlit, the legend is a flat pale grey — the white backing showing through a paint cut, with a cutter's faint fuzz; lit, each letter glows evenly from within with a soft halo bleeding into the black around it, and nothing else on the panel lights up; the sheet's own edge is the brightest line on the piece; color="red" switches to the saturated, low-halo red of night lighting; the black paint carries one soft satin reflection so it reads as glossy plastic, not chalk. brightness dims the glow and the edge line together, the way a cockpit rheostat would. Lamps fade up over about 150 ms and down over about 300 ms. With wear, a chip in the paint near a screw leaks a small irregular fleck of light when lit.

Left out: the panel's mounting frame and the specific wording of any real aircraft placard (the legends here are generic).
