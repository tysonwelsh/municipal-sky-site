THE SOURCE

The GMT-Master bezel of 1955 was made for Pan Am crews: a steel ring with a coin-edge carrying a 24-hour insert split into two colours, red for the daylight hours from 6 to 18 and blue for the night, so a second hand pointing at the bezel gives a second time zone and tells you whether it is day or night there. The 6542 insert was bakelite with the numerals lume-filled from behind, later replaced by anodised aluminium because the bakelite cracked; both fade with the sun to a pink and a soft blue. The bezel turns both ways with 24 clicks, so you set the offset by turning the 24 triangle to the hour hand.

The tells kept here: the red/blue split with the boundary exactly at 6 and 18; even numerals only, set radially in Barlow so the 12 at the bottom is upside down, and the lume triangle at 24; a coin-edge of 96 teeth, each with two facets lit from above-left so the ring is bright at the top-left and dark at the bottom-right; the insert set below the steel lip with an inner shadow and a domed sheen that is the shape of the room's light, not of the numerals; a fixed lume index on the dial's rehaut to read the offset against. The value is the hour that stands at 12 o'clock (0 is the triangle; 19 is New York, five hours back). Drag follows the pointer's angle around the centre, clicking either way; the wheel and the arrows click one hour, Shift-arrows ten, Home turns it round to the triangle, End to 23. half gives the 48-click half-hour bezel; faded the sun-bleached one.

Left out: the case, crown and crown guards, the hands and the dial printing (an empty dial keeps the bezel the subject), the cracks in the bakelite.
