THE SOURCE

The GM headlamp switch sat at the left of the dash of every Chevrolet, GMC, Pontiac, Buick and Oldsmobile for forty years: a plain black knob on a chrome shaft through a chrome bezel. Pull it one click for the parking lamps, a second click for the headlamps; turn it to run the rheostat that dims the instrument lights, and turn it past the last detent, with a click, to light the dome lamp. Ford and Chrysler did the same thing with a different knob.

The tells kept here: the shaft, drawn in a raked side elevation so that more chrome shows and the knob's shadow on the dash lengthens as it comes out to its two stops; the black satin knob with its stamped headlamp pictogram whose top edges catch the light, turning with the rheostat; the dash legend that follows dim; the small dome-lamp glyph that lights on the last clockwise click, with a warm cast on the vinyl around it; the chrome bezel with pitting where the plating has lifted. A click pulls one stop (OFF, PARK, HEAD, and back in); an outward drag pulls, an inward drag pushes; a vertical drag or the wheel turns for dim, and going on past full brightness clicks the dome on. Space pulls, arrows dim, Home is dark, End is the dome. Events carry { value, dim, dome }.

Left out: the wiper switch and cigarette lighter that keep it company on the dash, the ignition-key buzzer, the plating flaking off the shaft on a rusty truck.
