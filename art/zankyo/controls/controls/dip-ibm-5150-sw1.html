THE SOURCE

The IBM PC 5150 motherboard carries two eight-way slide DIP blocks, SW1 and SW2, soldered straight to the board: a moulded blue (some batches brick red) plastic body with eight tiny ivory slider nubs in a row, each in its own dark well, "ON" silk-screened at the top edge with a little arrow and the numerals 1–8 along the bottom, on a green board with a couple of pale traces and a "SW1" silk-screen label beside it.

The tells kept here: the blue-and-ivory colour pairing; the nub filling only the top or bottom half of its well so the other half stays dark; the block seen dead-on from above with a bevelled, slightly domed face; the tin pins peeking from the board under the block's long edges; one nub sitting two degrees askew as if a pen had nudged it; and a cursor that turns to a crosshair over the nubs, because a real finger is too big for them. A click flips one nub, a drag along the row sets every nub it passes to the direction of the drag, and arrows move a focus box between nubs with Space to flip the one that's focused.

Left out: the chip's part-number moulding, the board's other components, and the pin count matching a real 16-pin DIP package exactly.
