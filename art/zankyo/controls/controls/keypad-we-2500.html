THE SOURCE

The Western Electric 2500 is the Touch-Tone desk telephone: the 500 rotary set with the dial replaced by twelve keys, from 1968 until the Bell System stopped making them. Twelve glossy white ABS keys in a 3 × 4 grid, let into a rectangular recess in the housing, with the numeral large and the letters small beneath it, OPER under the 0, and * and # in the bottom corners. The housing came in black, ivory, beige, red, green, yellow and — this being 1972 — avocado.

The tells kept here: two weights on one key, a heavy Helvetica numeral over 6 px letters; 1 with no letters, 7 = PRS and 9 = WXY because Q and Z were left out; the six-pointed Bell asterisk and the upright octothorpe drawn as paths rather than typed; the slight dome on the key top with its soft highlight above and a hairline bright rim below; the whole block let into a recess whose floor is the housing colour, with a dark gap around every key; the top row yellower than the rest and a line of grime along the recess edge. A press travels 2 px in 50 ms and comes back spongily in 110 ms, and the key brightens for a frame where the DTMF pair would sound.

Left out: the handset, the cradle, the finger-wheel-era number card and its plastic window, the network block behind the keys, and the tones themselves — the element has no audio.
