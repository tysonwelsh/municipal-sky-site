THE SOURCE

The Gray Telephone Pay Station's coin head, made by Western Electric from 1911 into the 1960s, is the nickel-plated fascia on top of every black wrinkle-enamel payphone case: three slots of three different widths side by side, "5¢" "10¢" "25¢" cast into the metal above them, the nickel worn warm and slightly dull from decades of thumbs. A nickel drops with a single bell, a dime with a double bell, a quarter with a gong — the operator (and later the mechanism) told them apart by ear as much as by sight.

The tells kept here: three different-width chutes, narrowest to widest, left to right; the cast relief numerals, a light top edge and a dark underside on the same nickel; the warm, slightly dull plating with a hard specular on the cast edges; a coin that tilts ninety degrees and drops into the slot's plane when a station is activated, named with its chime in the event detail. Each slot is a real button: click it, Tab to it and press Space or Enter, or move between slots with the arrow keys.

Left out: the coin-return lever and cup, the black case below the head, the brass lock, the wear patch worn to brass around the quarter slot.
