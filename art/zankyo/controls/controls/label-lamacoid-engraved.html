THE SOURCE

The lamacoid — a two-layer laminate, a thin coloured cap over a white (or black) core — engraved on a rotary cutter so the legend is the core showing through, not printed ink. It rides every equipment bay, aircraft placard and rack module from the 1940s on: two small screws at the ends, or, for a lighter fixing, a strip of double-sided tape that never sits quite straight.

The tells kept here: monoline letters with rounded terminals and a soft half-pixel fringe where the cutter's cone widened the cut at the surface; a pale hairline all round the edge where the bevel exposes the core; the plate sitting a pixel proud with a hairline contact shadow; two slotted screws with their slots not aligned; a low satin sheen, never a gloss. The tape-mounted variant drops the screws, tilts a degree or two, and gets a broader, softer lift-shadow instead of the screws' tight one.

Left out: the engraver's occasional slightly-off baseline wobble, and a fully chipped corner (the wear hook is wired but not shown here).
