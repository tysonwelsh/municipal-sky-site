THE SOURCE

Straight ahead of the captain on a 737-200 or -300, on the black glareshield above the instrument panel, sit three things: a square amber MASTER CAUTION light, the six-light system annunciator everybody calls the six-pack, and a square red FIRE WARN light. All three are Korry-type dead-face caps — opaque black faces with the legend cut through them, so nothing shows until a lamp behind it comes on. A system fault lights its tile in the six-pack and MASTER CAUTION together; pushing MASTER CAUTION acknowledges it and puts it out, leaving the tile lit so you still know what broke.

The tells kept here: the dead face — unlit, the caps are near-black and the legends are only a barely-there grey ghost of the letters. Only the letters light, never the tile: the face stays black around glowing amber text, and the letters bloom a little past their strokes and tint the face around them. The six-pack is two columns of three in the fixed order FLT CONT · IRS · FUEL over ELEC · APU · OVHT/DET, with OVHT/DET wrapping to two lines. FIRE WARN is the one red thing on a panel of amber. The two square caps sit apart from the six-pack with a hairline gap all round, because they are buttons, and each carries the horizontal split line of a two-lamp Korry cap. Each cap has a slight brightness gradient across it — the lamp is at one end. The glareshield itself is matte black with a faint diagonal sheen and no screws: the caps are retained from behind. Lamps come up in 120 ms and die in 250 ms.

Left out: the dimming/test switch, the six-pack's rear lamp cartridges, and the FO's mirrored set on the right of the glareshield. flash puts FIRE WARN on a 1 Hz flash with the fire bell implied; the second instance here shows the steady state instead so the still frame is honest.
