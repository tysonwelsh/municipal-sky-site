THE SOURCE

The Patlite LR stack sits on top of every CNC machine in Japan: three 60 mm segments of clear polycarbonate, tinted red, amber and green and moulded with fine vertical flutes, screwed one on top of the other, a black buzzer cap with a slot grille above them and a short pole into a round flange base. Red on top, always. Green means the spindle is turning; amber means it wants something; red and the buzzer means it has stopped and someone is walking over.

The tells kept here: the flutes — nineteen ribs per tier placed by angle around the cylinder (x = R·sin θ, width and brightness by cos θ), so they crowd at the edges; unlit they are dark tinted glass with one thin light line each and the pale reflector showing through from inside; lit, the lamp turns them into a comb of bright verticals over a broad soft highlight. A lit tier throws its colour down onto the tier below it and onto the pole, and a halo of it onto the panel behind. A hairline collar rings each joint, the pole is a little cylinder of its own, and the base has two screw heads. The lamps are LED-fast: about 60 ms up, 90 ms down.

Left out: the moulded maker's name on the cap (a generic boss instead), the cable gland out of the base, the tiers' bayonet twist-lock lugs, and the buzzer's actual noise — pulse only shakes the cap, which is what you see from across a shop floor.
