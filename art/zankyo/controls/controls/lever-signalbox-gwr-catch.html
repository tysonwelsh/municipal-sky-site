THE SOURCE

The Saxby & Farmer pattern lever frame is the row of tall painted levers a signalman heaves in a British mechanical signal box, one per signal, point or lock, standard on the GWR, LMS and LNER from the 1870s into preservation today. Each lever stands upright (NORMAL) in a notch on a cast quadrant plate and is pulled back toward the signalman (REVERSED) to work its wire or rod; a spring catch under the top grip must be squeezed before the lever will leave its notch, so a lever cannot be pulled by a stray touch.

The tells kept here: the paint stopping in a hard line where a bright polished-steel grip begins; the small catch handle just below the grip that closes 6° and lifts a rod out of the quadrant notch when squeezed (press and hold, or Space); the block colour code — red for stop signals, black for points — carried by the three levers in the demo; the round brass number plate and the white enamel description plate riding on the lever's face; the quadrant plate's two notches and its bright arc of wear; the floorboards and a brass strip underfoot; the long ~50° throw with a hard clack and jolt as it lands, and the softer 350 ms return.

Left out: the wire and crank below the floor, the interlocking that would refuse some levers when others are reversed, the full frame of the six colours together (built here as three, at a common scale).
