THE SOURCE

The lever-lock toggle on the Apollo Command Module's Main Display Console is a plain MS-type chrome bat toggle with one extra part threaded onto its base: a short knurled chrome sleeve that must be lifted before the bat can throw, so a stray glove or a floating checklist cannot flip a critical switch in flight. It sits on the same grey panel, same black-filled engraved legend, as the console's ungaurded toggles beside it.

The tells kept here: the knurled collar riding the bat's base, visibly a separate turned part; a pointerdown that lifts the bat 2 px and slides the collar up before the throw is allowed — try it without holding and the bat only shivers and refuses; the release drop with a one-frame bounce as the collar reseats over the bushing's tooth; the small engraved rule under the switch name standing in for the lock legend; the gap of shadow between collar and bushing that closes when locked. Drag (a short pull down/away then a vertical throw), click-and-hold, Space/Enter, and arrows all work; a plain click shakes the bat and does nothing.

Left out: the hex nut's exact thread pitch, the console's engraved reference designator numbers that would sit beside the legend on the real panel.
