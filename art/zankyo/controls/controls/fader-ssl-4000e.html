THE SOURCE

The SSL 4000 E channel fader is a Penny & Giles 100 mm conductive-plastic fader let into the bottom of every module of the console that mixed most of the 1980s: a black anodised slot rail on a light grey painted module face, a felt dust brush parting in the slot around the stem, and the knob that any session engineer recognises across the room — a plain rectangular block in a desaturated blue-grey that no other console used, wider than tall, with a shallow dish on top and no index line. On the G-series a 3 mm red fader-status LED sits at the head of the rail in a black holder.

The tells kept here: the blue-grey block knob with its barely-there concave dish and one soft polish on top; the felt brush strips meeting in a hairline down the slot; the audio-taper scale printed white to the right of the slot — +10, +5, 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 60, ∞ — with the 0 tick longer and the spacing crushing toward ∞; the LED and its holder at the top right; the two cross-head screws at the ends of the rail; a grime shadow in the slot below the knob's usual resting place near 0 dB. The knob follows the pointer exactly along the slot; the wheel steps 0.5 dB; arrows step 0.5 dB and Shift-arrows 5 dB; Home/End go to ∞ and +10. The LED is a status lamp and does not follow the value.

Left out: the mute and solo buttons above the fader, the VCA group selector, the module's scribble strip.
