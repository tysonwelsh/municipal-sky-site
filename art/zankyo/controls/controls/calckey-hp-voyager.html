THE SOURCE

The HP Voyager calculators — the 10C, 11C, 12C, 15C and 16C of 1981–82 — are a landscape slab with a three-legend keyboard: the unshifted function in white on top of the key, the f-shifted one in gold silk-screened on the case above the key, and the g-shifted one in blue on the key's own front slope, where it faces you as you look down at the machine. Two shift keys carry those colours: a gold f and a blue g, both legended in black.

The tells kept here: all three legends in their three places; the key as a small rounded block with a faintly cylindrical top and a visibly sloped front face in a lighter shade, narrowing toward the bottom; the hairline of case that shows between keys, which are close but never touch; the brushed-and-lacquered dark brown-black case with a fine horizontal grain and the room's light gathered at the top-left; the gold-and-blue pair a shade deeper than their own legends. The click is HP's: one pixel of travel over 25 ms with the top face darkening for a frame as it goes, and activate on the press rather than the release — a Voyager registers on the way down.

Left out: the LCD and its bezel above the block, the serial-number plate and the rubber feet, and the two-row ENTER — the tall attribute builds it (one block spanning two rows, legend centred) but the stage here shows the 7 key with its shift pair instead.
