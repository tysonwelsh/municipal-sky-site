THE SOURCE

The Casio F-91W (1989) is the best-selling watch ever made and the most-cloned LCD face in the world: a moulded black resin case around a reflective TN LCD, showing HH:MM big, seconds smaller at the right, a tiny day and date top-left, and small alarm/signal flags along a printed rule under the top edge. A single micro-lamp behind the glass tints the whole field green-amber when the LIGHT button is held.

The tells kept here: slanted seven-segment digits built as SVG paths, never a font; every unlit segment stays faintly visible as a grey-green ghost, the LCD's most recognisable quality; each lit segment carries a small offset duplicate underneath (the polariser's doubled image), so the digits read as very slightly embossed; a 5×7 blocky glass-mask font for the day letters and the AM/PM flag; alarm and signal pictograms that ghost the same way as the digits; a warm amber-green wash from the lower-left corner when `light` is set; and the resin bezel's small "LIGHT · ALARM · MODE" legends with a thin cyan rule above the glass. Segments switch on hard and fade off over ~90 ms, the LCD's characteristic slow release.

Left out: the full 14-segment day/date table (a compact 5×7 block font stands in), the stopwatch and alarm-set modes, and the four case buttons.
