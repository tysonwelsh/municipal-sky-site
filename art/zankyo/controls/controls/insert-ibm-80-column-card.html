THE SOURCE

The IBM 80-column card — the 1928 Hollerith form, 7⅜ by 3¼ inches, corner cut top-left so a back-to-front or upside-down card is obvious at a glance — fed every tabulator, sorter, and keypunch from the 1930s to the 1970s. A spring-loaded hopper holds a deck on edge under a chrome weight; the read station pulls one card at a time under a guide with a column pointer, twelve rows of tiny holes running down each of its eighty columns.

The tells kept here: the 45° corner cut and the deck's stepped-diagonal shadow behind the weight; the dense grey column-of-digits printing that reads as texture at a glance and as ten digits up close; real punches — the text attribute is encoded in simplified Hollerith code (a digit punches its own row; a letter punches a zone row, 12, 11 or 0, plus a digit row) and the interpreted characters are printed along the top edge the way an IBM 029 printed them; the satin-steel hopper and its chrome weight bar; the card's slight edge rounding and its hard 1 px shadow on the guide. Click the station or press Space to feed a card in or eject it.

Left out: the moving read brushes, the stacker the card falls into after reading, the "DO NOT FOLD SPINDLE OR MUTILATE" legend some decks carried.
