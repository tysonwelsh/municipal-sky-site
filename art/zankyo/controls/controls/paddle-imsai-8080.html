THE SOURCE

The IMSAI 8080 of 1975 answered the Altair's tiny toggles with twenty-two flat paddle switches — blue for address and data, red for the control group — in a row of black slots under sixteen diffused red LEDs, on a black anodised panel silk-screened with the bit numbers.

The tells kept here: the paddle as a real flat blade hinged at the panel and seen from slightly above, so a switch that is up shows only its narrow lit top edge with a long shadow falling down the panel, and one that is down presents the whole coloured face; the 5:1 width-to-thickness and the square-cut tip; the slot a hair wider than the blade with a pivot pin showing grey at each side; the ridge moulded along the centre line; the LEDs as dull dark red discs in black grommets that go a flat orange-red when lit, with a small halo and no lens flare; the red control paddles springing back to centre; and a hairline scratch across the anodising below the row.

Left out: the full twenty-two-switch panel with its address group, the blue-grey legend band, the IMSAI badge, and the paddle's moulded part number.
