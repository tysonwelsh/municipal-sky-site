THE SOURCE

A century of drive recesses: the straight slot of pre-war brass, Phillips' tapered cross that took over 1950s American gear, Pozidriv's four extra ticks on European hi-fi, JIS's telltale dot beside a Phillips-like cross on Japanese chassis, the hex socket of black-anodised synth panels, Torx on 1980s automotive trim, and the plain knurled ring of a thumbscrew. Each finish reads by the colour and hardness of its highlight as much as by the metal itself.

The tells kept here: the recess is drawn specific to each drive — a tapered rounded-centre cross for Phillips, the four 45° ticks added for Pozidriv, a small dot for JIS, a dark hexagonal well, a six-lobed Torx star, a knurled rim with no recess for the thumbscrew; every head is a small dome lit from above-left with a shadow rim below-right and its own contact shadow; finish reads in the highlight — nickel hard and cool, brass warm, black-oxide nearly flat, cad-yellow with a green-to-orange shimmer; slots and crosses sit at random angles unless angle is set; a cup washer's black nylon dish and a star washer's twelve teeth sit under the head as their own ring.

Left out: the burr-and-grime wear state (the entry allows it; this build keeps every head pristine), and Pozidriv's and JIS's rarer head-shape variants.
