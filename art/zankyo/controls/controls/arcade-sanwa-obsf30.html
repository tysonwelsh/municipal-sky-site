THE SOURCE

The Sanwa Denshi OBSF-30 is the snap-in 30 mm button on the control panel of every Japanese candy cabinet since the early 1990s — Astro City, Blast City, Versus City, and then every fight stick built in the thirty years since. It is a short-throw microswitch button in a translucent polycarbonate cap, and the whole design is one idea: a big flat disc of colour with almost no travel.

The tells kept here: the plunger top is genuinely flat — the colour is even right out to about 85 % of the radius, and the convex roll happens only in the last two pixels, which is why a Sanwa reads as a disc and a Happ reads as a bowl; the same-colour bezel rim is a thin 1.75 mm flange with a black hairline gap between it and the plunger; the cap is translucent, so there is a warm core of light bouncing back off the white body inside it, brightest just below centre; a broad soft highlight crescent sits at ten o'clock and a thin bright line of piped light runs round the inside of the cap's edge; and a fine reflected-light line lies on the bottom-right of the rim. Pressed, the cap drops one pixel and the gap ring goes black. These are new buttons, so the only grime is the dark ring in the gap.

Left out: the two 4.8 mm spade terminals and the microswitch body under the panel, the snap-in tabs, and the six-button Vewlix arc — three colours in a row show the palette better on a bench. The little Michroma legends are the panel's, not the button's.
