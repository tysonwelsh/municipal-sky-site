THE SOURCE

The Smiths Industries centre-zero ammeter — badged Lucas on many cars — is the 2-inch gauge that sits in a walnut or crackle-black dash and tells you whether the dynamo is putting current into the battery or the battery is giving it away. It is the plainest instrument on the car: a matte black dial, three numerals (30, 0, 30), a tick every ten amperes, a "−" at one end and a "+" at the other, a long white needle hinged low on the dial, and a chrome bezel rolled over domed glass.

The tells kept here: only three numerals and the two signs; ticks every ten with a finer one at each five; the needle dead vertical at zero, which is the first state shown; the chrome bezel with its hard horizon — sky above, ground below, and a bright specular where the light comes in above-left; the domed glass with one soft elliptical highlight at the top-left and a curved catch along the lower rim, neither of which is the shape of anything on the dial; the pivot low on the face so the needle is long and the sweep is about 90°; "AMPERES" in small white caps under the zero; and the black-crackle case ring showing as a narrow band outside the chrome. The needle is a little heavy: a damped approach of about 250 ms with a small overshoot, stopped when the element leaves the page.

Left out: the maker's name and the "Lucas" script (a build never reproduces a logo), the two rear studs the cables bolt to, and the slight yellowing the glass takes on after fifty years on a dashboard.
