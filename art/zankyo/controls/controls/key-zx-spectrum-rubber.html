THE SOURCE

The ZX Spectrum 48K (Sinclair, 1982; industrial design by Rick Dickinson) put forty moulded rubber keys through holes in a matte black ABS case. Each key carries a letter and a BASIC keyword in white; the case around it carries a green extended-mode word above-left, a red symbol-shift character above-right, and a further white word beneath. Five legends per key, because the machine had one key for everything.

The tells kept here: the "dead flesh" grey — matte, dull, faintly warm, with a grey rim light along the rounded top edge and almost no specular highlight; the key as a rounded slab with a domed face sitting in a hole in the case with a black gap all round and no bezel; the five legend zones in a wide grotesk; the wider SYMBOL SHIFT key that carries only a word; and the rainbow stripe standing at the bottom right, which is the tell from across the room. There is no travel to speak of: pressed, the key compresses one pixel and the rubber flattens (40 ms in, 90 ms out — rubber creeps back). activate fires on press, as the membrane underneath does.

Left out: the whole forty-key mat and the membrane ribbon; the moulded ridge between key rows; the case's front lip and the ZX Spectrum badge. The stripe is stage decoration, not part of the element.
