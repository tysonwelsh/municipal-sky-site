THE SOURCE

The unidirectional dive bezel of the 1953 Submariner and its 1962 5513 successor (and the Fifty Fathoms and the Seiko 6309 that copied the idea): a steel ring with a coin-edge knurl you can grip with a wet glove, carrying a black anodised aluminium insert printed with 10 20 30 40 50, a bar every five minutes, a hash every single minute for the first quarter hour, and an inverted triangle at 12 with a lume dot inside. You set the triangle to the minute hand at the start of a dive and read elapsed time off the insert. The ratchet only lets it turn anticlockwise, so a knock can only ever shorten the dive.

The tells kept here: the triangle at 12 with the lume pip that glows faintly against the dark panel; the marker density that changes at 15; a coin-edge of 120 teeth whose two facets are each lit from above-left, so the ring reads bright at the top-left and dark at the bottom-right with a little reflected light at the far edge; the insert sitting below the steel lip with an inner shadow at its top-left; a flat crystal over an empty dial with one wide window-shaped reflection. The value is the minute reading at 12 o'clock: each anticlockwise click adds one (or a half with clicks="120") and it wraps at 60. Pull it clockwise and it twitches a degree and settles back; bidirectional frees it. Drag follows the pointer's angle around the centre; the wheel and the arrows click; Home turns it round to the triangle at 12. The aged palette turns the insert grey-blue and the pip cream.

Left out: the case and lugs around the bezel, the hands and the dial printing (an empty dial keeps the bezel the subject), the lume ageing into cracks.
