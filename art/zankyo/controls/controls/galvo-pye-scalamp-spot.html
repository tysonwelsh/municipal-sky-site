THE SOURCE

W. G. Pye & Co. of Cambridge built the Scalamp for school and works laboratories: a grey hammertone box with the galvanometer, its lamp, its optics and its scale all inside one case. A mirror on the suspension throws a slit of light up onto a milky ground-glass rule across the front, so the reading is a bright spot with a black hairline down its middle sliding along a centre-zero ruler — no needle, no pivot, no friction.

The tells kept here: the horizontal, linear, centre-zero scale with figures every five that repeat on both sides of nought and a fine tick at every millimetre; the spot as a soft bright blob that is brightest in its middle and fades at the ends, blooming on the glass, with one crisp hairline; the milky glass glowing faintly all along from the same lamp, and the darker bands where the metal frame overlaps it; hammertone paint with real dimples and a brighter rounded edge; the sensitivity knob with its printed skirt ×1 ×0.1 ×0.01 CLAMP, the engraved and black-filled nameplate, and two brass terminals. Because the coil hangs on a suspension, the spot overshoots and swings back two or three times before it settles, and it never sits quite still.

Left out: the lamp switch and mains lead, the levelling feet, the spirit level, and the clamp that the CLAMP position of the knob actually operates — here the knob only labels itself.
