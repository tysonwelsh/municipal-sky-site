THE SOURCE

On an F-4's armament panel and on the A-10's, the MASTER ARM switch is an ordinary MS chrome bat toggle wearing a red flip guard: a moulded U-channel hinged on a steel pin at its base, sprung so it falls shut, with a rectangular hole in its front so the crew can read the switch's position without lifting it. The guard is not a lock. It is there so that nothing — a knee, a checklist, a glove — can arm the aircraft by accident, and so that dropping the cover disarms it: the lip sweeps the bat down on its way past.

The tells kept here: the cover really is a box — a front frame standing thirteen pixels off the panel on two side walls and a top wall, drawn in perspective so you see slivers of its insides through the window; it hinges at the bottom on a pin between two ears and flips 110 degrees, hanging below the switch with its darker inner face and moulded rib toward you; the bat is hidden behind it when shut, with only the ball peeking below the lip; ARM above and SAFE below, engraved white on black, with the function name on a screwed-on grey plate; a chip on the guard's lower corner showing pale plastic under the red. Closing the cover over an armed switch drives the bat down at sixty degrees of travel and then finishes shutting; trying to arm it while the cover is down only makes the cover twitch.

Left out: the torsion spring's coils behind the hinge (the guard here stays where you put it rather than falling shut), the safety wire, and the neighbouring GUN/MSL/BOMB selector that gives the switch its context on the real panel.
