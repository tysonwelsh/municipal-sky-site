THE SOURCE

A Korry 318 is the square switch-light that covers the Airbus overhead panel and the Boeing MIP: a black anodised frame, a laser-engraved light plate split into two independently lit legends — FAULT over ON, AVAIL over OFF — and incandescent lamps behind each half. It is sunlight readable, which is the whole point of the design: unlit, the plate is as black as the frame and the words are simply not there.

The tells kept here: the dead face — nothing of the legend shows until a lamp is behind it; the hairline that splits the lens into two halves lit in their own colours; the lens sitting proud of a frame with a 45° chamfer that catches the light on its top-left; the lit half glowing faintly as a whole, not just at the letters, with a blurred copy under the crisp legend and four faint lamp hot-spots across it; a cold reflection of the room across the top third of the plate that ignores the shape of anything under it; and a press of the lens alone onto a hard stop — the frame never moves. The lamps warm up over 120 ms and cool over 250 ms, so a half fades rather than switches.

Left out: the press-to-test and the dimming bus, the lamp-pair redundancy (a real Korry keeps half-brightness on a failed filament), and the dust in the frame's chamfer that every flight-deck photograph shows.
