THE SOURCE

The Daka-Ware pointer knob is the one on every tweed and blackface Fender, on Hammond chassis, on Ampex and RCA broadcast gear: a moulded phenolic body with a flat-topped wedge that reads as a pointer from across the room, a brass set-screw into the shaft's flat, and the scale printed on the panel around it, 1 to 10 over 300 degrees.

The tells kept here: the wedge's flat top and rounded tip; the slight dome of the body catching one highlight; a set-screw on the skirt at 4 o'clock; the panel's silk-screened numerals in a Futura-like face with heavier majors at 1, 5 and 10; a ring of finger grime that is off-centre because hands come from below-right; a soft contact shadow under the skirt so it sits on the panel rather than floating. Drag is vertical (up is clockwise), the wheel steps, arrows step and Shift-arrows step by ten.

Left out: the shaft's D-flat visible in a rear view, the mould seam.
