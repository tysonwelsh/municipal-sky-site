THE SOURCE

One inch of oiled buff paper, eight data tracks on a tenth-inch pitch with a row of smaller feed holes between tracks 3 and 4 — the offset row is the single detail that makes a strip read as real tape rather than a dotted ribbon. An ASR-33 punched it at ten characters a second: seven bits of ASCII in tracks 1–7, even parity in track 8, so H (0x48) punches tracks 4 and 7 and a rubout punches all eight. The five-level Baudot tape beside it is narrower, its feed row between tracks 2 and 3, and it opens with the LTRS shift — all five holes.

The tells kept here: real bit patterns, checked against the parity rule; the leader of blank tape with a marker-pen label and a run of rubouts before the data; holes that show the dark behind them with a paper-fibre fringe and a faint shadow on the translucent tape; the fanfold crease; two chads lying loose where they fell; the tape drifting through a reader window whose star-wheel turns one tooth per character; a 1 px lateral wobble as it feeds; the fibrous, slightly wavy cut edges.

Left out: the reader's photocell lamp and its glow through the holes, the spooling reels, and the pink and black-Mylar stocks (the colour attribute carries them; the demo shows buff and yellow).
