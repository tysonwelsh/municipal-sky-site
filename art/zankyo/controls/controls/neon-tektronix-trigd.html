THE SOURCE

On a Tektronix 453, a 454 or any of the 500-series scopes, the trigger section has one or two small rectangular windows beside the knobs. Behind each is a bare NE-2 neon lamp a few millimetres back, and in front a flush milky-white plastic diffuser let into the blue-grey painted aluminium. The legend — TRIG'D, UNCAL — is screened underneath in small black caps.

The tells kept here: the window is a flush rectangle with barely-rounded corners and a faint dark rim where the diffuser meets the paint, sitting a hair proud with a lit lip along the bottom-right. Lit, the two electrodes show through the diffuser as a pair of bright parallel bars, a little left of centre, with the orange-red concentrated there and going paler and weaker toward the window's edges — never an even fill. It strikes instantly: no incandescent warm-up and no fade on the way out, only a 3 % ripple at mains rate while it burns, and a soft orange spill on the paint around the window. Dark, the window is dull warm off-white plastic, not black — you can still tell it is a window, and the bulb shows as a faint shadow behind it. The third one carries flicker: on the edge of triggering the neon stutters in random bursts instead of glowing steadily, which is exactly what a marginal trigger looks like.

Left out: the lamp's wire leads and the phenolic socket behind the panel, the neighbouring LEVEL and SLOPE knobs, and the silk-screened section rule that boxes the trigger controls on the real panel.
