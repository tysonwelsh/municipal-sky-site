THE SOURCE

The Tektronix 465 is the portable 100 MHz scope that sat on every bench of the 1970s. Its tube has an internal graticule — the ruling is etched on the inside of the faceplate, in the same plane as the phosphor — so the trace lies exactly on the lines with no parallax, and a green filter in front turns the screen's black into a dark green.

The tells kept here: the graticule is dim etched grey-green, never white, and its centre lines carry hash marks every 0.2 division, with dotted 0 % and 100 % rise-time lines across the face and 10 % / 90 % marks at the left; P31 green — where the beam moves fast the trace is thin and dim, where it dwells on a flat top it is fat and bright, with a white-green core inside a soft bloom that widens with intensity; a short persistence, so a changing signal drags a fading tail behind it and a still one sits rock steady; the retrace is blanked, and a single sample parks the beam as one bright dot; the corners fall off darker behind the filter; the beam writes its own channel numbers at the left of each trace; and illum lights the graticule from the edge the way the 465's GRATICULE ILLUM control does.

Left out: the whole front panel around the bezel — the 465's cream and blue-grey deck, its collet knobs and its camera-mount lugs are other controls; there is no sweep animation, because at the default 2 ms/div the whole width is written in 20 ms, faster than a frame; and the waveform array is not reflected back to the value attribute, since it changes every frame.
