THE SOURCE

The Bourns 3006P is the multiturn trimmer everyone has set with a jeweller's screwdriver: a 19 mm cobalt-blue moulded body, a brass leadscrew in a boss at one end, three tinned pins beneath, and a slip clutch at each end of travel so the screw keeps turning when the wiper has nowhere left to go. Twenty-five turns take the wiper from one end to the other, so a whole turn moves the value only four per cent.

The tells kept here: the blue that reads from across a bench, slightly glossy with a moulded speckle and a parting line; the brass slotted screw, the only moving part, in its boss on the end face, drawn in the same oblique projection as the body so the slot turns in perspective; white monospaced type and value code on the top; three pins in a row under the body, the middle one the wiper, each with a tinned fillet on its pad; the body's shadow falling down-right onto the board and the RV1 silkscreen beside it. Drag or wheel turns the screw (4° per px, Shift for ten turns), arrows step 0.1 %, Shift-arrows 1 %, Home/End go to the ends; with the screwdriver attribute a blade tip sits in the slot while you drag. At either end the clutch slips: the screw turns and the value stays.

Left out: the Bourns wordmark, the leadscrew's O-ring seal, the date code.
