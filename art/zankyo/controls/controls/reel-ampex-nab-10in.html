THE SOURCE

The 10½-inch precision aluminium reel on an Ampex 350, AG-440 or ATR-100: a machined flange with three wide spokes and three big windows, a brushed rim, and a black NAB hub adapter whose three-lobed knurled knob you thumb tight over the centre screw. Through the windows you watch the amber-brown pack of oxide grow on one side and shrink on the other, and you can tell from across the control room how much tape is left.

The tells kept here: the pack showing as a ring of oxide through the three windows, with its fine concentric grain, a dark edge where it sits down on the hub and a bright edge ring at its rim — and it grows and shrinks with value; the three-lobed hub knob and its centre screw turning with the reel; the brushed highlight that does not turn — it is a reflection of the room, so it stays on the upper left while the metal spins under it; the rate that rises as the pack shrinks (rate ∝ 1/radius, the reason a supply reel speeds up as it empties); the spokes blurring into a grey ring above about two turns a second, and a hard shadow thrown down and right onto the deck plate.

Left out: the tape's run to the heads, the reel-retaining knob's spring, and the maker's name — the engraved wordmark on the rim is a generic one. The direction attribute is spelled role as the style entry asks, so the element's accessible image role and label are carried on its inner wrapper rather than on the host.
