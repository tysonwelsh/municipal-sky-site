THE SOURCE

Sony's TC-K71 and its TC-K81 sibling replaced the deck's moving-coil VUs with a pair of horizontal LED peak programme meters: twelve rectangular LEDs per channel behind a smoked acrylic window, green up to −1, amber from 0 to +3, red at +5 and +8, with the dB figures silk-screened in white on the anodised panel below each row. Sony called the trick double indication — the bar follows the programme while a single LED hangs at the last peak and drops a moment later.

The tells kept here: the uneven scale (coarse at the bottom, −30, −20, −10, then one step per dB near zero) with the colour break sitting just under 0; unlit LEDs as dark green, amber and red rectangles, never black, so the whole ladder is visible at silence; the smoked window with one shallow reflection across its top-left corner and a bright lip along its lower edge; white index ticks between the window and the numbers. PPM ballistics: the bar rises in about 10 ms and falls 20 dB in 700 ms, and the peak dot holds hold ms (1500 by default) before it drops.

Left out: the rest of the fascia — the transport keys, the Dolby switch and the cassette door that would sit around it — and the deck's MPX filter lamp.
