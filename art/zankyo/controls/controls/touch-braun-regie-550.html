THE SOURCE

The Braun Regie 550 receiver of 1977 — Dieter Rams and Peter Hartwein — put the source selector on a row of flush brushed-aluminium squares that do not move at all. A finger on the metal is enough; the only answer is a 2 mm red LED next to the plate. The legends are set small, lower-case, in Akzidenz-Grotesk, left-aligned to the plate below them, on a 22 mm grid that never varies across the whole front.

The tells kept here: no travel and no shadow crease — the plate is flush in the panel, separated from it only by a hairline black gap and by having a different texture (horizontal brushed grain against a matte panel); the LED is instant, tiny, a hot core in a bloom, and it casts a faint red wash on the panel around it; touching a plate lifts its brightness about 3 % for a tenth of a second — the light a fingertip returns — and then it is over; the legends sit above their plates in a light grotesk on a strict grid; there is one greasy fingerprint on tuner, the plate this owner uses. The silver-panel variant and the single-plate form are both in the demo.

Left out: the actual capacitance — a real plate answers a finger 2 mm away, this one wants a click or a key; the engraved BRAUN wordmark; the seam where the extruded panel meets the wooden case.
