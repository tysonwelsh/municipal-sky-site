THE SOURCE

The Ekco AD-65 and its round-cased siblings were moulded in one shot from brown phenolic — Wells Coates's circular case is the best-known, but the material itself, not the shape, is the point here: a deep, faintly translucent brown that looks like polished stone rather than paint, poured into a two-part mould that always leaves its seam somewhere on the finished piece.

The tells kept here: the body built in layers — a dark base, a lighter flow-marbling that follows the material the way it moved in the mould, then one wide soft gloss and a single hard glint, so it reads as glossy depth rather than a flat fill; a faint mould-seam ridge crossing the swatch, lit on one side and dark on the other, with a tiny bright flash where it reaches the edge; the edge itself running warmer and lighter — a hint of amber translucence where the material is thinnest; and a legend that is moulded, not printed — raised in the same brown, read only by a light top-left edge and a dark bottom-right one. color swaps in Ekco's black phenolic or a mottled walnut run; arc gives the swatch the convex curve of a cut from the round case; wear adds a network of fine gloss scratches and a rubbed pale patch where a hand habitually rested.

Left out: the case's full disc form and control cut-outs (this is a flat material segment), and the AD-65's chrome escutcheon ring, which belongs to a different swatch.
