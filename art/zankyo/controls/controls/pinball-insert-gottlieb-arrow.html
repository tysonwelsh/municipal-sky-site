THE SOURCE

A Gottlieb wedgehead playfield is birch plywood, screen-printed in flat colours and lacquered, with holes routed through it for the inserts: little slabs of milky translucent plastic, printed on top in red, dropped in flush and lit from underneath by a #44 bayonet lamp in a socket. The arrow says SPECIAL; the words WHEN LIT are printed on the wood beside it, because the arrow only means anything when the lamp is on.

The tells kept here: the insert is a different material from the playfield — a hard edge with the dark routed wood seam around it, and a thin shadow inside the top-left of the hole from the plywood's thickness. Unlit it is dull ivory with a red word on it; lit it goes yellow-white with a hot spot that is not in the middle (the lamp never is), the tip of the arrow stays dimmer, and the red print turns translucent orange while the light spills a little way onto the lacquer around the hole. The printed art — a red keyline that follows the insert and stops, blue border rules — carries a fine screen texture, and the ball has worn a curved swirl through the lacquer to bare wood on the way past. The lamp is incandescent: ~120 ms up, ~250 ms down.

Left out: the lamp socket and wiring under the board, the insert's chamfered underside, the cigarette burn and the screw holes of a real forty-year-old playfield. shape gives the round and rectangular inserts from the same game; flash is the 4 Hz attract-mode blink.
