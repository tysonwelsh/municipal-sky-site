THE SOURCE

The Lucas starter push is the black button on the dash of a pre-war Austin or Riley, and on the bulkhead of every Lister stationary engine: a 20 mm phenolic dome with the word START moulded into its crown, standing proud of a 32 mm chrome escutcheon that is let into the panel and held by a knurled lock ring. You press it with a thumb, the solenoid clacks, and it springs back.

The tells kept here: the dome as a true cap — highlight at ten o'clock, a broad warm mid-tone, a deep core shadow at four o'clock and a thin band of reflected light along the underside rim; the legend moulded in the same material, catching light only on its top-left edges; the escutcheon as a shallow chrome cone with a hard horizon, sky above and ground below; forty-eight knurl facets on the lock ring with every third one brighter; the dome standing proud, casting its own shadow into the escutcheon, and dropping onto a hard stop while the escutcheon never moves; a browner, glossier patch of thumb-polish on the crown; and a hairline of shadow where the escutcheon meets the wrinkle paint, so it reads as let in rather than stuck on.

Left out: the rear terminals and the fibre washer, the chrome's real environment map (the horizon here is a two-stop gradient, not a room), and the click of the contact, which is the one tell a screen cannot keep.
