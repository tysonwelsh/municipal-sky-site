THE SOURCE

The MS91528 is the standard-issue bar knob of American military and aerospace hardware: a black phenolic oblong with a square-shouldered, chamfered pointer nose and a rounded rear, sitting on a serrated round boss, fixed with a socket set-screw. It is the knob on the Apollo Command Module's main display console, on the Shuttle's flight deck, on radar consoles and shipboard radios, and it survives in the catalogues today. The legend is engraved through grey paint and filled white.

The tells kept here: the bar's flat, sharp-edged top with one thin highlight along its top-left edge, lit from a fixed above-left light however the bar turns (the shading gradients counter-rotate); the white-painted nose as a clean rectangle of paint with a chipped corner; the boss visible beneath the bar with its fine serration ring and the set-screw on the rounded-end side; the ring of engraved white legends in a Futura-like face, each with a short radial line; countersunk Phillips screws at the corners of the panel tile; a hard 30° detent snap. Clicking a legend jumps to it; drag, wheel and arrows step; Home/End go to the ends. mode="range" makes it a smooth 300° potentiometer with a 0–10 scale.

Left out: the boss's height (it is seen from straight above), the MS part-number moulded into the underside, and the panel's edge-lit plastic overlay used on later Shuttle panels.
