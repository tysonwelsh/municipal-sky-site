THE SOURCE

The gate-valve handwheel is the same casting on a Crane, a Nibco, a Kitz, a Soviet one: five spokes with a slight S in them so the shrinking casting does not crack, a rim of rounded section fat enough for a gloved hand, the word OPEN and a curved arrow cast proud on the rim, and in the hub a hex nut through which the stem rises as the gate lifts. Painted red for steam; green for water; black when nobody cared. Seen from above on the bonnet flange.

The tells kept here: the five S-curved spokes joined to a thick rim, lit by an SVG lighting filter so the rim reads as a highlight ring over a shadow ring and the whole casting has a rough, grainy skin; OPEN and the anticlockwise arrow as raised cast letters whose bevels stay lit from the top-left however the wheel turns; three chips of grey iron with a rust bloom where hands land on the rim; a hex nut in the hub with the bright stem boss growing and casting a longer shadow as the valve opens; a one-degree wobble while it spins. Drag follows the pointer's angle around the hub and the wheel coasts for about a second and a half; the wheel steps finely; arrows step one percent (Shift ten), Home shuts, End opens. Eight turns shut to open by default (turns).

Left out: the stem's thread, the packing gland below, the brass name tag on a chain.
