THE SOURCE

The Sargent & Greenleaf 6730 is the dial on most of the safes in most of the back offices of the world: a black spun-aluminium dial, 0 to 99 engraved white around a face that shows a faint circular brushing when the light catches it, a bright chamfer stepping down to a knurled edge for the thumb, sitting in a black ring bolted to the door. The ring carries two white marks: the opening index at 12, and, at 11, the changing index that only locksmiths use — the second mark is how you know it is a 6730.

The tells kept here: both indices, the opening one heavier; numerals every 5 with a tick every unit and a bolder 0, engraved as white fill over a hair of dark shadow and standing radially; the spun sheen as fine concentric rings that turn with the dial under a highlight that does not; the chamfer as a bright ring lit top-left and dark bottom-right; the knurl as ninety-six ridges whose light and dark faces are shaded by where they sit under the lamp, with a thumb-polished patch on the edge; a flat centre disc with a slotted spindle screw; a grime halo on the grey door around the ring. Drag follows the pointer's angle and the dial coasts for a moment; the wheel and arrows step one number (Shift ten); Home and End go to 0 and 99. With combination="25-50-75" the element counts the classic 4 left / 3 right / 2 left / 1 right and fires open; a wrong direction or an overshoot starts you over, as the lock would.

Left out: the spline key hole in the dial's edge, the ring's mounting screws under the dial, the lock's clicks.
