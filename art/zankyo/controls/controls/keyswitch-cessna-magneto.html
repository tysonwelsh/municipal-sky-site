THE SOURCE

The Bendix / ACS ignition switch on a Cessna 172 (and nearly every piston single since) is a chrome-bezelled rotary with a flat black face, five printed positions across its top — OFF, R, L, BOTH, START — and a plain brass key standing up out of the barrel. Turning past BOTH into the spring-loaded START position engages the starter; let go and it snaps back to BOTH, running on both magnetos. The key only pulls free at OFF.

The tells kept here: the five legends silk-screened in an arc across the black face with START swung down past BOTH toward 4 o'clock; the brass key's flat rectangular bow standing proud of the barrel, seen from slightly above, rotating with it through the full throw; the thin red index line on the barrel pointing at the live position; the key pulling free only at OFF, leaving a flared empty keyway; a scuff worn into the black face at START where thumbs land. The key follows the pointer's angle while dragged, clicks to a position, steps on the wheel and arrows, and springs back out of START on release or after a 400 ms Space/Enter pulse from BOTH.

Left out: the switch's rear terminal block and the separate P-lead ground wires, the exact Bendix part stamp on the barrel face.
