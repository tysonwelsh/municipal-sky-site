THE SOURCE

The Sperry SP-352 is a Panaplex: two sheets of glass frit-sealed a millimetre apart with neon between them, the anodes and the seven-segment cathodes printed flat on the inside faces. One panel does the whole readout, so a 1970s bench meter gets its digits from a single glass sandwich the size of a stick of gum rather than a row of tubes. Everything is in one plane and there is no depth to it at all.

The tells kept here: every segment is a pill — a bar with fully rounded ends, not a chamfered hexagon — and the pills of neighbouring segments almost touch at the corners. There is a low orange fog over each digit cell that brightens where segments are struck and lags about 80 ms behind them, while the segments themselves switch in 20 ms, and a two-percent shimmer at 12 Hz stands in for the multiplex flicker. The glass sandwich shows its thickness as a grey-blue line top and bottom inside a thick black frit border, leading zeros are blanked, the minus sign lights in its own cell, the decimal is a bright pill, and gold ribbon leads run out of the bottom edge. Under the default dark-red acrylic the glow goes orange-red and the unlit segments almost vanish; filter="none" shows the bare panel with its unlit cathodes at full strength.

Left out: the keep-alive cathode in the corner, the getter, and the wide-angle fall-off that makes a real Panaplex dim when you look at it from the side.
