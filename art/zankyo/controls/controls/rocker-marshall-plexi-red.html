THE SOURCE

Every plexi-panel Marshall from the JTM45 to the 1959 Super Lead carries the same pair of red Bulgin-type rockers at the right-hand end of the gold panel — POWER first, then the neon pilot in its chrome bezel, then STANDBY — silk-screened in black capitals on brushed gold anodised aluminium.

The tells kept here: the gold itself, brushed with a fine horizontal grain and crossed by one broad diagonal reflection, warm rather than yellow; the rocker as a real see-saw about a visible fulcrum line, the pressed half sunk flush into the bezel with the cavity dark behind it and the other half standing proud, its outer edge catching the light; the moulded ON and its arrow in the top half, painted white and half worn away; the translucent moulding, so the compressed half goes darker and the raised half glows red along its edge; the black bezel with a hair of bare aluminium round the cut-out; the neon pilot warming over 300 ms and cooling over 250 ms; and the scuff on the gold where a hand rests to reach across.

Left out: the Bulgin part number moulded into the side of the body, the mains voltage plate, and the sag of a forty-year-old panel that no longer sits flat in its cabinet.
