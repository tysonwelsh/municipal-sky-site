THE SOURCE

Gino Valle's Cifra 3 for Solari di Udine, 1966: a lying-down ABS cylinder with a rectangular window cut in its flat front face, two banks of black flaps behind it, and nothing else — no dial, no buttons, no branding on the front. It has been in production ever since and sits in MoMA's design collection.

The tells kept here: the body is shaded as a tube — a bright band along the top curve, a deep shadow underneath and a sliver of reflected light at the very bottom edge, with the flat front face marked off by the thin recess line that runs round it; the flat end discs at either side; the small moulded bar beside the window on the right where the setting wheel lives; the figures are large and geometric with a closed 4, cut across the middle by a one-pixel black split line with a lighter edge under it, and the two halves of a figure never line up perfectly — the lower half sits half a pixel over; each of the four positions is its own flap stack, so :09 → :10 is a two-flap cascade and a jump cycles the intermediate flaps through; and the fall is in two phases — the top half hinges down 130 ms from 0° to −90° in perspective, darkening as it turns away from the light and dragging a shadow across the card below, then the next figure's bottom half swings from +90° to 0° over 110 ms and bounces once on its stop.

Left out: the mains cord and the synchronous motor's hum, the setting wheel itself (only its moulded access bar is here), and the Solari wordmark.
