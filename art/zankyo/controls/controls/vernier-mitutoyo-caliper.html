THE SOURCE

The Mitutoyo 530-104 is the plain 150 mm vernier caliper that sat in a thousand toolroom drawers before dial and digital ones: a satin stainless beam engraved and black-filled in millimetres along its lower edge and in sixteenths of an inch along its top, a slider carrying a bevelled vernier plate of twenty divisions reading to 0.05 mm, outside jaws below, inside jaws above, a thumb roller, a lock screw, and a depth rod that comes out of the far end of the beam by exactly as much as the jaws are open.

The tells kept here: the vernier is real. Its twenty divisions span thirty-nine millimetres, so each is 1.95 mm against the main scale's 2 mm, and exactly one line of it coincides with a main-scale line — at 37.45 mm it is the ninth, as it must be. The satin finish is a broad soft highlight along the beam with a fine horizontal grain and no hard chrome horizon; the engraving is black-filled with a longer line every five and a numeral every ten; the inch scale runs in sixteenths with its own eight-division vernier on the slider's top bar; the thumb roller turns as the slider travels; the lock screw sits a little askew when it is thrown; there is a hairline of oil where the slider rides the beam and a dot of polish on each jaw tip.

Left out: the 1/128 inch graduations, which at this scale would be a fifth of a pixel — the inch scale is drawn in sixteenths and the fractional reading is left to its vernier. The jaws are foreshortened: a real 150 mm caliper's outside jaws are 40 mm deep, which would double the height of the drawing. The depth rod runs off the right edge of the frame at large openings, as the real one runs off the end of a photograph.
