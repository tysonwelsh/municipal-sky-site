THE SOURCE

The Sony CDP-101 of 1982 was the first Compact Disc player sold to anyone, and its readout set the house style for a decade of Japanese hi-fi: a cyan-green vacuum fluorescent display sitting well behind a sheet of smoked acrylic, on a black front with a chrome hairline and a brushed silver skirt. Nothing on it has a hard edge — the phosphor is diffuse, the smoke softens it further, and the whole window glows faintly around the numerals.

The tells kept here: the phosphor is minty and cool, not green, brighter down the centre of each segment, with a bloom that spills onto the smoke; the anode grid is drawn as a fine 3 px mesh over the lit segments only, which is what makes a VFD look like a VFD and not an LED; every unlit segment is still faintly there, a ghost at about a tenth of the lit value; the legends light as words — TRACK, MIN and SEC stay on, PLAY ▶ and PAUSE ‖ and REPEAT come and go, and the ones that are off are just visible; a small eight-bar level meter at the right whose top bar is amber, the period's way of marking peak; the window has a dark moulded bezel edge and one diagonal reflection band that is not the shape of anything behind it. Segments fade up in about 60 ms and down in 90, so the seconds do not snap; a change of track blanks the number for 100 ms before it relights, exactly as the real transport does when it re-reads the subcode.

Left out: the CDP-101's fifteen-step music calendar and its AMS legends, the wired remote socket, and the Sony wordmark (a plain chrome line stands in for it).
