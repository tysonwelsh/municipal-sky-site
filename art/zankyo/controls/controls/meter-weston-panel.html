THE SOURCE

The Weston Model 301 and its Simpson and Triplett cousins are the square black meters on every 1940s radio bench, power supply and transmitter: a moulded Bakelite bezel with four slotted screws, a cream paper scale under glass, a mirror strip along the arc so you can put the needle over its own reflection and read it without parallax, a black knife-edge needle, and a slotted zero-adjust screw at the bottom of the bezel.

The tells kept here: the bezel's soft-cornered moulding with a bevel that catches the top-left light; the scale's yellowing, darker toward the corners; the mirror strip as a band that reflects a little of the needle; the needle's taper and its pivot cap; the glass as one broad reflection that is not the shape of anything under it; the screws with slots at different angles. The needle has mass: it approaches the value with a damped spring and overshoots a touch, and it stops moving when the element leaves the page.

Left out: the rear terminals, the "Weston" script logo (replaced by a plain word to avoid a trademark).
