THE SOURCE

The Vox V846 (built for Thomas Organ in 1967 and copied ever since, most famously by the Dunlop Cry Baby) is a rocking treadle in a black die-cast housing: a ribbed black rubber tread, a chrome nameplate riveted to the treadle's front slope, a rack-and-pinion under the skirt turning the pot as the pedal rocks, and a hard mechanical stop at each end of its travel. Heel down backs the wah off; toe down is fully forward.

The tells kept here: the treadle rocking about a hinge near the heel, so the toe end travels through a real arc and closes toward the base as it goes down; the rubber tread's lateral ribs, each with a highlight on its crown and a shadow in its trough; the die-cast housing's flat, speckled, gloss-free finish with a crisp edge light from the top-left; the chrome plate catching a horizon reflection; a firm push past the toe stop (or Space) clacking the bypass switch, with a small red LED on the led variant. Drag is vertical — pull up for heel, push down for toe — and the tread attribute swaps the plate for the Cry Baby's white script.

Left out: the return spring (real wah treadles have none — they stay where you leave them), the underside rack-and-pinion, the side-mounted input/output jacks.
