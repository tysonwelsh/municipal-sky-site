TRANSPORT & VOLUME — options

Five treatments for PLAY/STOP and five for the master volume, each sitting on the real receive-panel metal so it is judged against the surface it would live on. Everything is clickable and draggable — press the buttons, drag the faders, spin the wheel. Nothing is wired to the engine; this is a look decision only.

NOW — what is on the machine today

CURRENTArcade domes + knurled knob

The glossy candy dome and the pure saturated red are the tell — they read as a game cabinet, an object designed to be attractive, while everything around them now reads as equipment built to be serviced. The knob is closer in spirit but its knurl is regular and clean where the rest of the panel is worn.

押釦 BUTTONS — five treatments

B1Anti-vandal, machined stainless

The real 19 mm vandal-resistant switch: a turned stainless collar, a flush black face, and the symbol etched through the face so the lamp behind it shows as a ring of light rather than a coloured plastic cap. Nothing is glossy. This is the most literal match for the rest of the machine — it is the same family of part as the bolts.

B2Illuminated legend, broadcast-desk

The rectangular lamp button off a studio desk: a translucent cap that is dim and grey until it is live, then lights from inside with the legend floating in it. Brings Japanese type onto the control surface, which the machine otherwise only does on the screens. The largest, most legible target of the five.

B3Tape-deck keys, latching

Mechanical piano keys out of a cassette deck, in their own recessed bed. The played key stays down until STOP releases it, so the machine's state is carried by a physical position rather than by a glow — the most tactile option, and the only one that reads correctly with the lamp off. Tallest of the five; it wants the panel's full height.

Keeps the round shape you already have but remakes it as moulded phenolic: matte, a visible parting line round the equator, paint gone off the high side, a dulled oxblood instead of the neon red. The buttons stop glowing entirely — a separate 運転 lamp beside them carries the running state, which is how a machine of this vintage would actually do it. The smallest change from today's silhouette.

B5Behind a punched guard plate
操作

The buttons sit below a brushed guard plate, reachable only through punched holes — the pads never rise above the panel, so a sleeve cannot catch them. The plate is its own piece of hardware with its own lit cut edge, which adds a layer of depth the flat panel currently lacks. The most severe and industrial of the five, and the one that reads least like a "button" at a glance.
