OPTION A · 磁石 THE MAGNET SET interface-first

Argument. Every other control on this faceplate is a picture of a control. The magnet is not: it is an object lying on the cabinet, and the only way to use it is to pick it up and put it somewhere. That is the whole argument. ZANKYŌ makes its music by itself — the owner's real relationship to it is fiddling, not operating, and a magnet is the purest fiddle there is: you drag it onto the picture, the raster bends into its poles, you leave it there for an hour and the night looks different. It is also the one Paik reference that is a mechanism rather than a style: Magnet TV (1965) did not filter the signal, it put a lump of steel on the bell of the tube and let physics ruin it. So the warp here is in the pixels — a per-pixel displacement summed from two opposite poles, radial (the swell) plus tangential (the deflection), with a smear along the scan and a bloom where the field is strongest — and the scanlines are drawn rather than laid over in CSS, so they curve into the poles instead of staying flat while the picture moves underneath them. Around it, the casing is the most ordinary object in the four options on purpose: a 1970s moulded-ABS portable, parting line down both walls, louvres, a carry bail folded flat, four rubber feet. A television you could have carried into the yard under one arm — which is the point of putting a magnet on it.

The bay, and what the controls do. The chin is gone; under the tube is a moulded shelf angled 12° toward the viewer, drawn entirely in light and shadow — the key slot is recessed because it is dark at the top and lit at the bottom, the rotary's boss is the other way round, and there is no border anywhere on it. On it: four piano keys (走査 SCAN · 遅延 DELAY · 反転 REVERSE latch as a bank, one down at a time, with a red band on the front edge of the latched one; 消磁 DEGAUSS is momentary and springs back), a 44 px rotary with a fluted grip, a brass insert and a pointer that casts its shadow on the engraved plate — twelve detents over 270°, and it pushes: a press tunes the next signal — and a slide fader with 70 px of travel in a moulded slot, a ridged thumb pad and its scale silk-screened beside it. What they drive is deliberately open (PLAN §5 A): the labels are plausible, the feel is the thing being judged. The one exception is 消磁 DEGAUSS, which does the only honest thing a degauss key can do — press it and it shakes the field out of the tube for about a second. Every control is keyboard-reachable: arrows turn the rotary and move the fader and the magnet (shift for a bigger step, Home parks it), Enter on the rotary receives.

Phosphor. P39 — long-persistence yellow-green, the live tube's own ramp, unchanged (black → #061a0c → #1a8a3a → #7dea9a → pale mint at white). Persistence is real: the previous frame decays under the new one at 0.62, so movement smears and a dropout leaves an afterimage on the glass. It is kept because the field effects read as light — the swell and the bloom need a phosphor with a long tail to look like a beam being pulled, not like a lens. The crack survives, and one piece of the yard's tape with it: the picture still arrives across four slipped shards.

Raster and mobile. 192 × 144, the reels' native size — four times the pixels the live shader renders, at no download cost (PLAN §1). The source-y and source-height arguments in the tear-band routine were b * 8 (a hardcoded 72 / 9); they are now b * (SH / 9), which was the only other place the old raster was baked in. At ≤ 700 px the two column wrappers dissolve with display: contents and the four things take an explicit order: television → spectrum scope → 段階 progress bar → PLAY / STOP / volume. The tube stays full width. The cabinet loses its bail and its louvres (there is no room for a handle you cannot reach), the bay wraps to two rows — keys and rotary above, fader, speaker and lamp below — and the magnet shrinks, snaps inside the cabinet edge and stays draggable with a finger.

Two deviations, declared. (1) The brief asks for the speaker grille to run up the right edge of the cabinet. At the settled 516 px column with a 488 px tube there are 14 px of wall on each side, so a side grille is arithmetically impossible — it would cost about 36 px of tube width. The walls carry louvres at the width they have (honest: the set gets hot), and the grille proper — fine horizontal slots, moulded straight into the shelf — sits at the right end of the bay, where a portable's speaker usually was anyway. If the owner wants the grille beside the glass, that is a decision to spend tube width on, and it should be made deliberately. (2) The idle no longer uses the CSS scanline overlay on this tube; the scanlines are drawn in the shader so they can bend. That costs about 5,400 field evaluations a frame (122 lines × 45 points) and is included in the number above; the picture itself costs another 27,648.

Idle. The tube is dark but never still: a faint raster crawls with a slow vignette and thermal sparks, a horizontal line of light crosses it on the station's own clock, the MSHI test card surfaces from the depth about every twenty seconds with the hold slipping, every so often the whole raster folds into Paik's one line and goes out or shrinks to a dot — and around the magnet's poles a bloom breathes on a 1.5 s period, as if the field were alive when nothing else is. A signal arrives on its own about every 25 seconds; the rotary's press brings one now.
