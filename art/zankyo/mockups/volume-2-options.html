VOLUME THUMBWHEEL — second set

Five wheels, all thicker than the ported roller (16–22 px against its 12), each on the real receive-panel metal. Drag any of them. Every one carries a little indicator above it showing the level.

The difference from the roller you have now is not the styling — it is the geometry. Every rib, flute and numeral below is placed by angle and projected onto the flat (x = R·sin θ, squashed by cos θ, faded as it turns away), so the marks crowd toward the edges the way a real cylinder's do. The ported roller scrolls a flat repeating gradient, evenly spaced right across its width — which is exactly why it reads as a strip sliding sideways rather than a wheel turning. That spacing is the whole tell.

送り THE WHEELS — drag to turn

W1Machined knurl, 18 px

A straight knurl cut into bar stock, lit hard from above. The ribs are 2° apart on the drum, so near the centre they are ~3 px apart and at the edges they compress into a dense band — the single most convincing cue that this is a cylinder. Brightest and most "machined" of the five; the closest relative to the bolts and the key bed.

W2Printed number band, 22 px

The numbered one. 0–100 in tens printed right around the wheel's circumference, 30° apart, with minor ticks every 2 between them — so the print runs off round the back and comes up the other side, and only three or four numbers are ever in view. The indicator above is a fixed pointer here rather than a travelling pip, because on this wheel it is the numbers that move. Thickest of the five; the print needs the room.

W3Moulded rubber roller, 16 px

The mouse-wheel: soft black rubber over a hub, with fat moulded treads 5° apart and a broad matte sheen instead of a specular line — rubber does not glint. Quietest of the five and the most obviously grippable; it reads as the one part of the machine you are actually meant to touch all the time. Thinnest here, and the one that asks least for attention.

W4Fluted lighter wheel, 18 px

The spark wheel off a petrol lighter: deep-cut flutes in a yellowed brass, each one carrying a hard bright edge where it catches the light. The only warm-metal part on the machine, which makes it read as a component sourced from somewhere else and fitted — very much this piece's story. Most characterful, and the least neutral.

W5Faceted drum, 20 px

Turned from hex bar rather than round: 40 flat facets, each taking its own flat tone by the angle it presents to the light, with a hard parting line between. Because a facet is flat there is no gradient across it — that hard step from one tone to the next is what separates a polygon from a cylinder, and it gives the wheel a faint ratcheting look as it turns. Most industrial; reads as a part rather than a texture.
