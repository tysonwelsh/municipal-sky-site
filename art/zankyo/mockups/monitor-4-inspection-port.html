点検窓MOCKUP 4 · THE INSPECTION PORT

Argument. Not a monitor: a maintenance port that happens to receive. The signal is a visitation of five to fifteen seconds a few times an hour, so the hardware that shows it should be the smallest thing on the panel and the most honest about what it is — a round scope behind a hatch some technician left open, watched through a porthole like the little set in TV Buddha. Idle it is a working instrument (a sweep, a dot, a graticule), not a dead screen waiting for content; playing, the picture is painted in clockwise by the sweep and, on loss, erased by it, torn, folded to a line, snowed and killed. The scope, the bargraph and the console are untouched. Small is the point: at 390 px it is still a 72 px tube you lean in to.

Phosphor: P7. The radar cascade phosphor — a blue-white flash directly under the beam that decays into a long yellow-green persistence. It is the historically correct phosphor for a PPI tube and it motivates the sweep physically: the picture is freshest just behind the line and ages warmer around the dial. It reads as a cousin of the machine's cyan rather than the default P39 green, so the port is visibly a different decade of hardware.

Placement: the control rail, right of the pitch module (tried both; the plate-row version is a toggle below). In the rail the port lives in the same viewport as the CRT, the bargraph, the play button and the VFD line that names the signal; the 受信 lamp sits a hand's width from the 電源 LED and reads as part of the same wiring. In the plate row it is a beautiful detail on the chassis skirt but it is below 230 px of VFD, off-screen on a phone while the signal plays, and it makes the serial plate share its moment.

Mobile. Under 700 px the rail wraps as it does today (transport and master on the first row); the module drops to the second row and the port stays beside it, at full size, exactly where the plan puts it. Under 380 px the plate shrinks to 90 px. Nothing stacks below the fold, nothing scrolls.

What I'd change in the plan. A hatch that "sits open" past ninety degrees shows its inside, not its stencil — so the stencil and the hazard stripe live on the inner face here (the way high-voltage warnings really do) and the 点検窓 stamp is engraved under the frame. §2.5's tune-in (0.4 s of snow) and the radar paint are sequential, not the same beat: snow, then one revolution of paint (~1.9 s) before the hold clock starts. The sweep keeps refreshing the picture during the hold — a live raster wiped clockwise is more Paik than a still.
