SET B · 修理台 THE SERVICE BENCH

Argument. Every other option has to invent a cabinet and then justify it. This one takes the cabinet off, which is the only move on the table that adds nothing: the faceplate already has 機動注意 silk-screened on the console and its guts on show, and Paik's proposition was never that a television is a nice object — it was that a television is raw material. So the bezel goes, and what is left is the thing a bezel exists to hide: a bare CRT clamped to two steel posts over an open chassis, the funnel falling away from the worklight into the dark, the deflection yoke in copper on the neck with its harness cable-tied twice, the neck board on its socket, the anode cap in a fat rubber boot, a chassis pan with a rust bloom where it stood on a wet floor. The picture is framed by nothing but the tube's own rolled glass edge, its steel implosion band, and two clamp straps. Every moulding here is one a real tube has. And because you do not operate a set on a bench — you service it — the control area stops needing an excuse: a service rail is supposed to carry eleven things, so it carries eleven, at component scale, silk-screened, on olive phenolic bolted through into the chassis.

What the eleven do is deliberately open (this is the interface-first half of the study). They are named in the machine's own register — 焦点 · 直線 · 幅 · 回路選択 · 高圧 · 遮断 · 試験 · 測定点 — and they all move for real: the three trimmers take a screwdriver cursor and step in 24 detents (drag, scroll or arrow keys); the wafer switch has six detents with a mechanical overshoot and an audible tick, and its position is silk-screened on the board; the two guarded toggles are a genuine two-step — the red guard must be flipped up before the bat handle is even reachable (it is disabled while the guard is down, so the keyboard cannot cheat either), and the guard falls back on its spring after eight seconds; push-to-test is momentary, and its bulb has a filament that takes 160 ms to come up and 420 ms to die; the four test points take a probe clip, and the scope's trace genuinely jumps — offset, gain and a sync spike — for as long as the probe is on. Which variable each one eventually drives is the owner's call, not the mockup's.

Phosphor. P39, long-persistence yellow-green, the same LUT the live set runs (black → #061a0c → #1a8a3a → #7dea9a → pale mint at white), and the persistence is real: each frame decays under the next, so motion smears and a dropout leaves an afterimage on the glass. No change is proposed — a bench tube would be whatever tube is on the bench, and the scope beside it keeps its neon so the two read as different decades. The crack survives, and it earns more here than it did in a cabinet: a cracked tube is exactly what would be out of its case on a service bench, with a piece of yellowed tape still on it.

Raster 192 × 144, the reels' native size — four times the pixels the live shader draws, for no new bytes. SW, SH go to 192, 144 and the tear-band routine's baked-in b * 8 becomes b * (SH / 9) for both the source-y and the source-height. Those are the only two places the old raster was hardcoded in the shader; the test card is drawn through a scale(SW/96, SH/72) so its 96 × 72 coordinates still land.

Measured frame cost, this browser, this viewport, whole shader chain (source → P39 LUT → persistence/bloom/tear/roll/ghost → shard compositing): measuring… The first 30 frames after any resize are discarded as warm-up; the scope, the bargraph and the rest of the faceplate are outside the number. For reference, on a 2× display in headless Chrome it measures ≈ 6.9 ms mean / 10.9 ms worst at 960 (tube 488 × 366), ≈ 11.0 / 13.7 ms at 700 (tube 634 × 476, where the set is full width) and ≈ 3.0 / 3.5 ms at 390 (tube 326 × 245). The per-pixel P39 pass is fixed by the raster — 27,648 pixels either way — so what actually moves the number is the tube’s size on screen, where the bloom blur, the tear bands and the shard compositing are paid. That is the honest read of §1.1: 192 × 144 is cheap; a big tube is not, and a 1× display halves it.

Mobile (≤ 700 px). Order: television → spectrum scope → 段階 progress bar → PLAY/STOP/volume. The chassis-behind view flattens — the crop keeps the pan, the harness and the 高電圧 decal, and the bell and yoke go, which is what you would see of a bench from a phone's distance. The service rail becomes two rows of three. The tube keeps its full width and does not shrink; at a 390 px phone it is ≈ 322 px of glass at 1.68 screen pixels per source pixel instead of today's 3.3, so it gets sharper without getting bigger. Idle. A dim raster with a sync bar drifting slowly up the tube (≈ 9 s a pass, the classic mains-hum bar) — the set is not off, it is unterminated. The yoke's copper catches a specular highlight that moves as the page scrolls. Every 12–30 s the raster folds into one line of light — Zen for TV — holds a beat, and closes, sometimes shrinking to a dot first. A test card surfaces from the depth now and then. Nothing else.

Gesture. Tune-in (0.4 s of snow resolving) → hold 8–12 s with weather (flutter, ghost, frame-holds, dropouts) → loss over 1.6–2.8 s (dropouts thicken, the vertical hold slips, tear, collapse to a line, snow burst, dead) → 1.6 s dead → idle. It runs on a ~25 s timer so the page can be left alone and watched, and on demand from the 受信 momentary on the bench-top row, whose lens is lit only while the receiver will actually answer. The 受信 lamp beside it lights on hold and flickers through tuning and loss; the VFD logs 「受信 · title · year」 and 「消失」, and the rail's own gestures log as 修理. Deviations from the brief, and why. (1) The plan's mobile mechanism — display: contents on both bank groups — is used at every width here, with explicit grid-column / grid-row. It gives the settled 3fr / 2fr with the set in column 1 exactly as specified, and it lets column 2 carry scope → 段階 → transport — the owner's own mobile order — with PLAY / STOP / volume dropped to the bottom of the column so the two columns close level with each other.
