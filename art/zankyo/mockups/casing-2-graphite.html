What changed, and the trade-offs

  • One plastic. The scope, speaker, module, log, console and now the second set all read from --plastic with one radius (8), one top highlight and one shadow recipe: 156 hand-tuned hexes become about 30 tokens. The set's brown-black and cream highlights are gone; it stays the old tube through the crack, tape, its model line, a heavier soot corner and the 受信専用 label.
  • The ledge is cut with the deck's tool. Rocker ends and the 受信 push use the deck-key cap (same gradient, 2/2/3/3 profile, finger ribs, glyph low on the cap, same 2 px travel treatment), the well is the key bed, and the number/ladder windows are the same glass as the log and module.
  • Chassis: brushed-metal stripes, rust blooms and neon spill replaced by darker powder-coated pressed steel with orange-peel grain, soot, bare-metal nicks and a stamped rim line. It is a step darker than the plastic, so the casings read as raised mouldings. Trade-off: the machine loses its rust, which was warm and a good part of "derelict"; the grime now has to carry that on its own.
  • Colour is now information. Kanji on the console are ink, not red; the log tags can no longer be told apart by colour (they use solid / dashed / dotted / filled outlines instead), and the 14 magenta preview buttons become small deck keys with a PLAY-green triangle. The bargraph is the classic green / amber / red ramp, no colour of its own.
  • Not done here: the live spectrum and waveform colours are painted in zankyo-viz.js (not CSS) and need the same signal-green; the set's own picture (set.png) is kept as is. Legacy tokens --cyan / --magenta / --red / --amber / --green are re-pointed in this file so nothing stray survives; they should be renamed outright on port.
