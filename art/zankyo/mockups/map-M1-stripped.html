MAP OPTION M1 · 標 THE TALLY pure indicator · no interaction

The argument. The ribbon is the cheapest thing in this set of six and it should stay that way, because the thing it does is already enough: at four in the morning a picture arrives out of nowhere, and a light comes on over Pyongyang. Nothing is clicked. Nothing is hovered. There is no tooltip, no zoom, no legend that appears on mouse-over — a second tube does not have controls, it has a picture, and the picture is the world. What that buys is the one thing an interactive map cannot have: it can be looked at from across the room, and it is never in a wrong state because there is no state to put it in. The design work went into two places instead. The first is idle: the signal is rare, so the ribbon spends the night refreshing itself cell by cell under a slow terminator, and once every half-minute it stops being a map and becomes a census — every reel in the pool flashes at once, and you see the archive's real shape (East Asia and northern Europe on fire, the Pacific empty). The second is honesty: half this pool is country-level only, and the ribbon says so in its own grammar rather than in a footnote — see below, because it is the whole design.

PROJECTION

Cropped equirectangular (plate carrée), 180°W–180°E by 60°N–48°S, 240 × 72 cells of 1.5° each. Square cells: one cell is 1.5° of longitude and 1.5° of latitude, so nothing is stretched and the grid is a real graticule rather than a decoration — the ribbon is a piece of graph paper with the world printed on it, which is exactly the lo-fi register asked for. Equirectangular is also the only projection where the longitude rail above the ribbon can be a straight linear scale, and where a dot's position is trivially checkable against the numbers printed beside it. The crop is chosen by the pool, not by taste: the southernmost reel is Auckland at 36.9°S and the northernmost placeable cluster is Tallinn and Helsinki at 59–60°N, so 60/−48 holds everything the archive has with two cells to spare. The exception, declared: three reels fall above the crop — the two Igloolik ones (Inuit Broadcasting Corporation, Nunavut, 69.4°N) and Helsinki at 60.2°N. They are pinned to the top row of cells, a caret lights on the rail above them and the plate is stamped 北緯60°超 >60°N; they are never silently moved. 108° of latitude against 360° of longitude gives exactly 10 : 3 — 488 × 146 CSS px under a 488 × 366 tube, which is the ribbon the owner described.

Rejected: a squatter crop (±40°) would have given a truer ribbon (9 : 2) at the cost of London, Tokyo, Moscow, Berlin and Warsaw — about 90 reels. Any pseudo-cylindrical projection (Robinson, Winkel) curves the meridians, which kills both the graticule and the straight rail. A vertically-compressed cylindrical would have bought a wider ribbon by lying about a cell's shape, and a cell's shape is the one thing this map has.

CONFIDENCE — THE WHOLE DESIGN

Half the pool is country-level only (…). A reel that names a broadcaster and nothing else — NHK, SABC, TRT, Egyptian Channel 1 — is placed on that broadcaster's home city, and drawing it as a pinprick over that city would be a lie about roughly every second reel. So the three confidences get three different marks, and the difference is enforced in the renderer, not by eye:

confmarkwhat it means, and the rule
citya 1-cell pinprick, a tight halo, edge crosshairsa place actually named in the title or notes. One cell = 1.5°, ≈ 160 km — the only mark allowed to reach the white core of the phosphor ramp, and (with region) the only one given crosshair ticks at the ribbon's edges, which is the plotting board saying this column, this row.
regiona 3-cell bloom, no corea sub-national area; the city is inferred. Capped at 0.72 of the ramp, so it never whitens; it keeps the edge crosshairs, because a region still has a column and a row.
countrya wide soft bloom, no core at allonly the nation is evidenced. A Gaussian of radius 5 cells (≈ 800 km) stretched by 1/cos φ so it is a circle on the globe, hard-capped at 0.46 of the ramp — it is numerically impossible for a country-confident reel to draw a bright dot. No core, no crosshair, no column, no row: it reads as a glow over a region, which is what the data is.
unknownnothingone reel (a Nyingmapa ritual, site unrecorded). Its coordinates are a cultural anchor, not a location, so the ribbon draws no mark and the plate reads 位置不明 NO FIX. Drawing it would be the worst thing this map could do.

COINCIDENT POINTS — WHY IT IS CALLED THE TALLY

Reels stack: …. At 2 px per cell those are the same cell, and a naive dot would give you one hot blob over East Asia that never changes. The answer is in the name. Every reel that plays leaves the cell lit, and a repeat visit makes it brighter — the residue is logarithmic (0.17 · log₂(1+n)), so the fourth visit to Tokyo still reads as more than the first but the cell never blows out. The plate prints ×n the moment a cell has been visited before, so the count is a number as well as a brightness, and the night accumulates into a record you can read at a glance: by morning the ribbon is the log. The half-minute roll-call does the same thing for the whole archive at once — every located reel contributes its count, and Pyongyang's 13, Tokyo's 10 and London's 6 are legible as three different brightnesses rather than three identical dots. This is the one place where the pool's shape is visible anywhere on the faceplate.

THE TWO SPECIAL CASES

TV-DX (15 reels) and the arc (6). Fifteen reels are flagged dx — a long-distance catch — but only six name the receiver, so only six can have two ends. A dx reel without a receiver gets a cyan return-ring that expands out of the transmitter cell and dies, twice a second, in the grid's own steps: it says this was heard a long way off without inventing where. The six that do name a receiver get the arc: a great circle from transmitter to receiver, rasterised into lit cells (never a smooth curve — the ribbon has no curves), drawn in over 0.9 s, with the receiving end marked by a hollow cyan ring and the plate printing the real great-circle distance. Quezon City → Tokyo is 2,950 km; Moscow → Brisbane is 14,500 km and crosses two thirds of the ribbon, which is the most spectacular thing this map does all night.

Off-Earth (7 reels). Two from the Moon, four from orbit, one from interstellar space. These have no honest dot, so the ribbon admits it: the whole strip drops to 45 % — the world is not where this came from — and the marker moves off the map onto the longitude rail above it, at the longitude of the Earth station that received it, with a dotted tether down to that station's cell and the realm in kanji (月 MOON · 軌道 ORBIT · 深宇宙 DEEP SPACE). The ground station is still true and still drawn, quietly; the claim that the sound came from there is the part that is withdrawn. For deep-space the tether is drawn but the marker sits hard against the rail's right edge with an arrow off the page, because Voyager is not at a longitude. (Note: Mercury-Redstone 1 deliberately carries no realm — that flight never left the pad, so it is simply Cape Canaveral.)

IDLE — MOST OF THE NIGHT

Never a static image. Four things run at once and none of them is a loop you can count: a refresh head walks the grid column by column on a 21 s pass, re-lighting each column of land as it crosses and leaving a decaying trail — the tube redrawing itself, the way a slow raster does; a terminator (a 96 s day/night band) rides across underneath it so the land's base brightness breathes from west to east; the graticule — equator, ±30°, the meridians every 30° — pulses on a 7 s period about a third as bright as land; and single cells spark at random, a few a second, like dust in the phosphor. Every 26–40 s the roll-call (点呼) interrupts: the land drops away and all 251 placed reels light at once by count, column by column left to right over 1.4 s, hold, and fade back. The bench button reproduces it on demand.

WEAK POINTS, DECLARED

(1) The country bloom is the same size everywhere. A country-confident reel from Luxembourg and one from Russia get the same 5-cell cloud, because geo.json carries no national extent. It is honest about precision and dishonest about extent; the fix is a country-radius column in the geo file, and it is the first thing I would ask for. (2) At 390 px the ribbon re-rasters to a coarser 160 × 48 grid (2.25° cells) so that a cell stays about 2.2 px and the grid stays visibly a grid — the map is genuinely lower-resolution on a phone, and small islands (Hawaii, the Philippines' smaller islands) drop below one cell. Declared rather than hidden: a 1.35 px cell is not a cell. (3) The dot is a lit cell, not a labelled place — with no interaction, the only way to learn a place name is to read the plate under the ribbon, which means the map and its caption are two glances, not one. That is the price of the no-interaction rule and it is the thing most worth arguing about.
