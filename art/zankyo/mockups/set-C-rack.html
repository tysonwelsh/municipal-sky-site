SET C · 放送架 THE RACK MONITOR — variable-first

The argument. Every other control on this machine changes how the night looks or sounds; these four change what arrives. The archive is 250 reels of other people's television and it is wildly lopsided — 107 reels from the 1980s and three from the 2000s — and until now that shape was invisible, a weight buried in zk-broadcast.js. The drum dial puts it on the panel: the pool's own histogram is etched into the cylinder, so spinning past 1955 you can see that the fifties are a low band and the eighties a wall, and the reel that arrives thirty seconds later proves the dial did something. That is the whole case for this option — it is the only one of the four whose controls the owner would reach for on a given night, because "tonight, the fifties" is a thing a person actually wants, and 常時 (the picture never leaves) turns the visitation into a television, which is the one mode change this machine does not yet have. The casing is the most institutional of the four on purpose: a master-control monitor is a thing that is watched for a living, which is the correct posture for a set that is receiving rather than performing. The ears, the punched face, the handles and the tally are all load-bearing — the ears are how it is in the rack, the perforations are how the tube's heat gets out, the handles are how 20 kg of glass got lifted in, and the tally is the only lamp in a gallery that means something. Nothing here is a bevel for the sake of a bevel.

PHOSPHOR

P39, long-persistence yellow-green — the live set's LUT, unchanged (black → #061a0c → #1a8a3a → #7dea9a → pale mint at white), with real persistence: the previous frame decays under the new one at 0.62, so motion smears and a dropout leaves an afterimage. A master-control picture monitor was a mono tube and this is the correct one; the scope beside it keeps its neon, so the two tubes read as different decades. The crack survives. The yard re-cased the set — it did not re-glass it — so the same fracture data drives the same four shards and the same refraction edge, and one piece of the old tape came across with the tube.

MOBILE

At ≤ 700 px the bank collapses to one column and the order is the owner's: television → spectrum scope → 段階 progress bar → PLAY / STOP / volume (display: contents on the two bank wrappers plus explicit order). The rack ears crop from 13 px to a 4 px hairline and lose their holes — the flange is still there, the fastenings are not worth the pixels. The drum dial goes full width as its own row above the other three controls, which wrap beneath it; the handles narrow to 14 px. The tube keeps its full width — 336 × 252 at a 390 px phone, measured — and gets sharper, not bigger: 1.75 screen px per source pixel instead of 3.5.

IDLE

The tally is dark, the readout shows --, and the tube runs a monitoring pattern: a dim pulse crossing the raster top to bottom on the station's own ~100 s jo-ha-kyū cycle, brightening with the arc — so the height of the bar on the glass tells you where in the night you are without a number anywhere. Under it the faint raster crawls, and now and then everything folds into Paik's single line of light and closes.
