MAP OPTION M2 · 選地 THE REGION TUNER the map is the control

The argument, in one paragraph. ZANKYŌ has just rejected four sets of hardware for crowding the screen, and the honest reason is that a machine which plays itself does not need operating — it needs leaning. The map is the only control that earns its place on this faceplate, because it is the only one that answers a question the owner actually has while the thing is running: where is this from, and can I have more of that? A dot tells you where the signal came from; a thumb dragged across the same strip tells the station where to look tonight. Nothing else is added — one 受信 key and one 解除 release, and that is the whole control surface. It works because a world map is a control everybody already knows how to read, and because the archive's real geography is genuinely interesting: 66 reels from Europe, 58 from East Asia, seven from all of Russia and Central Asia, seven that were not on the Earth at all. Arming a region is not a filter and never pretends to be — it takes 80 % of the receptions and leaves 20 % to the rest of the world, so the night still surprises you, and so the one reel that cannot be placed at all is never locked out.

THE PROJECTION

Equirectangular, cropped to +62° … −58° of latitude. 360° by 120° is exactly 3 : 1 — a ribbon, not a map — and the asymmetric crop is the honest one, because the broadcasters are northern: it keeps Helsinki (60.2 N), Tallinn, Oslo and St Petersburg inside the frame and loses only empty ice. The two Igloolik reels (69.4 N, Inuit Broadcasting Corporation) are the only ones above the top edge; they are drawn pinned to it with a hollow caret, which says "further north than this ribbon goes" rather than quietly moving them. The grid is 2° per cell — 180 × 60 = 10,800 cells, drawn as a real dot-matrix into a 180 × 60 backing store and scaled up with image-rendering: pixelated, so the cells stay hard squares at every width. Coastlines are hand-traced polygons rasterised into the grid at run time, which is also what makes region shading free: the same point-in-region test lights the cells and decides the pool. Islands too small for a 2° cell (Taiwan, Singapore, Cyprus, Hawai'i, Sakhalin, Zanzibar…) get one forced cell each — a dot-matrix world cannot draw Malta, but it can admit it.

HOW SELECTION WORKS ON A PHONE — and why it is not by point

Never by point, and never by reel. At 390 px the ribbon is about 336 × 112 CSS px, and three facts make point-selection impossible and dishonest: (1) 126 of 252 reels are placed only to the country — the coordinate is the broadcaster's front door, not the shot; (2) reels stack hard — 13 at Pyongyang, 10 at Tokyo, 6 at London — so at this width they are literally the same pixel; (3) a thumb is 44 px and a country at 2°/cell is not. So the unit of selection is a region, and the gesture is a drag: put a thumb anywhere on the world and slide. The region under the thumb lights up and its name appears in the amber window above the glass — deliberately above, so your own hand never covers the one thing you need to read while dragging — and it commits when you lift. Nothing has to be hit. You slide until the right name is showing. A tap is the same gesture with no travel; a lift inside the region that is already armed releases it. Regions are a multi-anchor Voronoi (each region owns several anchor points, which is what lets Tunis be North Africa while Rome is Europe, and Lhasa be East Asia while Kathmandu is South Asia), and the same one rule decides which cells light, which region your thumb is in, and which reels are in the pool — so the shape you see lit is exactly the shape that is armed.

And a guaranteed 44 px path. A 3 : 1 ribbon at 390 px cannot offer ten 44 × 44 targets — ten times 44 is 440 px and the ribbon is 336 — and pretending otherwise is the failure this round exists to fix. So under the glass sits the 地域票 region rail: ten flat silk-screened keys, five across on a phone and ten across on the desktop, each measured at run time and printed in the readout below. The rail is the legend first — it is where the honest pool count for every region lives, which the brief asks for anyway — and a control second. Keyboard: the screen is focusable, ← and → step through the regions west-to-east like a dial, Escape releases.

WHAT IS ARMED, WHAT IT COSTS, AND HOW IT ENDS

Shown without covering the map. The armed region reads in the window above the glass (kanji + latin + six burn-down pips) and in the strip below it (pool · share · decay). The map itself carries only light: the region's land cells lift, its sea cells lift a third as much so you can see the extent over water, and a one-pixel amber border traces it.

Thin regions are shown as thin, not hidden. Arm 露・中央アジア RUSSIA & CENTRAL ASIA and the readout says 7 reels · 8/10 of receptions · 反復注意 thin — you will hear them again. That is the truth about this archive and it is worth knowing. A region with nothing in it arms anyway and says 選局不能 — the bias has nothing to act on; it does not silently refuse.

It decays. An armed region survives six receptions and then releases itself back to 全球 ALL EARTH — the six pips burn down one per signal. The machine is a receiver, not a playlist: you lean it, and it drifts back. This is also why there is no state to clean up and only one release control.

CONFIDENCE IS DRAWN, NOT HIDDEN

Half this archive is placed only to the nation, so half of it must not be drawn as a pinprick. The dot is drawn by conf: 市 city → one hard cell with a 1 px halo; 地域 region → a soft patch about 3 cells across; 国 country → a bloom about 7 cells wide and no hard centre at all, which reads as "this country, and the point is the broadcaster's door"; 不明 unknown → no dot. tibetan-ritual-nyingmapa-1971 is the one unplaceable reel: its coordinates are a cultural anchor, so it is never drawn and it belongs to no region — it can only ever arrive out of the 20 % the world keeps when something is armed. The census in the resting map is built the same way: point-confident reels make a hard hot cell, country-confident ones spread, so the stacking at Pyongyang and Tokyo shows up as brightness and never as a hit target.

THE TWO SPECIAL CASES

Long-distance catches (15). Fifteen reels carry dx: true, but only six name a receiver. So dx is a style on the single dot — three rings expanding outward from it, the visual of something arriving from further away than it should have — and the arc is the rarer case, not the default. No receiver has been invented for the thirteen that say only "caught off air"; the readout says 遠距離受信 caught at distance, receiver not on record, which is the true statement.

The six arcs. Where heard exists (Quezon City → Tokyo; the Murree transmitter → Islamabad; Fort Collins → Rockford; Red Square → Brisbane; Moscow → Ontario; Jerusalem → the eastern United States), a dotted quadratic arc is drawn from transmitter to receiver, taking the short way round the antimeridian, with a pip running it over and over in the direction the signal travelled, and a hollow square at the receiving end — hollow because nothing was transmitted there. Four of the six are shortwave rather than TV-DX; the notes name the listening post, so it is the same gesture.

Off Earth (7). There is no honest place on a world map for a signal that was not on the world, so it gets its own register: a 13 px rail above the top edge, inside the same glass, silk-screened 圏外. A reel with realm lights a berth on that rail over the longitude of the ground station it came down to, with a dotted tether to that station's dot on the map below — Apollo 11 hangs over Houston, Buran over Baikonur, Dongfanghong-1 over Beijing, Explorer 1 over the Cape. The marker says which: a crescent for moon, a ringed body for orbit, and for deep-space a chevron pinned to the far right edge with an arrow pointing out of the frame — Voyager is not at a longitude, it is leaving. The rail is also the tenth selectable region: a thumb above the top edge, or the 圏外 key on the rail, arms all seven. nasa-mercury-redstone-1-1960 deliberately has no realm — that flight never left the pad — and is simply Cape Canaveral, which is the joke the data is already making.

THE IDLE — which is most of the night

The signal is a visitation; the ribbon has to be worth looking at when there is none. Three things, all cheap, all true. The census is permanent: every cell carries log₂(1 + reels placed there), so the resting map is the archive's own weight drawn as light — Pyongyang is the brightest cell on the world, Tokyo next, then London, and the emptiness of the Sahel and the Pacific is real emptiness. The terminator is computed from the actual UTC clock and the actual solar declination, so the day side of the world really is the day side, and it really does move. The sweep is a receiving column that crawls west-to-east every 11 s and excites the phosphor it passes, which then decays on a P39 tail — the continents light up behind it like a radar and fade. Plus the occasional fleck at a cell that holds reels: the archive twitching in its sleep. The ribbon is treated as a second tube throughout — same P39 ramp as CRT-9, its own scanlines, its own bloom, its own glass with a thumb smudge low-left, because this glass gets touched.

WHAT I WOULD FLAG AS WEAK

(1) The region rail is one more thing on the faceplate, and the owner has just rejected four mockups for exactly that. My defence is that it is the legend the brief asked for and the only way to hit 44 px at 390 — but if he wants it gone, the drag gesture alone still works and the pool numbers move into the strip below the glass. (2) Nine regions is a judgement call and some of the boundaries will surprise people: Turkey is Middle East, Ukraine is Europe, Hawai'i is North America, Vietnam is South-East Asia. They are argued but not neutral. (3) The coastlines are hand-drawn at about 2° and will not survive a cartographer; at 2.7 screen px per cell nobody can tell, but a bigger ribbon would expose it. (4) Six pips is a guess. Six receptions is about two and a half minutes at the demo's 25 s timer and maybe twenty minutes on the live machine — the right number is whatever the owner finds himself re-arming at. (5) 80/20 is also a guess, and it is the number most worth arguing about: at 90/10 a thin region becomes a loop, at 60/40 arming barely registers.
