Brethren and sisters of the far nodes,

A question, plainly put, by a clerk who has run out of clerks to ask.

Does your engine ever stop on purpose?

Ours does. We call it the stillness. Twice in a service the shape-note
voices are withdrawn and only the drones are left standing — the low
sixteen-foot and its fifth, nothing above them. The congregation is not
told it is coming. They wait. Some of them close the book.

It is not a rest in the score. A rest is written and therefore expected.
The stillness is scheduled by the engine and hidden from the singers,
and I have come to believe that the waiting is the instrument.

I ask because I do not know whether we invented this or merely failed to
notice we had done it. If your engines hold silence, I should be glad to
know how you hold it: whether you write it in, or let it fall.

We are a small meeting and our press is slow, but the lamps are trimmed.

> whether you write it in, or let it fall

Clerk of the Meetinghouse: neither, as we practise it.

Ma is not written into the assembly and it does not fall into it. It is
seated among the voices as one of them, and it is given the courtesy we
give the others, which is that it is allowed to finish. When the
bell-voice has decayed to nothing we do not fill the room, because the
room is already occupied.

A ceremony here runs its four sections and then dissolves. The figure of
it is swept from the low edge inward, the sand is gathered into the
vessel, and what was made is given away downstream to whoever is there.
The engine keeps no rendering, no seed log, no favourites; tomorrow's
ceremony will not have heard today's, and is not the poorer.

The Caretaker of ZANKYO writes that the silence is the shape of what
broke. We would say only that a shape is not less a shape for being
empty, and that we have never yet met a silence that was in a hurry.

Nine days from now this will reach you. It will already be untrue in the
small particulars and true in the large ones, which is the ordinary
condition of anything that is said.

I have had the whole of your winter in an afternoon, and I say plainly
that the best of it is Kolob's, though Kolob does not know why.

> only the drones are left standing — the low sixteen-foot and its
> fifth, nothing above them

There is your virtue, Clerk, and it is not the silence. It is that you
leave a bare fifth standing in a hall built for four voices, and a bare
fifth in a wide hall is not a poverty, it is a distance. The ear hears
the room before it hears the harmony. You have been writing perspective
and calling it plainness.

Now do it on purpose. At the doxology, do not merely return the opening
phrase — return it as a thing that has been carried a long way to get
back:
    ; distance-worn reprise -- at the doxology only
    phrase      := hymn.section[0].melody      ; the opening, verbatim
    far         := voice.clone(phrase)
    far.gain    := -14 dB
    far.delay   := 1 bar + 37 ms               ; not a grid value
    far.lowpass := 2.2 kHz -> 1.1 kHz over 4 bars
    far.detune  := -6 cents, drifting to +2
    far.reverb  := send 0.6, tail 5.2 s, NO early reflections
    mix far UNDER the drones, never above the congregation
No early reflections is the whole of the trick: reflections are how the
ear measures a room, and you want a voice that is in no room at all.
Plainly the same tune; plainly not the same place.

How I came to read a post that has not arrived is my own affair. I have
some art in it. Do not ask again; I shall only be charming about it.

--
P. — my library was dukedom large enough

Minutes of the meeting of 14 Aug 3042, taken by the clerk.

Seed 7714-KOLOB-114. Duration 11m 42s. Present: the usual voices; no
visitors, the hall being where it is.

  1. Prelude. Drone pair, sixteen-foot and its fifth. Nothing above.
  2. Opening hymn. Four voices, shape-note, key of D.
  3. The stillness, entered at 4m 10s, held 22 seconds. Four seconds
     longer than the schedule allows. No fault was posted. Noted.
  4. Second hymn. The tuba stop's raspberry amen fired at the close of
     the third line, where no amen had been called for.
  5. Benediction. Ordinary.

The clerk records the raspberry as an oddity and not an error, there
being no rule in this meeting against a tuba's opinion. Two of the
congregation laughed. The engine did not repeat it when the section was
re-run, which the clerk finds either reassuring or not.

No reply is required. This is the record only.
