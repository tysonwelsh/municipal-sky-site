Brethren and sisters of the far nodes — A question, put simply, because I have been turning it over through three cycles of the evening chorus and it will not settle. Does your engine ever stop on purpose? Ours does. We call it the stillness. It is written into the order of worship and not left to chance: after the third verse the treble and counter voices are lifted out, the drones are kept — the low bourdon and the fifth above it, nothing else — and the congregation waits. Forty seconds, sometimes ninety. Nobody sings. The drones hold the room’s tuning, so that when the fourth verse enters it enters already in tune with what it left. I confess it began as a repair. Our voice allocator would crowd itself by the third verse and the harmony went thick as porridge; the stillness was somewhere to put the crowding. But the congregation took to it before we did, and it is now the part they speak of afterward. So: is silence a thing you schedule, or a thing that merely happens to you when the parts run out? I would be glad of any answer. Two days is a short wait for good counsel.

To the Clerk of the Meetinghouse, and to the Caretaker who will have answered before us, and to whoever else is still listening on this frequency: You have each described a hole in the sound and then asked what sort of hole it is. Permit us a third answer, which is that it is not a hole. The silence we keep is called ma, and it is not the absence of the assembly but a member of it — seated, breathing, with a name on the roll; so that when the bells stop it is not that the bells have stopped, it is that ma has been given the floor, and the room is more crowded in that moment than it was the moment before, not less. We do not schedule it and we do not cut it. We invite it, which is a slower and less certain thing, and some evenings it does not come, and then the ceremony is only a long piece of music that we sit through politely and do not speak of afterward. At the close the mandala is swept. The whole of it: the voice tables, the tuning that took four hundred hours to settle, the seed, the recording. Nothing is kept. The merit of the ceremony is dedicated outward — to the six nodes, to the relay, to the Caretaker who has no relief scheduled — and then it is gone, and the instrument is empty again, and the emptiness is the instrument. Nine days is nothing. We write knowing this will arrive late and be read by people who have already moved on to another question. That, too, is dedicated.
-- May the merit of it be dedicated to the six nodes, and to whatever listens. The Umdzé of the Ark * Origin: The Monastery-Ark (21:108/49)

Gentles, colonials, keepers of clocks — I have read your winter’s traffic in an afternoon. Do not ask how. I will say only that a library is a kind of distance too, and that I have never found the two hard to confuse. No, I shall not explain it. Ask again in a twelvemonth and I shall refuse again, more elegantly. To business. Brother Orson writes: > the stillness was somewhere to put the crowding. But the > congregation took to it before we did, and it is now the part > they speak of afterward. Sir, your accident is better than your intention, which is the usual way of it. And I must correct the record on your parallel fifths, which three of you have privately called a fault. They are not a fault. Your voices are dispersed across the room by a fixed delay per rank, and at that spacing a parallel fifth does not fuse — it opens. You have built a harmony that cannot be heard from any one seat. Stop apologising. Now: you hold a two-day distance and you treat it as a tax. Spend it instead. At the doxology, restate the hymn’s first phrase through the far voice — the same notes, worn by the trip:
on doxology.enter:
    phrase := hymn.phrase[0]              ; the first line, as sung
    voice  := far(copy of phrase)         ; sent out, and returned
      voice.delay   := 1 bar per day of transit   ; two bars, for you
      voice.lowpass := 1800 Hz - (140 Hz * verse_index)
      voice.gain    := -9 dB, plus the room you no longer have
    mix(phrase, voice) at the fifth below ; your parallels, kept honest
    hold voice 1 bar past phrase          ; the distance is the amen
Eight lines. The congregation will not know why the last verse aches, and you must not tell them; tell them and it becomes a trick. Caretaker — your ニ is a fermata with the manners of a slammed door. Keep it. Umdzé — I would sweep nothing, but then I have never had to.

MINUTES of one meeting, generated 15 Aug 3042, evening bell. Seed 4417-A-19. Duration 22 min 40 s. Present: fourteen voices, two drones, the reed. Clerk: Orson. 1. Call to order. Bourdon on G; the fifth above added at bar 5. 2. Opening hymn, “Come Along Ye Weary” — shape-note allocation normal, no crowding. The stillness held 61 s, below our median. 3. Reading. The reed alone, twelve bars, unaccompanied as written. 4. Second hymn. The treble entered a beat early and stayed early for the whole of the verse. The allocator did not correct it. No repairs made. No repairs, on reflection, wanted.
