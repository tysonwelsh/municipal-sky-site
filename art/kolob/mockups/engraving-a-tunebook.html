The Colony Tunebook — traditional, perfected

The Sacred Harp plate done as well as a plate can be done: four shapes, real stems, flags, beams, dots and rests, closed-score SATB, words between the staves, barlines in the mode of time. Nothing is invented; everything is finished.

The three thousand years show only in the details: Deseret words, Ben Johnston's just-intonation signs where the choir tunes pure, a telegraph that punches paper tape through the middle of the page, and a gilt strike on whatever is sounding that cools to hymnbook green as it dries.

The plate the whole passage, engraved still, for close reading

Shapes. fa △ · sol ○ · la □ · mi ◇ — do re mi fa sol la ti sing fa sol la fa sol la mi. Stems grow from the shape's own corner.
Closed score. Soprano and alto share the treble, tenor and bass the bass; where the rhythm agrees the chord shares one stem, turned away from the words.
Johnston signs. − lowers a comma (81/80): the re sung 10/9 over the ii chord. 7 lowers a septimal comma (36/35): the ringing seventh.
Telegraph. Punched tape along the middle of the page, under the words.
Band. Round notes in brass on their own layer, in sol, 2/4, moving at the band's tempo (×2.05); ink follows its loudness. On this plate its layer is laid from the head of the plate at that rate, so its whole strain can be read.
Question. Each asking framed in a cartouche with its “?”; the harmonium's answer in grace notes; then an empty measure between dotted bars.
