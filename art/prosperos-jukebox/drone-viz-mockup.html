Drone Visualization Mockup

~ sound wave + coupled waves + ring + coupled rings + pulse constellation ~

Five visualizations for the library drone. The first four are the same slowed-sound-wave concept in four geometries: line (summed), two lines (per-tone), closed loop (summed), and two side-by-side loops (per-tone). Real partial frequencies (~65–200 Hz) become 1–3 Hz visible at 60× slowdown, matching the slow meditative pace of the drone. Pulse Constellation carries the pitch / harmonic / interval / cycle-progress information on a chromatic circle.

Library track 1.6× 35%

1. Slowed Sound Wave

single line · the actual wave, slowed

A single line whose shape is the literal sum of slowed-down partial waves — each active partial contributes a sinusoid whose spatial wavelength is proportional to its actual frequency (lowest partial fills the canvas with ~2 wavelengths), and whose temporal amplitude oscillates at the partial's slowed pitch rate (real freq ÷ 30). The line takes the shape of a real sound wave on a string, just slow enough to see. Beats emerge naturally as relative phases drift.

2. Coupled Sound Waves

two stacked lines · one per tone

The horizontal counterpart to Coupled Wave Rings — two stacked sound waves, one per drone tone. The top lane carries the lower fundamental + its harmonics + sub-octave; the bottom lane carries the upper fundamental + its harmonics. Each lane is normalized to its own tone's fMin so each fundamental gets ~2 wavelengths across the lane width. The pitch ratio between the two tones is visible as the rate ratio of the two waves' oscillations.

3. Sound Wave Ring

closed loop · sound wave in a circle

The same Slowed Sound Wave wrapped into a closed circle. The base ring has a fixed radius; each angle θ around it carries the summed wave displacement (radially in/out from the ring). Lowest partial fits ~2 wavelengths around the circumference, higher partials add more bumps. For partials in integer ratios (like a perfect 5th, 3:2), the shape repeats around the ring and closes cleanly. Each partial's contribution pulses in amplitude at its slowed pitch rate, so the ring shape breathes in place.

4. Coupled Wave Rings

two concentric rings · one per tone

Two side-by-side closed loops — one per drone tone. The left ring carries the lower fundamental plus its harmonics (and the sub-octave when present); the right ring carries the upper fundamental plus its harmonics. Each ring is normalized to its own tone, so each fundamental fits ~2 wavelengths around its own circumference — the spatial signature of each tone is visible separately rather than summed. Both pulse at their slowed pitch rates. These two rings correspond directly to the two partial nodes on the Pulse Constellation — the same data, just laid out by physics here vs by pitch class there.

5. Pulse Constellation

chromatic geometry + pulsing rings

Active partials are placed on a chromatic circle (12 pitch classes labeled C–B around the ring, octave-from-center via radius). Each partial node emits an expanding ring outward — the ring's radius tracks the cycle's progress (0 at note start, full size at fade-out), its opacity follows the envelope. The connecting line between simultaneous partials is colored and thickened by interval consonance; the interval name (P5, P4, m3...) sits at its midpoint. Smaller dots at 3f and 5f harmonic positions show where the triangle overtones land in pitch-class space. Sub-octaves render in deeper amber, with their own smaller ring.
