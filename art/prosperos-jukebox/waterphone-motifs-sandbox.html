Waterphone Motifs

~ seven options for the Sycorax waterphone layer ~

The waterphone layer currently has one motif type — a single sustained tone. These six new patterns add melodic motion, dynamic contrast, and dramatic gestures. The synthesis core is the same as voice 3 (Sea Bell); the difference is what notes get played, with what timing, and at what intensity. Pitches are pulled from the Sycorax chromatic-Locrian scale (Eb4 – B4) like the actual track.

1. Tone

Current default · sustained single note

One sustained waterphone note, 8 seconds. The motif that the jukebox already plays. Included here for A/B reference against the new options.

ready

2. Call

Two-note gesture · breath between

First note holds for ~2.8 s, then a brief breath of ~0.3 s, then a second note at a dissonant interval above (tritone 60% / minor 2nd 40%) for ~3.6 s. Feels like the instrument is asking a quick question and then answering itself. Adds gestural rhythm to a layer that's otherwise static-pitched.

ready

3. Lament

Slow descending phrase

A 3 – 4 note descending Markov walk, each note 4 – 5 s, overlapping into the next. The phrase glides downward through the chromatic-Locrian scale. Adds melodic motion — a falling line where previously the waterphone could only hold a single tone.

ready

4. Reach

Slow ascending phrase

Opposite of Lament — 2 – 3 notes climbing stepwise, the last note held longest. Tense, anxious feel; like something rising slowly out of water. Weighted rarer than Lament in the rotation so the descending gesture stays the dominant melodic motion.

ready

5. Pendulum

Zigzag · alternating high-low

4 – 5 notes alternating above and below the starting degree, with intervals shrinking toward the center as the phrase progresses (e.g., +2, −2, +1, −1, 0). The zigzag motion contrasts with Lament's straight descent and Reach's straight ascent — like a slow swing settling toward rest. Sometimes plays a tighter 4-note +1, −1, +1, −1 oscillation instead.

ready

6. Apparition

Very long, very quiet single note

Same single-tone structure as Tone but 15 seconds long with the envelope peak dropped to about 55%. Feels distant, barely there. Adds dynamic variety — a moment where the waterphone recedes rather than presents.

ready

7. Resonant Pair

Two simultaneous notes · beat patterns

Two waterphone notes played at the same time at a dissonant interval (tritone or minor 2nd). The close partials produce audible beat patterns — interference rhythms that move slowly through the sustain. Like a ghost-tone chord but with the waterphone's bell + bloom texture. Intense; weighted rare.

ready
