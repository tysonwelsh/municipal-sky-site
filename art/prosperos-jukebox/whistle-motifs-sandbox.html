Whistle Motifs

~ seven options for the Ariel whistle layer ~

The whistle currently has one motif type — a 2 – 3 note Markov phrase. These seven cards explore alternative patterns, all built on the same flute-PeriodicWave + breathy-noise + 5.5 Hz vibrato synthesis the actual track uses. Pitches come from F Lydian (F G A B C D E), the same scale Ariel uses. Lydian Gesture specifically highlights the F-to-B tritone — the interval that gives Lydian its fairytale character.

1. Phrase

Markov phrase · 2–8 notes

2 – 8 notes Markov-walked over F Lydian, each 0.6 – 1.1 s with a tiny breath between. Stepwise-biased Markov so the line wanders gently. Length varies widely — sometimes just a quick two-note utterance, sometimes a longer meandering phrase.

ready

2. Lone Tone

Single held note · deeper vibrato · occasional bend

One sustained note at the starting degree, 2 – 5 s. Vibrato depth bumped up (4 cents vs the default 2) so the held tone breathes more visibly. ~35% of fires include a subtle pitch bend mid-note (±0.7 – 1.2 semitones, going slightly flat or sharp then returning) — reads as the whistler's voice catching expressively rather than the note sitting perfectly still.

ready

3. Climb

Ascending Markov walk · 4–5 notes

4 – 5 notes trending upward via a directional Markov walk — most steps go up by one scale degree, but ~20% step back, ~15% repeat, and ~5% leap up by two. Each note slightly longer than the last; the final note held longest. Less mechanical than a strict ascent — each fire takes its own path.

ready

4. Sigh

Descending Markov walk · diminuendo

3 – 4 notes trending downward via a directional Markov walk (mirror of Climb), with each note quieter than the last and the final note longest at the softest dynamic. Reads as the whistle's gentle exhale — a downward sigh that occasionally pauses or briefly rises before continuing down.

ready

5. Lydian Gesture

F → B tritone · the Lydian color

Always plays F → B → (resolve to F), highlighting the augmented-fourth that defines F Lydian's fairytale character. Optionally returns to F at the end (50% chance). This is the gesture that says "Lydian" most directly — worth weighting fairly rare so it lands as a special moment.

ready

6. Bird Call

Rapid leaps · 1–3 clusters of 2–5 notes

1 – 3 clusters (weighted toward 1: ~60% one cluster, ~28% two, ~12% three) of 2 – 5 short notes (~0.2 s each) with leaping intervals of 3 – 5 scale degrees, alternating up and down. Each cluster resets to the starting degree. Brief silence (0.4 – 0.8 s) between clusters — like a real bird pausing between calls. Most "Ariel's Day Off" of the bunch.

ready

7. Trill

Rapid alternation · decorative

5 – 8 rapid alternations between two close notes (step apart 70% of the time, third 30%). Each ~0.12 s. Closes with a held note on the lower of the two for resolution. Ornamental in the way a bird or flute player decorates a phrase.

ready
