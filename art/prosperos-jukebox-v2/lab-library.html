Library sound lab

how this page works

The real engine, unmodified, plus three candidate instruments for the Library's empty register. Turn one on, let the evening run, and move the knobs while it plays — every knob is read at the moment the next note is scheduled.

You never have to wait. "SOUND ONE NOW" plays the instrument on the current chord immediately; presence ×3 makes it enter constantly; the hush chips leave the drone alone so you can hear the candidate naked. Reseeding and waiting for a reverie is not the plan.

Each card shows its last two decisions. Tap KNOBS ▾ to open one instrument's knobs; opening one folds the others away.

Stops — the same phrases in new clothes

A stop is a variant of a body the engine already owns. Switching one on mutes that engine layer and re-renders every note it plays through the variant here, at the engine's own times — its phrasing, new clothes. Switching it off unmutes the layer and stops the shadow. One stop per layer.

Notes arrive about a quarter-second before they sound, so the shadow lands where the engine's note would have. The notes a stop does not change are re-rendered through a copy of the engine's own body, so the layer keeps its phrases.

What a shadow cannot carry. The prototype pours into the bus through its own room, so it cannot reach the engine's private sends: shadowed plucks do not excite the halo, the shadowed music box does not feed the far-wall delay, and shadowed notes take the page's room instead of the scene's room blend. A shadowed cello or hum note also gets its own swell and vibrato instead of one per bow, and the cadence consort — one body for two or three parts in the engine — is re-rendered part by part. So judge the STOP's colour here, not the layer's whole place in the room: some of what you lose while a stop is on is the plumbing, not the stop.

Roster — who plays in which scene

A tick means "audible in that scene". Engine layers go through the engine's own mixer; the three candidates go through their entry gates. The grid is applied at every scene turn (and the current scene's column is lit).
