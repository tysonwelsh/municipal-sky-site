The Room — impulse-response prototype

Tier 1 of the sound rework, offered for approval before anything touches the engine. The voices below are faithful ports of the real Library patches from pj2-library.js — same oscillators, same envelopes, same formants. The only variable is the room. Room A is the IR generator pj2-voice.js ships today, transcribed verbatim. Room B is the proposed replacement. Both run in parallel and are always fed, so switching is instant and the comparison is honest — you can flip mid-tail.
Read this before trusting your ears. Everything here is v2. “A” is the IR generator pj2-voice.js ships today — but an IR on its own is not what the Library sounds like, so two caveats: (1) the real Library never runs one room. It blends roomClose and roomWide continuously (equal-power, balance 0.15), and much of its space comes from the far-wall delay and the sympathetic halo rather than the convolvers. Leave Engine chain ON to hear the apparatus the engine actually builds; switch it off only to isolate the convolver as a variable. (2) the engine never sets convolver.normalize, so it runs at the browser default true. The toggle below defaults to matching that. It is not cosmetic: normalization gives back much of the high-frequency energy room B removes on purpose, so the two settings are genuinely different experiments and only ON describes the engine.

3 · What the two rooms actually are

Impulse responses, left channel, amplitude on a dB scale so the tail is visible. A · current B · proposed ▪ early reflections — note A is maximally dense from sample 0 and keeps one spectral tilt for its whole life; B opens with discrete taps, ramps into diffusion, and darkens as it decays.

4 · What changed, and why

Frequency-dependent decay. Today's IR multiplies white noise by env × hf, both scalars. Scaling a noise sample attenuates every frequency equally — so the hf term is a second amplitude envelope, not the HF damping its name promises. The tail keeps one spectral tilt from onset to silence, which is the metallic-wash signature. B splits the tail into four bands with independent T60s (highs decaying 4× faster than lows) via a one-pole filter bank, so the room darkens as it dies, the way air and soft furnishings actually behave.

Early reflections. A real IR opens with sparse, discrete, loud taps — the first bounces off nearby surfaces — before diffusing. A has none. B places its taps on an irregular lattice inside the ER window, decorrelated L/R, with slightly randomized signs. These are what let the ear read size and shape rather than just "reverb".

Density ramp. Echo density in a real space builds roughly as t². A is fully dense at sample 0, which reads as a burst of noise with a decay on it. B gates its diffuse noise with a rising density function, so reflections thicken into the tail.

The mix. Independent of the IR: today every room is built at wet 0.30–0.35 with dry pinned at 1.0, so reverb only ever adds and the source stays fully present. Pull Dry level down to about 0.5 and push Wet up past 0.6 on either room to hear how much of the "web audio" character is mix rather than IR.

One caveat on judging: B is usually a touch quieter at matched wet, because banded decay removes real high-frequency energy. Louder reads as better in an A/B, so trust the character rather than the level, or nudge Wet up a hair on B before deciding.

Relative density counts samples above 15% of each window's own peak, so the amplitude envelope cancels and only sparseness shows. A is flat on every axis by construction — it has no mechanism that could vary with time.

5 · If you approve

The whole change lands inside PJ2.Voice.reverb in pj2-voice.js plus the six reverb({...}) spec objects in the three track files. No new dependencies, no assets, no architecture. The IR pour is already exempted from the determinism contract ("Unseeded Math.random is permitted HERE ONLY: it shapes texture, not music"), so REPRO cannot break — the harness compares event and note streams, and neither is touched. v1 stays frozen.
