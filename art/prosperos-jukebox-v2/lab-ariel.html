Ariel sound lab

how this page works

The real evening, unmodified, plus four candidate voices for the registers Ariel does not have — a plucked string, a reed, a struck bowl, a motor-driven bar. Turn one on, let the evening run, and move the knobs while it plays: every knob is read at the moment the next sound is scheduled.

You never have to wait. "SOUND ONE NOW" plays the voice on the current chord immediately; presence ×3 makes it enter constantly; the hush chips leave the breeze alone so you can hear a candidate naked against the pad.

The release owns the ending. Ariel's evening dissolves UPWARD, so nothing here may be louder or lower as it goes: the lyre's rolls climb an octave at x ≥ 0.6 and two at x ≥ 0.9 and thin as they climb; the concertina stops at x ≥ 0.4; the handpan and the vibraphone at x ≥ 0.5. Nothing is cut off at the boundary — whatever is still sounding ends by itself. The line under the meter says where the release is and who is still allowed to speak.

Nothing startles. The landscape-class voices (the concertina, and the breeze and aeolian shadows) keep their attacks and releases at a second or more. The melodic-class ones (the lyre, handpan, vibraphone, and the whistle / chime / flutter / bass shadows) may be fast — they are events, not weather.

The air is not ours. All four candidates are landscape-chordal: they sound under a whistle phrase at their own level and never claim PJ2.Air (the page only wraps it to capture the run).

Each card shows its last two decisions. Tap KNOBS ▾ to open one instrument's knobs; opening one folds the others away.

Stops — the same notes, a different instrument

the test a stop has to pass, and what a shadow cannot carry

A stop replaces a body the engine already owns. Switching one on mutes that engine layer and re-renders every note it plays through the variant here, at the engine's own times — its phrasing, its notes, its loudness, a different instrument. ENGINE puts the layer back.

The test. These are not shades. A listener who knows the layer should name a DIFFERENT INSTRUMENT within two seconds: blown becomes reed, bell becomes bronze, struck becomes bowed, tone becomes water, the day sky becomes the night sky. Each variant carries one line naming the change so you know what to listen for. What a stop never changes is WHAT and WHEN — the engine still decides that — or HOW LOUD: A and B are matched to the engine's own layer within ±1.5 dB on the same phrase.

Notes arrive about a quarter-second before they sound, so the shadow lands where the engine's note would have. Every card has PLAY A PHRASE NOW: play it under ENGINE, then A, then B, on a hushed bus, and you have the comparison in six seconds.

What a shadow cannot carry. The prototype pours into the bus through its own room, so it cannot reach the engine's private sends: a shadowed whistle, chime or flutter does not feed the two delay walls or the sympathetic halo, and every shadow takes the page's room instead of the scene's room blend (which in the release ramps to the sky). A shadow also cannot re-voice what the engine does not emit a note for — the breeze's hiss mist is texture, not a note, and stays gone while the breeze is shadowed. HALO has no card at all: it is an fx RETURN, excited by sends the page cannot reach. Judge the STOP's colour here, not the layer's whole place in the sky.
