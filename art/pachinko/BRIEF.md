# MOTHER LODE — creative brief (2026-09-26)

The third cabinet in the possum's arcade (see `../arcade/WORLD.md`), after
HOLLER ROLLER (skee ball) and SCRIP CREEK (coin pusher). A pachinko / plinko
machine whose board is a **miniature working coal mine in cross-section**.
This brief is art direction only; the technical plan comes after the owner
signs off on it. Decisions below come from the owner interview of 2026-09-26.

Folder: `art/pachinko/` (mechanism name, per house convention).

Read `../arcade/HEART.md` first: it's the why behind every call here.

---

## The one impossible thing

Behind the glass is a **model of a working mine, cut in section**, and the
figurines in it are alive. That's the whole conceit. Nobody says where the
cabinet came from: it was just there, one more machine in the possum's
building. The magical realism is never explained and never winks.

## What this cabinet is about

The **descent**: the first machine about what's under the arcade. The mine
is the unconscious. Its bottom holds both the hope (gold, a vein, a
restart, the past redeemed) and the fear (nothing; time only running
forward). The deeper sections should carry both: the vein really glints
and the dark is really dark. The lost things in the rock are a child's
junk drawer, buried: specific, useless, loved.

## Tone

**Same register as HOLLER ROLLER:** a deranged 1990s Nicktoon, dark in a fun
way. The machine has personality and occasionally cheats, and dread is a
seasoning, not the main course. The session should feel **hypnotic** (the
pachinko trance of watching the marble tick down the pins) with a **big
jackpot rush** as the payoff.

## Visual language: the company museum

The cabinet borrows the look of a mid-century **company museum or natural
history hall diorama**. The coal & land company's name is never legible,
but the institutional voice is everywhere. Use the whole kit:

- **Numbered legend.** Small numbered markers in the diorama, with a typed
  key card on the glass: *1. Main haulage way, 2. Ventilation door, 3. [scratched
  out]*. Payout slots are listed in the legend like exhibits.
- **Painted backdrop.** Above ground, the top of the section is a
  hand-painted mural of the ridge and night sky, slightly too idealised,
  with the moon in it.
- **Brass and placards.** Engraved plates, a typed exhibit card in a frame,
  PLEASE DO NOT TAP GLASS. The tone is dry and institutional, with odd details.
- **Scale and specimens.** A scale bar (*1 in = 40 ft*), specimen labels
  on things in the rock (*Fig. 7: pocket watch, still going*), and a
  figure of a man for scale.

Marquee: **MOTHER LODE**, loud arcade lettering. The museum's dry exhibit
voice lives on the plates and cards below it, not on the marquee.

## The diorama

**A cutaway cross-section, lit like a mine.** Layered strata in section,
mostly dark. Light comes only from carbide lamps and headlamps, the pools
around the knockers' work, coal seams that glint, and the marble catching
light as it passes. Palette: WORLD.md's night (blues, bruised purples,
black, fog grey) with lamp amber, plus pink and bone white as accents.

**What is in the rock** (specimen-labelled where it's funny):
- **Fossils and old bones:** ferns, trilobites, something big's ribs deep
  down. The Carboniferous swamp the coal came from.
- **Lost things:** keys, a doll's arm, a wedding ring, a lunch pail, a
  pocket watch still ticking. Some may become prizes later.
- **The company's secrets:** ledgers, a strongbox, a sealed payroll, the
  company plaque with its name scratched out.

Tunnels, timbering, rail, carts and shafts are cut into the strata. The
pins of the pachinko board live inside this world: rail spikes, props,
lamp hooks, ore. They aren't an abstract grid laid over it.

## The figurines: tommyknockers

**Diorama toys of little old miners, and they shouldn't be alive.** They're
painted figurines, about 20 px tall: bearded, miner's caps with candle
lamps, leather aprons, arms slightly too long. They are clearly models,
with painted faces and peg joints, but they move with intent.

What they do:
- **They build the board.** Between games you watch them rework it: move
  a few pins, lay a length of track, prop a tunnel. The board **drifts a
  little every game**, so your read of it never quite settles. Attract
  mode is them at work.
- **They steal.** Sometimes a knocker grabs the marble mid-fall and carries
  it off down a side tunnel. It might come out somewhere good or somewhere
  bad. This is the machine's cheating.

## How it plays

- **Framing: the cabinet, then a dive.** Attract mode shows the whole
  cabinet head-on (marquee, glass, museum plates), the same way HOLLER
  ROLLER does. When you start a game, the view pushes in through the glass
  until the diorama fills the portrait screen.
- **Skill: drop position.** Slide the hopper along the top and tap to
  drop. It's plinko-style and readable with one thumb. Reading the board,
  which keeps drifting, is the skill.
- **The ball: a glass marble,** a cat's-eye. It refracts light and shows
  the mine upside-down inside it as it falls.
- **Payout: scrip,** like skee ball. Tokens go in and pink scrip comes out.
- **Sound: pachinko clatter.** Bright glass-on-pin ticks, a chiming tune
  and the marble's roll, like a pachinko parlour reimagined as a mine.
  Under it, the house sounds (HEART.md): the electrical hum, scrip and
  coins hitting a plastic bucket, and now and then a **distant train
  whistle**. The small-win shift whistle should rhyme with that train.
- **Air:** the purple-black night around the cabinet; nicotine-yellowed
  placards and a tinted glass front (stale smoke, evoked, never shown).

## Events and mischief

The same rule as HOLLER ROLLER: mostly skill, some telegraphed mischief.
The player should blame the machine, not the input.

| Event | What happens | Cheating? |
|---|---|---|
| **Knocker theft** | A figurine snatches the marble mid-fall and hauls it off down a side tunnel; it reappears elsewhere. | yes |
| **Lamps go out** | A section of the mine goes dark; the marble falls through unseen, and you only hear it. Sometimes it never comes out. | the vanishing is |
| **Cave-in** | Knocking first, then a tunnel collapses: pins shift or a slot is blocked with rubble for the rest of the game. | no, it's an event |

## Jackpot tiers

1. **Small wins:** the shift whistle blows and the tunnels light up.
2. **The jackpot:** the marble finds the **13** and the knockers strike the
   **mother lode**. The section cracks open to a glittering vein, every
   lamp flares, carts race and scrip pours out.
3. **The rare tier:** **a placeholder only.** Something that advances the
   larger adventure game, probably by handing the player a story object.
   The trigger and the reward are deferred. HEART.md suggests its
   meaning: the first hint of **a restart** at the bottom of the mine. Build the hook (a tier, a
   flag, an empty reward slot) so it can be filled later.

## Deferred (not in the first build)

- **The pit mule,** the planned cabinet critter. Its role in the game
  is unresolved. The cabinet can have a spot for it; build nothing that
  depends on it.
- **Offerings and feeding the knockers.** Set aside until the core game is
  great to play.
- **Rising water:** cut, not worth the effort.
- **The rare jackpot's content** (see above).
- Where the cabinet came from: left unsaid on purpose.

## The bar for the first build

A game that is **really fun to play, creative, and dense with visual
detail**. The diorama should reward staring at it. Get the drop, the
clatter, the drifting board and the knockers right before adding anything
else.
