Junk Drawer · Mockup 15C · The Pneumatic Tube

Round 15 rebuilds the seven turn cards under one fiction: the bureau runs on pneumatic tubes, and a turn is a dispatch through the building. You write a slip on Form 27-T; it rolls into brass carrier no. 4417 and the latch throws. The capsule goes down the west trunk, splits at the diverter into LINE A and LINE B, and the route board watches it pass. It comes back with two slips in it. You grade them at the receiving desk, set the diverter with the call, and the winner is stamped in at Hatch 3. When a line fails, the capsule comes back unopened.

Design notes

The fiction, and its rules

One sentence: a turn is a dispatch through the building. Everything else follows from it, and the vocabulary is fixed so it can be reused without re-explaining:

  • THE SLIP — what the visitor writes. Form 27-T. It is paper: it can be torn, ruled, stamped, and it can carry a red margin.
  • THE CAPSULE — brass carrier no. 4417, felt-gasketed. Its number is minted at SEND IT and printed on every card afterwards, which is what makes the seven cards feel like one errand rather than seven screens. (Integration: reuse the existing client_ref — take a short hash of it. Never invent a second identifier.)
  • THE LINE — a tube run. There are exactly two past the diverter, LINE A and LINE B, which is the app’s existing A/B with no meaning added or lost. Slot letters never change.
  • THE STATIONS — ST 1 sending head, ST 2 trunk riser, ST 3 the diverter, ST 4A/4B the drafting rooms, ST 5 return header, ST 6 your desk. Six numbers, one per real thing the backend actually does. Nothing on the board is invented traffic.
  • THE BOARD — the mimic panel that watches the capsule. The Darkroom, and the only lit machine in the flow.
  • THE DESK — the receiving desk, where grading happens. Paper, ink, brass. No lamps.
  • THE HATCH — Hatch 3, Accessions. Where the winner is stamped into the drawer.

Why amber, when the button outside is blue

The arcade button in the pile glows #85d2ff/#1f8ee4: it is the thing you press, and its blue is a lens lit from behind. The board is the building telling you what it is doing, and its amber (#ffb43a lamp, #ffe7ad hot core, #7d4604 in the bore) is signalling, not invitation. Keeping them different colours means the visitor never mistakes a status light for something to push — which matters most on the Darkroom, the one card with no controls at all. The amber also sits inside the drawer’s existing warm family, so the panel reads as part of the same building as the manila, not as a second design.

How progress reads

  • Position on the route, not a percentage. A generation has no honest progress fraction — it finishes when it finishes. What the board shows is where the capsule is, which is knowable and true: past ST 3, in the rooms, not yet at ST 5.
  • Three signals per station, never colour alone. Dark lens + dim legend (not yet), lit lens + spill + bright legend (passed), lit + ring + travelling highlight on its bore (current), cold lens + red ✕ + red legend (obstructed).
  • The map is decoration; the annunciator is the truth. The SVG is aria-hidden; the two-line list beneath it is the aria-live="polite" region and carries the same three words the live slotLine() already produces. A screen reader hears “line A — arrived / line B — drawing”, which is exactly what ships today.
  • The rail is the route again, in ink. The Bench’s step rail keeps the live component’s affordances (three real buttons, 44px, disabled until reached, red ground + red border + 3px inset bar on the current one) and gains the connectors and lamps, so the reader recognises it as the same diagram as the board. Progress reads the same way in both places.
  • The call is a diverter position. Five stops, A hard left to B hard right — unchanged geometry — but the chosen stop grows a spur and a plain-words readout (“diverter set LINE B — narrowly”). The likert’s weakness has always been that a dot on a rail is easy to mis-set and hard to confirm; the readout is the confirmation, and it costs one line.

Motion, and its absence

Two animations, both inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference): the travelling highlight on a running bore, and a 2.2s breath on the current station’s spill and its annunciator lamp. The highlight is a stroke-dasharray: 5 95 chase on a path carrying pathLength="100", so a single keyframe (5 → −95) is correct on every segment regardless of its real length — no per-path JS measurement, no getTotalLength(). Under reduce the running bore becomes a solid amber line at 0.6 and the current spill sits at a fixed 0.62: the reduced state is a legible still of the same instrument, not a blank one. There is no motion anywhere else in the flow — the desk, the hatch and the notice are all static paper.

Functional parity — what did not change

  • Textarea + 500-char count + is-over state; SEND IT disabled until non-empty and in budget; the honeypot still sits off-screen at left:-9999px.
  • The disclosure is the canonical JD_CONSENT.text, word for word, still fine print, still under a hairline. Only the rubric above it is new.
  • Bench: native <select> per axis (phone OS pickers intact, font-size:16px under 600px to kill iOS focus zoom), skip as the honest default, one ⓘ per row driving an in-flow popover, the hover/focus singleton tooltip unchanged, the “broken or offensive” checkbox with its folded note.
  • The call is still winner + margin from five stops; a tie still has no margin and still routes to the keep-chooser on the unveil.
  • Every control ≥ 44px. Every selected/current state carries at least two signals.
  • The scrim still reserves the 56px banner (padding-top: max(12px, 2vh, calc(var(--banner-h) + 8px))); the card is still width: min(680px, 100%), max-height: 100%, border-box, growing downward with internal scroll only when the content genuinely exceeds the reserved viewport.
  • Visitor content — the prompt — is escaped text in a textarea. Every piece of the fiction (capsule, board, stamps, hatch) is CSS or inline SVG the visitor cannot reach.

Integration watch-outs

  • The board is the only new tall thing. At 680px the wide map is 640 × 200 and the whole board lands about 330px — comfortably inside the reserved viewport even at 1280 × 680. The portrait map is 300 × 380 and is what makes the phone case fit; check both when the copy above it changes length. If the board ever has to compress, compress it (it is the soft part), never the annunciator.
  • Two maps means two DOM trees. They are show/hide, not re-render, so the station state classes must be written to both. Cheapest correct shape: render both once from the same state object and toggle by media query — do not try to swap innerHTML on resize.
  • SVG ids are document-global. The gradients (ptBezelG, ptGlowG, ptBrassG, ptBoreG, ptBrassRing, ptWoodG) live in one hidden defs block and every board instance references them. That is deliberate — but it means these ids must be namespaced away from JD_svgInst’s per-copy prefixing, which rewrites ids inside visitor artwork. Keep the pt prefix and keep them out of any SVG that passes through JD_svgInst.
  • The capsule number needs a source. Derive it from the existing client_ref (e.g. last four hex digits, uppercased) so it survives a reload and a recovery re-send. Do not mint a second id, and do not show a server id the client may never have received.
  • Slot failure is not the same as turn failure. The board must be able to show ok + dead at once and hand off to the one-survivor Reveal, which drops the rail entirely and files after one panel. The board state function here takes the two slot statuses independently for exactly that reason.
  • The perforation, the curl and the spike are pseudo-elements. .pt-slip::before, .jd-turn-art::before/::after and .pt-spike all sit above the artwork box; none of them take pointer events. The slip perforation paints in var(--tpaper), so if the card ground is ever changed the scallops go with it — that colour is load-bearing, not decorative.
  • The UNSEALED and ACCESSIONED stamps use mix-blend-mode: multiply so they sink into the paper. On a ground that is not the manila token they will look printed rather than stamped — keep them on --tpaper/--tpaper-hi only.
  • The small-screen rules are @container in this file only. The scrim carries container-type: inline-size so a 375px demo frame and a 1280px one can share one stylesheet on one page. Ship them as the @media (max-width: 600px) / (max-width: 768px) blocks the live file already has — a real phone's viewport and the scrim's inline size are the same number there, so every rule transfers unchanged.
  • Copy that is contractual stays verbatim: the disclosure, “this response is broken or offensive”, and the abandon confirm. Everything else on these cards is new wording and the owner should read it as such.
  • Demo-only JavaScript: none. There is no <script> in this file at all — every state shown above is a separate statically-rendered instance, so what you see is what the CSS actually does.

What I would cut if it has to get smaller

In order: the loading dock on the Brief (the capsule is established well enough by the board), then the receiving-head strip on the Reveal, then the connectors on the Bench rail. The board itself, the station band, and the diverter readout are the fiction — cut any of those three and this becomes a manila card with a picture of a pipe on it.
