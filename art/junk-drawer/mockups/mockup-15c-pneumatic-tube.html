Design notes

What this pass cut

The first 15c pass wrapped every card in the dispatch fiction: a station band across the top of all seven (DISPATCH DESK · CAPSULE 4417 · UNSEALED), a Form 27-T rubric on the slip, a brass loading dock, a receiving mouth with the capsule in it, a route sheet with an UNSEALED rubber stamp, a brass Hatch 3, obstruction strips, an annunciator list, a pressure gauge, a knurled-button caption under every button, and a paragraph of instruction on every card. All of it is gone.

What is left is the thing the visitor needs in order to act, plus one 15px brass tube-mouth beside each heading. Three physical marks survive because they cost no vertical space and are made of nothing but CSS: the perforation the slip was torn along, the red margin rule it is written between, and the knurl on the two buttons that seal a capsule (SEND IT, FILE THE GRADES). The drawings keep the shadow curl of paper that has been rolled.

The board, and why it stayed

It is the only place in the flow where the fiction does work the plain version cannot. The live loading card says response A — still drawing… twice; the board says the same thing but shows two lines actually in motion, which is the difference between being told to wait and watching something happen. It is also the only lit thing in the whole flow, the way the arcade button is the only lit thing on the drawer page outside it — amber inside the manila, blue out on the wood.

So it now gets the whole card and nothing else: heading, board, one line (a minute or two, leave this open). Inside the bezel there is one status line carrying both slots — A DRAWING · B DRAWING — and that line, not the map, is the aria-live region. The map is aria-hidden decoration; a screen reader hears exactly what the live card reads today.

How the board reads

  • Stations are lamps in machined bezels. Dark = not reached. Amber = passed. Hot amber + dashed ring + breathing glow = the capsule is there now.
  • Lines are casing over bore. A dark bore is cold; a warm bore is energised; a bright dash chasing the path is the capsule travelling it.
  • A failure reads three ways at once, never colour alone: the lens goes maroon, a red ✕ is struck through the lamp, the bore breaks into a red dashed run, and the status line spells obstructed.
  • Room A / Room B get plates around their lamps so the fork reads as two departments rather than two more elbows. This is the only place in the flow that says out loud that two different machines are drawing.

Motion

Three animations, all inside the board: the chasing dash (pathLength="100" + stroke-dashoffset, so one keyframe is correct for every segment whatever its true length), the breathing glow on the current station, the pulsing status lamp. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the dash becomes a solid amber bore, the glow parks at 0.62, the lamp holds — the same information, still.

Functional parity

  • Brief — textarea, live 500-character count, SEND IT (disabled while empty: see 1B), the third-party disclosure verbatim as fine print.
  • Darkroom — per-slot run / ok / dead, plus the slow-turn line (2B). Same three states slotLine() reports.
  • Reveal — both plates, blind, one button to the survey.
  • Bench — a real clickable step rail (A / B / CALL, disabled until reached, current one red with an inset bar), the pinned artwork, native <select> per axis so phones get their OS picker, the ⓘ popover in flow, the broken-or-offensive flag with its note, back/next.
  • Call — five stops on one rail, A-left to B-right, tie in the middle, filed as winner + margin; back and file.
  • Unveil — models revealed, the kept one marked, the tie's keep-chooser (6B), take another turn / done.
  • Apology — generation failed, rate-limited with its countdown (7B), filing failed (7C), each with its own retry path.

Integration watch-outs

  • The ⓘ popover now renders one line — the axis's description from taxonomy.json. The per-level <dl> loop in scaleRow() comes out; the level labels still reach the visitor, because they are the <option> text in the select.
  • The board is one component with two SVGs (wide, tall) sharing one class vocabulary; the renderer sets four segment classes and seven station classes from the same slot state it already has. Both maps are in the DOM and CSS picks one — no JS branch on width.
  • The status line must stay a single element for aria-live; repaint it by replacing its text, exactly as paintSlots() does today.
  • Every card here fits the reserved 800px viewport without internal scrolling — the bench, which was the tallest, now lands about 150px shorter than the live one. Internal scroll still works and is still the only overflow path.
  • The mockup's small-screen rules are @container so one page can show 1280 and 375 side by side. They ship as ordinary @media (max-width: 600px).
  • Demo-state toggles: there are none. Every card on this page is static HTML in a fixed state, and the page carries no script at all.

If it still has to get smaller

The next things to go, in order: the brass spike on the pinned plate; the slip's perforation; the tube-mouth badge on the headings (which would leave the fiction living only on the Darkroom, and honestly the board can carry it alone).
