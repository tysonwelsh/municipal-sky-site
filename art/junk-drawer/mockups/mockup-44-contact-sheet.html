The Contact Sheet

mockup 44 · replay as a filmstrip under the photograph · 2026-09-16

The replay leaves the photograph and becomes a contact sheet printed beneath it: eight small frames of the same drawing at successive moments of the draw-on, each labelled with how many elements have been begun by then — so the strip reads the stacking order at a glance (the leaves are all down by frame 3; the pot arrives in frame 4 and buries them). PLAY / PAUSE sits at the strip’s left end, a sketched ▷ in the pagers’ own box. Click a frame to jump the photograph to that moment; the ruled scrubber under the frames reaches every moment between them — drag, click, or arrow one element at a time — and the readout says which element the pen is on. The frames cost DOM: eight paused copies of the drawing (fine at 31 elements, a real bill at 600 — see the notes).

A · The report card

The Kimi K3 succulent — leaves drawn correctly, then buried behind the pot. The REPLAY icon has left the button row on the photograph; DOWNLOAD SVG keeps the corner.

Notes for the build

  1. A shared module, jd-core.js: JD_sheet(svg, mount, opts) returning {seek, play, pause, destroy}. It arms the drawing once with JD_drawOn(svg, {force:true}), then defeats the strip timer by setting svg.__jdDrawSeq to a value no run will ever stamp, so the inline animations live as long as the sheet does; svg.getAnimations({subtree:true}) is its handle on every element. Seek = pause + currentTime on all; play = play() on the animations whose end is still ahead (a finished one must stay paused, or play() auto-rewinds it to zero and the element vanishes mid-run). The element index comes from each animation’s effect.getTiming().delay, sorted in DOM order — the same order the engine scheduled. destroy() calls the engine’s strip so the DOM goes back to plain when the card closes.
  2. jd-record.js: plateBtnsHTML drops the .rc-draw button (the enlargement keeps its REDRAW word, or takes the sheet too — owner’s call); cardHTML appends the sheet’s mount after .rc-plate inside .rc-col-l, which in the ≤700px portrait flow puts it under the photograph on its own. The card already draws on open; that run becomes the sheet’s first play. The response flip and paper swap must call destroy() then re-mount on the new svg.
  3. jd-turn.js: the bench and reveal plates drop .jd-turn-draw--icon and mount the sheet under figure.jd-turn-plate’s caption. Its data-act="replay" handler becomes the sheet’s play.
  4. junk-drawer.css: the .mk-* rules on this page, renamed .jd-sheet-*, placed beside the alternatives strip. The frames reuse .rc-alt-port / .rc-alt / .rc-alt-art / .rc-alt-cap / .is-cur verbatim and PLAY reuses .rc-alt-nav; those rules read --form-rule(-2), --form, --red, --rc-mono, which the turn card maps from --trule, --tform, --tred, --tmono on its scrim (the .mk-turn block here) — the same trick .jd-turn already plays with --card.
  5. Cost. The frames are real paused copies: 8 × (elements + 1–2 CSSAnimations each). This specimen is 31 elements (≈8 ms to build the sheet on this machine, printed in the console); a 600-element drawing means ~4,800 extra nodes and ~7,000 paused animations per card — still idle once paused (a paused animation costs no frame time), but the build spike and memory are real on a phone. Mitigations, in order: build the frames lazily one per animation frame after the first play starts; six frames under 480px instead of eight; and, if a monster shows up, rasterise each frame to a canvas once its clone is seeked (drawImage of a serialised clone with the animated properties written into attributes) and drop the clone.
  6. Frame moments are placed at equal ELEMENT counts, not equal time (the engine packs starts by √length, so equal-time frames would bunch the little strokes): frame k sits halfway between element ck beginning and ck+1 beginning, with ck = k·M/7. The frame ticks on the ruler are therefore unevenly spaced, which is itself a reading of the pacing. “Element N of M” means the Nth element in document order has begun — elements 1…N−1 are beneath it in the stack.
  7. Open questions. (1) Is “begun” the right count, or should the label be “completed”? Begun matches what the eye sees start to appear; completed matches what is fully painted over. (2) Should the frames be evenly spaced in time instead, for a truer film feel? (3) Eight frames at ~45px under a 420px plate: legible for this specimen, tight for fine line-work; six is the alternative. (4) Should the enlargement carry the sheet too (it has the room, and the enlargement is where analysis happens)?
