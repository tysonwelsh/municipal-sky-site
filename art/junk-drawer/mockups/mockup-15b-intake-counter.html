15B — The Intake Counter

Three materials, quietly — brass for the building's hardware, enamel for what the machine says, paper for the transaction. Nothing else is on a card but the thing you act on.

Design notes — 15B, The Intake Counter (rev. 2)

What rev. 2 cut, and why

Rev. 1 lost the round for being designed for the designer. The worst offender was the claim-ticket strip riding every card: a four-position progress tracker that told the visitor where they were in a flow they were already walking through one screen at a time. It has been deleted. A single punched stub survives on the Unveil, where it is a receipt for a finished transaction, not a map of an unfinished one. Also gone: every eyebrow rubric, the level-by-level definition lists in the ⓘ popovers, the “clerk” and “the counter has served all it can” voice-of-the-building copy, the wall-clock board, the form JD‑1 header on the slip, and every hint line beside a button.

The test each card now has to pass: can the visitor act with only what is on the screen? Anything that was true but not needed came off.

The fiction, whispering

The card is still a service window — brass wicket, a dark slot under it, counter surface below — but the wicket is now 44 px of slim band rather than 54 px of stage set, and it carries only two things: the engraved window plate and the enamel number. Three materials still do all the work: brass for the building's hardware (wicket, buttons, likert track, clip, grommet), enamel for what the machine says (the number, the Darkroom's chip), paper for the transaction (slip, envelopes, assessment lines, the stub, the tag). The Apology's placard is painted steel — the one exception, because it belongs to the building shutting the visitor out.

  • The queue number is the whole ticket fiction now. № 0412 is lit in the wicket on every card, appears once more on the Darkroom's enamel chip, and is punched out on the Unveil's stub. Three appearances, no progress semantics.
  • Choices are still punched. The likert's five stops are bores in a brass track and the answer is a red peg seated in one; the rail's step numerals are punch circles. That vocabulary survived because it is doing interaction work, not narration.
  • The Darkroom's clock shrank to a 17 px chip beside the heading. It costs no row on the card, keeps the machine's voice, and the second hand still turns.

How progress reads, without a tracker

From the heading and the controls, which is where a user looks anyway: Describe an object → Two machines are drawing… → Two drawings came back → Grade what came back (with the three-step rail saying which of A / B / the call you are on) → Who drew what. The rail is the only progress control left, it is local to grading, and it stays clickable and focusable with future steps disabled — exactly as production has it.

Functional parity — nothing was cut but copy

  • Brief: textarea, live 500-char count, SEND IT, the disclosure as fine print. The heading is the only instruction.
  • Darkroom: per-slot pending / ok / failed and the slow-turn notice (toggle above the stage).
  • Bench: the rail as a real control; native <select>s for OS pickers; the ⓘ popover in flow, one line, covering nothing; 44 px targets; the broken-or-offensive flag and its folded note.
  • Call: five stops, A hard left, B hard right, tie dead centre, filed as winner + margin. Selection is never colour alone — peg vs. bore is a shape change, the caption goes bold, the chosen end-tab flips red.
  • Unveil: names, the placement line, the tie keep-chooser (A / B / neither), take another turn beside done, and the stub.
  • Apology: all three failures, all three retry paths.
  • Geometry: width: min(680px, 100%), max-height: 100% in a scrim reserving the 56 px banner, border-box, internal scroll on the counter surface only. See the clamp stage.

Integration watch-outs

  • The close ✕ lives in the wicket as a real 44×40 brass key, so the title uses the full width and production's 52 px title padding is unnecessary. Keep the scrim's banner reservation regardless.
  • The ruled textarea's rules are a background gradient and do not scroll with the text in Blink. Two things keep it honest: a --lh custom property the phone query bumps to 25 px beside the 16 px font (the iOS focus-zoom guard), and padding-bottom: 0 — a scrolling textarea paints overflow text inside its padding box, which otherwise leaves a half-cut line sitting under the last rule. Dropping the gradient costs nothing structural if the owner would rather not carry it.
  • Nothing is sticky but the bench's pinned print. With the ticket strip gone there is no second candidate, which removes rev. 1's stacked-sticky hazard entirely.
  • № 0412 must be one value. Render it once from the turn record and pass it to the wicket, the Darkroom chip and the stub; if they can disagree the conceit collapses.
  • Envelopes cost a wrapper, not a rewrite. .ic-env adds a flap and a foot around production's existing plate markup; the plate (aspect-ratio: 1/1, #f6efdc, inset shadow) is unchanged, so the SVG instancing path needs no edit.
  • Escaped-text discipline holds. The only visitor content is the prompt, rendered as text in the textarea. Every bar, hole, chain, clip, stamp and clock is CSS or inline SVG.
  • Motion is additive. The clock hand and the pending lamp live inside prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference; the base state is the reduced state.

What this page's script does (and only this)

Clones the seven authored desktop cards into the 375 px rail with ids re-suffixed so nothing collides; copies the Call card into the clamp stage; drives the demo-state buttons above the Darkroom, Bench and Unveil stages; keeps the Brief's character count live. Nothing in the design depends on it.
