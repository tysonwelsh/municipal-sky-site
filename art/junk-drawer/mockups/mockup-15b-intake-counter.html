15B — The Intake Counter

Every card is a transaction at the bureau's service counter. The modal is a window: a brass wicket across the top, a dark slot beneath it where paper passes, and the counter surface below where this transaction's paperwork sits. The arcade button outside is coin-op hardware — this carries the hardware inward and gives it a clerk. Three materials do all the work:

BRASS — the building's hardware: wicket, plates, buttons, the likert track ENAMEL — what the machine says: the queue window, NOW PROCESSING PAPER — the transaction: slip, ticket, envelopes, assessment lines, tag

Progress is one punched claim ticket, carried under the wicket on every card: № 0412, four punch positions (taken / drawn / graded / filed) and a rubber stamp saying where the transaction stands. Choices are punched too — the likert's stops are bores that take a red peg. Demo-state toggles sit above each stage, never inside the card; the only script on this page clones the seven authored cards into the 375 px rail, drives those toggles, and counts the Brief's characters.

Design notes — 15B, The Intake Counter

The fiction and its rules

The visitor is standing on the public side of a service window at the bureau. The card is not a panel floating in a scrim — it is the window: brass wicket, a dark slot under it, counter surface below. That single architectural idea is what makes seven very different states read as one place, and it is enforced by three hard rules.

  1. Three materials, three jobs. Brass is the building's hardware and only the building's: the wicket, the engraved window plate, the action buttons, the likert track, the bulldog clip, the tag's grommet. Enamel (dark lacquer, lit type) is reserved for what the machine says, never the clerk: the queue number in the wicket and the NOW PROCESSING board. Paper is the transaction: the request slip, the claim ticket, the envelopes, the assessment lines, the folded complaint note, the specimen tag. Nothing on any card is allowed to be a fourth material. The one deliberate exception is the Apology's placard, which is painted steel — and it is the exception precisely because it belongs to the building shutting the visitor out.
  2. Progress is one punched ticket. Ticket № 0412 sits under the wicket on every card, always in the same place, always the same object: perforated left edge, the number, four punch positions (taken / drawn / graded / filed), and a rubber stamp on the right end naming the current state. Punched holes are real holes — dark bore, lit paper edge. The next position wears a red ring and breathes. That is the whole global progress read; nothing else on any card is allowed to duplicate it.
  3. Choices are punched. The likert's five stops are bores in a brass track, and the visitor's answer is a red peg seated in one. The bench rail's step numerals are punch circles too (dashed = not yet, solid = been there, red = here). The vocabulary of "a hole made in paper by a machine" does double duty as both progress and choice, which is why the design never needs a second selection idiom.

How progress reads, card by card

  • Brief — no punches; stamp AT THE WINDOW; the "taken" hole is the one breathing.
  • Darkroom — taken punched; stamp IN PROCESS; the enamel board repeats the number so the machine and the ticket visibly agree.
  • Reveal — taken, drawn; stamp RETURNED.
  • Bench — same two punches; stamp UNDER EXAMINATION. The bench's own three-step rail is local — it never touches the ticket, so there is no ambiguity about which control means "where am I in the turn" and which means "which response am I on".
  • Call — stamp changes to AWAITING THE CALL; the rail's first two chits are marked done.
  • Unveil — all four punched; stamp FILED; the names are printed as the ticket's endorsement.
  • Apology — the stamp is the honest one: VOID — NO CHARGE when the generation died, STILL GOOD when it is only a rate limit (the ticket holds your place), NOT FILED when the filing jammed and the work is still on the counter.

Functional parity — what carried over unchanged

  • Brief: textarea, live 500-char count, SEND IT, and the disclosure verbatim as fine print (re-framed as "printed on the reverse of this form", which is what fine print is).
  • Darkroom: per-slot pending / ok / failed, plus the slow-turn notice, all as separate states of the same docket row (toggleable above the stage).
  • Bench: the rail stays a real, focusable, clickable control with disabled future steps; selects stay native <select> (OS pickers on phones); the ⓘ popover is in flow — it pushes the sheet down and never covers a control; every target clears 44 px; the flag checkbox and its folded note are intact.
  • Call: five stops, A hard left, B hard right, tie dead centre, filed as winner + margin. Selection is never colour alone — the peg is a different shape from a bore, the caption goes bold and ink-dark, and the chosen end-tab flips to red.
  • Unveil: names, the placement copy, the tie keep-chooser (A / B / neither), plus take another turn beside done.
  • Card geometry: width: min(680px, 100%), max-height: 100% in a scrim that reserves the 56 px banner, border-box, growing downward with internal scroll only on the counter surface. See the clamp stage above.

Integration watch-outs

  • The close ✕ moves into the wicket. Production pins it absolutely at top-right and pads the title 52 px to clear it. Here it is a real 44×44 brass key inside the band, so the title is free to use the full width and the long-standing "the ✕ is under the site banner" hazard cannot recur — the band is the topmost thing in the card and the scrim already reserves the banner. Worth keeping the scrim's padding-top reservation anyway; belt and braces.
  • The ruled textarea. The slip's rules are a background gradient on the textarea and the background does not scroll with the text in Blink. Mitigated by rows="5" and by tying the rule pitch to a --lh custom property that the phone query bumps to 26 px alongside the 16 px font (the iOS focus-zoom guard). If the owner would rather not carry that fragility, drop the gradient and keep the slip header/footer — the object still reads as a form.
  • The ticket is paper, so it scrolls. It is tempting to make .ic-ticket sticky so the progress read never leaves the frame on a tall card — but the bench already spends its position: sticky; top: 0 on the pinned print, and stacking two stickies means hard-coding the ticket's height into the pin's offset, which breaks the moment the ticket wraps on a narrow phone. The call here is: the wicket is the building and stays; everything made of paper scrolls. If the owner wants the ticket pinned, pin it and give the bench's print a plain (non-sticky) treatment — not both.
  • The ticket's punch labels are decorative at 375. They collapse to bare holes below a 600 px card, which is fine visually but means the <ol> must carry an aria-label naming the punched state (it does here) rather than relying on the words.
  • The wicket is 54 px of fixed chrome (48 on a phone) on top of the lip. On a short landscape phone that is real estate the counter surface loses. The band already shrinks in the container query; if landscape gets tight, the honest next move is to drop the engraved plate's “Specimen Intake” half rather than shrink the 44 px close key.
  • The queue number is fiction that has to be real. № 0412 must come from the turn record (any stable per-turn integer will do) and be identical in the wicket, on the ticket, and on the enamel board. If they can ever disagree, the whole conceit collapses — better to render the number once and pass it three times than to derive it twice.
  • Envelopes cost a wrapper. The Reveal's .ic-env adds a flap and a foot around production's existing plate markup; the plate itself (aspect-ratio: 1/1, #f6efdc, inset shadow) is unchanged, so the SVG instancing path (JD_svgInst, namespaced ids) needs no edit at all.
  • Escaped-text discipline holds. The only visitor content on these cards is the prompt, and it renders as plain text inside the textarea (note the typographic apostrophes surviving intact). Every wicket bar, punch hole, screw, chain, clip, stamp and clock is CSS or inline SVG — there is no path by which visitor words become decoration.
  • Motion is additive. The clock's second hand, the pending lamp's pulse and the next-punch ring all live inside prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference; the base state is the reduced state, matching the existing bench rules.

What this page's script does (and only this)

Clones the seven authored desktop cards into the 375 px rail with ids re-suffixed so nothing collides; copies the Call card into the clamp stage; drives the demo-state buttons above the Darkroom, Bench and Unveil stages; keeps the Brief's character count live. Nothing in the design depends on it.
