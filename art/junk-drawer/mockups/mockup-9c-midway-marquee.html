Junk Drawer · Mockup 9C · The Midway Marquee

9C — THE MIDWAY MARQUEE. The deliberately different take: where 9A and 9B recreate real arcade hardware, this is carnival — a landscape lozenge in a near-black housing, fifteen amber marquee bulbs running a slow chase around a warm ivory backlit lens with TAKE A TURN printed in dark ink on the glass. The drawer’s only light source, and the most theatrical “press me, this is a game” of the three.

1 · Hero — press it

click / tap, or focus and hit Enter / Space
idle: the bulb chase steps once a second; the lens breathes every ~6 s

 

the press — the lens sinks a visible step into the housing (travel plus a slight squash; the dark shaft shows around it), flares white, and every bulb snaps on together for a beat — the chase interrupts, then resumes · the halo swells with it · under reduced motion the same press is an instant sunk pose and the marquee holds frozen at full — all bulbs lit, no chase, no breath

2 · In the drawer — real floor, real neighbours, the real corner seat

honest scale, honest seat — floor, craquelure, frame, wall shade, varnish and vignette are the production values copied out of junk-drawer.css; the paperclip (xs × 0.8), brass key (s) and three of hearts (m) are the real items/ SVGs, sized by the real tier math in cqmin of the well · the marquee sits at the production trigger tier, m × 1.15 (box 17.83 cqmin; the 300×210 aspect normalizes to --w: 21.3, 21.3 × 14.9 cqmin), in the FIXED bottom-left corner seat, dead straight at 0°, constant z · it presses here too: rendered right now at —

the read test — at the production doorbell’s measured scale (1 cqmin ≈ 3.64 px on a 375 px phone: 58 × 72 px for its 240×300 box) this landscape box lands at ≈ 78 × 54 px — both sides clear the 44 px floor, the long side generously · below ~78 px the label goes decorative and the GLOW plus the chase carry the affordance, which is the whole thesis

3 · Lens tints — one custom-property swap, all fully lit

cheap variants — seven custom properties (--mq-l0..3 for the lens, --mq-h for the halo, --mq-g0/g1 for the glow core) resolved by stop classes, exactly the db-b0..4 pattern from turn-object.svg · the housing, the ink and the fifteen amber bulbs never change — a marquee is warm-white-and-gold whatever the show · ivory is the recommendation: an old theatre lamp, deliberately not red, so nothing else in the drawer can be mistaken for it

Notes · what I committed to, what implementation must watch

1 · First landscape candidate, built for the corner. The production seat (2026-08-10 revision) is fixed bottom-left, dead straight at 0°, so this plate is drawn landscape (300×210, 10:7) to lie along the bottom wall the way a marquee lies over a door. At 0° the corner reservation’s rotated-bbox math degenerates to the plain box, so the swap is only a footprint change: m × 1.15 normalizes to 21.3 × 14.9 cqmin (long side well under the 1.8 × box cap), ≈ 78 × 54 px on a 375 px phone against the doorbell’s 58 × 72.

2 · The halo is ink — and therefore tap target. The spill gradient is part of the SVG (an ellipse reaching within ~1% of every viewBox edge, fading to fully transparent), painted FIRST so the housing, shadow and bulbs sit on top of it. Under silhouette hit-testing (visiblePainted) that faint ink takes hits: a tap on the glow presses the button. Accepted on purpose — a light switch you can slap at. The housing itself still spans 83% × 71% of the box, so the drawer’s area-normalized size math is not fooled by halo-only margin; worst-side fully transparent margin is ~1%.

3 · The chase is page CSS on phase classes, not SMIL. Fifteen bulb seats were computed, not eyeballed: every 35.42 units along the 531.4-unit perimeter of the rounded-rect track (53,58)–(247,152), corner r 26 — 5 bulbs per phase group, symmetric left/right. Each seat is a baked “off” dome (socket ring, dark amber glass, faint spec) with a lit overlay (use of one defs group) classed .mq-bulb-a/-b/-c; the page runs mq-chase at 3 s with negative delays so the bright step travels clockwise once a second. With no stylesheet every overlay is opacity="1" — the asset’s resting still is the full marquee, which is also the reduced-motion state: frozen all-on, no keyframes anywhere.

4 · The label is printed on the lens and pinned with textLength. One line of fat dark uppercase inside a thin keyline box, printed-on-plastic style, on the condensed stack (Arial Narrow → Franklin Gothic → Impact). textLength + lengthAdjust squeeze whatever font the platform serves into the same 118-unit run, so the keyline never clips and the lens never rewraps. The ink stays dark in every tint — a backlit sign prints its message as shadow, it does not colour the letters — which keeps contrast honest even on the cherry lens.

5 · Two shadows, one bargain. The asset bakes a soft pool of contact shadow around the footprint (three feathered rounded rects — no filter primitives, so the sanitizer surface stays identical to turn-object.svg: no script, no on*, no foreignObject, no external refs, no SMIL). The pile’s own drop-shadow then adds the directional cast all items share; together they read as a heavy housing sitting in its own glow. As in 8B, varnish and vignette tint the halo slightly, and junk dragged onto the plate can bury it for the session — though burying a lit sign visibly leaks light, which is the best failure mode a hidden control has had yet.

6 · One host, one asset. The interactive element is the wrapper div (role="button", tabindex="0", aria-label, Enter/Space), itself pointer-events: none with visiblePainted on the ink — identical to .jd-item, so the object drops into the existing gesture wiring without special-casing. Ids are prefixed mq_ (underscore), classes mq- (hyphen), so per-copy id re-prefixing (here mq_ → m<N>_, production JD_svgInst) never touches a class the stylesheet drives. The template in this page’s source is the candidate production asset verbatim.
