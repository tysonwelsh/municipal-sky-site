The Sheet and the Bar

mockup 45 · replay by marks, not seconds · a blend of 42 and 44 · 2026-09-16

Mockup 42’s transport bar and mockup 44’s contact sheet, blended per the review: seconds appear nowhere. The control sits under the plate, the plate’s width: a row of eight thumbnail frames standing exactly over the track, then play/pause at the left, the track, and a two-line readout at the right that says only MARK over N of M. The track’s x-axis is the mark index — element order, first to last — and the little bar graph is now one bar per mark: bar i’s height is mark i’s ink, on the engine’s own scale (the square root of its measured length, which is also the share of the run the pen spends on it; raw length let one long outline flatten every other bar to a hairline). Thirty-one marks print thirty-one bars; six hundred print a dense profile. Drag the red caret, click the track, or use ← → (one whole mark a step; PageUp/Down a frame; Home/End; Space plays): the caret’s position is a fractional mark, mapped to the moment that mark is that far along, so between two bars mark i is drawn partway, and at a bar’s right edge that mark and every one before it are finished — the count is the mark under the pen, or just finished. The frames are the grouping: each stands over an eighth of the marks and shows the drawing with that eighth done (no dividers on the gauge and no hairlines up to the frames, because the frames already sit on their own stretch and lines would only add rigging); the frame whose stretch holds the pen wears the siblings strip’s red border, and a click on a frame jumps the caret to the end of its stretch. Trade-offs: the control is 60–70px tall under the plate (frames plus bar, by plate width), and the frames are eight real paused copies of the drawing — cheap here, a build spike on a phone for a 600-mark monster (notes below).

Notes for the build

  1. A shared module, JD_sheetbar(svg, host, svgText, pfx) in jd-core.js beside the engine, returning {seekMark, play, pause, destroy}. It arms the plate once with JD_drawOn(svg, {force:true}), parks the run by setting svg.__jdDrawSeq = -1 so the strip timer skips, and takes svg.getAnimations({subtree:true}) as its handle. Per-mark start and end come from the animations themselves (min delay / max delay+duration per target element, in document order); per-mark ink is getTotalLength() as the engine measures it. The mark axis maps to time through the cumulative end time Ti = max(Ti−1, endi), so dragging right never rewinds even when a small mark finishes inside the previous mark’s tail (its bar then spans no time, which is true). Play must call play() only on animations whose end is still ahead — a finished one auto-rewinds to zero and the element vanishes (mockup 44’s finding; mockup 42 had the bug and is patched). destroy() strips the plate and drops the frames.
  2. The frames are separate JD_svgInst copies (prefixed ids — a clone would share defs), each run through JD_drawOn(copy, {force:true, secs}) with the plate’s own secs, paused and seeked to Tc for c = (k+1)·M/8, strip defeated, held for the life of the card. No blank “zero marks” frame: a frame that summarises nothing earns no room, and putting each frame at the END of its own eighth keeps frame and stretch geometrically aligned. Build the frames IN PLACE and never move the control afterwards: re-inserting an element cancels and restarts its CSS animations from zero, and every frame would lose its moment (found on this sheet’s instrument exhibit).
  3. Cost. 8 × (elements + 1–2 CSSAnimations each). Build time is printed in the console ([mk45]); ~31 marks costs single-digit milliseconds. A 600-mark drawing means ~4,800 extra nodes and ~7,000 paused animations per card — idle once paused, but a real spike on a phone. Mitigations in order: build the frames one per animation frame after the plate’s first run starts; six frames under 480px (done here at build time); rasterise each frame to a canvas once seeked and drop the copy.
  4. jd-record.js: plateBtnsHTML drops .rc-draw (keeps .rc-dl); cardHTML appends the control’s mount after .rc-plate inside .rc-col-l (under the photograph in the portrait flow too). The response flip and the paper swap call destroy() and re-mount on the new svg. The click/key handlers’ .rc-draw exemptions become .jd-sheetbar exemptions.
  5. jd-turn.js: plate() with opts.replay mounts the control between .jd-turn-art and the figcaption instead of .jd-turn-draw--icon; the data-act="replay" handler and SKETCH_ICON retire.
  6. junk-drawer.css: the .mk-* block here renamed .jd-sheetbar-*, plus the two token maps on the scrims (the instrument also maps --form-rule(-2) so .rc-alt resolves). Delete .rc-draw / .jd-turn-draw and their zoom-fig variants; the enlargement should carry the same control in its bottom band in place of REDRAW.
  7. Reduced motion: arms with force:true, never autoplays under JD_reduced(), parks complete; scrubbing is the explicit request. Otherwise the card’s open-run becomes the control’s first play (this sheet autoplays once).
  8. Open questions. (a) Eight frames at ~34px under a 420px plate: legible for these specimens, tight for fine line-work — six everywhere? (b) √length as bar height is the engine’s dwell; raw length is the literal ink — a toggle is cheap if the owner wants to compare. (c) Should the enlargement carry the control (it has the room, and that is where analysis happens)? (d) Hover on a frame could preview its state on the plate without moving the caret — useful, or one gesture too many?
