4 · Notes

  • What I committed to. The tag is the candidate, not the bell. 8c only earns its place if the manila reads as the drawer's manila — so the paper is #f3e7c0→#d9c58e against the tag's #e8d9a8, the ink is the tag's #3a2a12 in typewriter caps, and the cord is the exact #b3402f the picked-item elastic uses in junk-drawer.js. Everything above the cord is confidently cartoon (4.5px ink line, one big soft window reflection, a squat 204×138 dome on a 260-wide plinth). The seam between the two registers is the joke: someone tied a museum tag to a Saturday-morning prop.
  • The tag is tied to the collar, not the plunger. The cord knots round the fixed collar at the base of the stem and the whole cord + tag group lives inside .sb-dome, so the knot can never drift off its anchor when the plunger drops 14px. The tag then counter-swings about its own grommet, which is what makes it lag the shiver instead of riding it rigidly. Watch the mechanism there: the tag is the one part with a placement transform attribute, and a CSS transform replaces an attribute rather than composing with it — so the attribute sits on a static seat group and the animated .sb-tag is the child inside it, swinging in its own local space via transform-box: fill-box. If implementation reparents or flattens the tag, that is the thing that breaks.
  • Tap target at drawer scale. The live readout above states it. On a desktop well (~980 stage, 64px walls) the bell lands near 90px wide — well past the 44px floor. On a 375px phone the same ruler gives roughly 62px. Production hit-testing is ink-only (pointer-events: visiblePainted), and the bell's ink is a near-solid 300×244 silhouette plus the tag, so almost the whole box is live — the best hit-area-per-footprint of the five, because nothing here is a thin rim. Do not let the plunger become the only "obvious" target: the dome is the target.
  • Label legibility — the honest bit. At a 90px-wide bell the tag ink is about 33×19px and TAKE A / TURN sits right on the edge — two lines of caps that a reader who already knows the words can read and a cold reader probably cannot. At the ~62px a phone gives, it is texture: "there is writing on that tag". So the tag buys affordance in the pile ("this one is labelled, it must be for something") and delivers the words only on the hero / picked zoom. If the owner wants the words legible in the pile, 8c needs a large tier or a two-word label — TAKE A TURN is four words' worth of characters on a 112×64 tag.
  • The far ding line leaves the viewBox. The near ring (r 120) stays inside; the far ring (r 148) crosses the top edge by ~11 user units on purpose, so the flare feels like it escapes the object. The resting art still fills the box edge-to-edge (≤2% margin on all four sides: plinth at x 4, tag at x 296 and y 239, plunger cap at y 4), so the ink-bounds check passes. Implementation must give the turn item's svg overflow: visible — production .jd-item svg does not set it and the UA default clips. If that is forgotten, only the outer ring's tips get trimmed; the press still reads. Both ring groups carry opacity="0" and pointer-events="none" as attributes in the asset, so the file is correct with no stylesheet at all and an invisible shape never steals a hit test.
  • Idle tell is page CSS, not SMIL, and it is quiet. One faint ring at 42% opacity for ~0.6s every 9s. Doubt worth the owner's attention: this is the gentlest idle tell of the five, and §1 leans on the idle tell to keep a control buried in clutter discoverable. At ~62px in a busy pile it may simply be too polite to do that job. Two cheap escalations if it tests weak on-device: warm the ring to the cream stroke only (drop the dark ink underlay so it reads as light, not line), or add a slow specular sweep across the dome — the chrome is begging for one. Under prefers-reduced-motion the ping is off entirely and the press becomes a discrete held state (plunger down, both rings on) for ~320ms: motion reduced, acknowledgement kept.
  • Z-order and the wood. The bell sits at the top of the pile's z-order here (the first-visit boost) and under the varnish + vignette, exactly as production stacks them; both are pointer-events: none so they tint it without stealing the press. Chrome is the risk in that corner — it takes its colour from whatever is behind it, and against dark wood a real chrome bell would go brown. I cheated it cool on purpose (the horizon band stays blue) so the object stays legible as hardware, and added the warm #e3ae63 bounce along the skirt so it does not look pasted on. The brass variant is the answer if the owner wants it to belong more; the cherry variant is the answer if it needs to shout.
