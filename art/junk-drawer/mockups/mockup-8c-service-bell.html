4 · Notes

  • What I committed to. The tag is the candidate, not the bell. 8c only earns its place if the manila reads as the drawer's manila — so the paper is #f3e7c0→#d9c58e against the specimen tag's #e8d9a8, the ink is the tag's #3a2a12 in typewriter caps, and the cord is the exact #b3402f the picked-item elastic uses in junk-drawer.js. Everything inside the plinth is confidently cartoon (4.5px ink line, one big soft window reflection, candy-gloss falloff). The seam between the two registers is the joke: someone tied a museum tag to a Saturday-morning prop.
  • Plan view, and what it cost. Round 1 drew this bell in ¾ elevation; the owner's ruling (PLAN-TURN-OBJECT §2) is that the drawer is a strict top-down plan, so it is redrawn down its own axis: concentric circles, outside in — plinth flange r82, dome r70, spun turning rings, collar r31, shaft well r26, plunger disc r20. What the elevation was carrying and this is not: the dome's height. From above a counter bell and a chrome drawer-pull are the same silhouette, so all of the "bell" now has to live in the shading — the radial falloff, the crescent specular on the lit shoulder, the dark reflected band on the far side, and the three lathe rings. Those rings are the cheapest and most load-bearing thing in the drawing: they say turned metal, seen down the axis in a way no gradient does. The tag, which used to hang off the collar, now lies flat on the boards up and to the right with the cord running flat across the open floor and under the plinth — and it is doing more work than before, because it is the only part of the object that is unmistakably not a drawer-pull.
  • Travel with no tilt. The press cannot translate anything "down" — from overhead, down is toward the viewer. So it is 8b's technique verbatim: the plunger disc shrinks (scale 0.79) and darkens, and because the dark shaft well behind it stays a fixed r26, the shadow ring around the disc widens from 4.5 to ~9 user units on its own. That widening ring is the whole travel cue and it is the part to judge. The dome answers rotationally: .sb-refl, the reflection group only, swims ±4° about the same axis — a struck chrome dome does not slide sideways, its highlights swing. The ding is now two full concentric circles growing out of the centre across the floor rather than arcs flaring off the top, which is what a ring looks like when you are above it. The tag pivots ~2° about its grommet, in the floor plane.
  • The tag is tied to the base, and it is drawn under it. The cord knots through the grommet, lies slack across the boards and its far end runs beneath the plinth — literally: the cord is drawn before .sb-base, so the flange covers where it lands. From above that is exactly how a tied-on tag reads, and it costs nothing to animate because the anchor is hidden. Mechanism worth keeping: the tag is the one part with a placement transform attribute, and a CSS transform replaces an attribute rather than composing with it — so the attribute sits on a static seat group and the animated .sb-tag is the child inside it, pivoting in its own local space via transform-box: fill-box. If implementation reparents or flattens the tag, that is the thing that breaks.
  • Tap target at drawer scale — better in plan than in elevation. The live readout above states it. On a desktop well (~980 stage, 64px walls) the bell lands near 92px wide; on a 375px phone the same ruler gives roughly 67px (both measured off the live readout above, desktop and 420px). Both are past the 44px floor, and the plan view improves the number: the object's ink is now a solid 164-unit disc plus the tag instead of a tapering elevation, so the live area is rounder and more centred under a thumb. Production hit-testing is ink-only (pointer-events: visiblePainted) and there is no thin rim anywhere here. Do not let the plunger disc become the only "obvious" target — it is 20 of 82 radius; the whole dome is the target, and at drawer scale the disc alone would be ~22px.
  • Label legibility — the honest bit, and plan view moved it. At a 92px-wide bell the tag paper is about 37×22px and TAKE A / TURN sits right on the edge: two lines of caps a reader who knows the words can read and a cold reader probably cannot. At the 67px a phone gives it is 27×16px — texture, "there is writing on that tag". Plan view helps slightly (the tag is flat-on rather than foreshortened by the ¾ angle, so no letter is compressed) and hurts slightly (it lies at 20°, so the reader tilts). Net: the tag still buys affordance in the pile ("this one is labelled, it must be for something") and delivers the words only on the hero and the picked zoom. If the owner wants them legible in the pile, 8c needs a large tier or a two-word label — TAKE A TURN is four words' worth of characters on a 122×72 tag.
  • Silhouette, honestly. The cost of the ruling: in plan the bell is a circle, and a circle is the drawer's most common shape (the pocket mirror next to it in §2 is one — compare them at 67px in that view). What separates them is the bullseye — dark well, bright disc, dead centre — plus the tag and its red cord breaking the outline off the upper right. That asymmetric outline is now the main silhouette read, which is an argument for never letting implementation crop the tag out of the box "to make it rounder". If the owner wants a stronger read, the brass variant is the cheap answer: it separates from the cool chrome of the mirror hardware immediately.
  • The ding circles leave the viewBox on purpose. Both rings are drawn at r80 and scaled at runtime — the near one out to 1.50, the far one to 1.85, which is r148 and well outside the box. They are born at 1.0-ish, i.e. already at the plinth's edge, so they spend their whole life travelling across the floor rather than sitting on the dome pretending to be a highlight. The resting art fills the box edge-to-edge — measured dead margin left 2, bottom 4, top ~1, right <1 user unit (0.7% / 1.7% / 0.4% / 0.3%) — so the ink-bounds check passes. Implementation must give the turn item's svg overflow: visible — production .jd-item svg does not set it and the UA default clips. If that is forgotten the outer ring gets trimmed at the box; the press still reads, because the travel cue is the shadow ring, not the ding. Both ring groups carry opacity="0" and pointer-events="none" as attributes in the asset, so the file is correct with no stylesheet at all and an invisible shape never steals a hit test.
  • Idle tell is page CSS, not SMIL, and it is quiet. One faint concentric ring at 44% opacity for ~0.6s every 9s, out of the centre. Doubt worth the owner's attention: this is the gentlest idle tell of the five, and §1 leans on the idle tell to keep a control buried in clutter discoverable. At 67px in a busy pile it may simply be too polite to do that job — and plan view raises the stakes, because the ring now expands past the object's own footprint onto the floor, where a neighbouring item dragged over the bell will sit on top of it. Two cheap escalations if it tests weak on-device: warm the ring to the cream stroke only (drop the dark ink underlay so it reads as light, not line), or rotate the dome's specular group a few degrees on a slow loop — a plan-view chrome dome is begging for that sweep and the group already exists. Under prefers-reduced-motion the ping is off entirely and the press becomes a discrete held state (plunger down, both rings on) for ~320ms: motion reduced, acknowledgement kept.
  • Z-order and the wood. The bell sits at the top of the pile's z-order here (the first-visit boost) and under the varnish + vignette, exactly as production stacks them; both are pointer-events: none so they tint it without stealing the press. Chrome is the risk in that corner — it takes its colour from whatever is behind it, and seen from directly above, a real chrome dome would be a mirror of the ceiling ringed by the brown of the drawer floor. I cheated it cool on purpose (the reflected band stays blue) so the object stays legible as hardware, and put the warm #e3ae63 bounce on the far flank of both dome and flange — the drawer's own light comes from the upper left, which is why every highlight arc in the asset sits between 180° and 270° and every shade arc between 0° and 90°. Keep that convention if the art is ever extended; it is the only thing making the disc read as domed. The brass variant is the answer if the owner wants it to belong more; the cherry variant is the answer if it needs to shout.
