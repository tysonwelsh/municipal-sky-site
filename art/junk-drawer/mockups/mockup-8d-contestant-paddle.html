Junk Drawer · Mockup 8D · The Contestant Paddle

Candidate 8d of PLAN-TURN-OBJECT.md §2 — a handheld quiz-show lockout buzzer that fell in the drawer, still plugged into something under the clutter. Drawn in plan view per §2's binding rule: the drawer is seen from directly above, so the paddle lies face-up on the floor and every cue of depth is concentric, not tilted. Most story, least button-shaped silhouette; this page exists to prove it still reads as pressable at drawer scale. Label everywhere is the placeholder TAKE A TURN.

1 · Hero — the object, press it

Click, tap, or focus it and hit Enter / Space. Nothing tilts: the press is travel, read the way a plan view has to read it — the cap's radius tightens, the dark shaft ring around it widens, the lamp comes on, and the coil shuffles across the floor on its boot. Left idle the cord twitches about once every eight seconds and one gloss sweep crosses the cap. All of it is off under prefers-reduced-motion, where the press becomes a held pose: cap parked down the shaft, lamp on.

Notes for the pick, and for whoever implements it

  • Plan view, and what carries the depth now. Round 1 drew this thing in ¾ elevation; §2 now makes the drawer's straight-down viewpoint binding, so it is redrawn lying face-up on the floor. Nothing stands, nothing recedes, there is no horizon and no side shadow. Depth is spent entirely on concentric radii — housing collar 82.5, chrome bezel 61±5.5, the dark shaft disc 53.5, the cap 48.5 — plus one light direction stated once: every gradient is gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse", so a single plastic ramp runs across the head and the grip from the upper-left and the two never shade like separate parts. The cap's own contact shadow is a disc offset 3.5 / 4 down-right under it, which is why the dark ring around the cap is thin at the upper-left and fat at the lower-right. The cast shadow is not baked in at all: it comes from the pile's own drop-shadow, the same one every other specimen gets, so the paddle pools its shadow around its footprint exactly like the key and the pencil beside it.
  • What I committed to. Cobalt plastic moulding, chrome bezel, fat red cap, charcoal rubber coil — unchanged from round 1, only re-viewed. The coil is now a slack flat helix seen from above, four turns relaxing unevenly toward the lower right and severed by the right edge of the viewBox rather than tidily terminated; that clipped cord is the whole "plugged into something under the clutter" story, and it costs nothing, because the root SVG clips to its own viewBox and the cut is clean at any scale. Measured ink in the 0 0 360 200 box leaves L 1.5% · R 0.1% · T 1.8% · B 2.0% of dead margin (check-svg-ink.sh: "tight enough"), so it can go into items/ with no viewBox normalisation.
  • The press had to be re-thought, not re-timed. In elevation a press is a dome sinking; from above that motion is invisible, so the travel is spelled out the way 8b spells it: the cap scales 1 → 0.875, which drops it down the shaft and widens the dark ring from ~5 to ~11 user units, the gloss and specular tighten with it (they are inside the same group), the lamp floods the cap, and a yellow impact ring expands past the bezel. The whole moulding gives a 2% cartoon jolt — a squash seen from directly above spreads, it does not flatten. The cord jiggle is now motion across the floor: rotate-plus-slide about the strain-relief boot at 292 131, no scaleY stretch, because a spring that stretched vertically here would read as standing up.
  • Tap target at drawer scale. Aspect is still exactly 1.8, so on the shared ruler (m = box 15.5) --w = 15.5 × √1.8 ≈ 20.8 — measured 112 × 62 px of ink in a desktop-sized well and 90 × 50 px in a 375px portrait well after production's ×1.2 small-well band; live figures for this viewport print under the swatch above. The whole painted silhouette is the hit target, so the item clears the 44px floor on both axes at both sizes. The cap alone got smaller in plan view — ~34px desktop / ~27px phone, against ~41 / ~33 in the round-1 elevation — and that is not a drawing error but the viewpoint being honest: foreshortening used to hide most of the grip's length, and now the grip claims it. No sizeScale rescues that at m (the cap alone would need ~1.8×); if the owner wants the red disc itself to be the touch target, that is an argument for 8a/8b, not a tuning knob on 8d. One trap survives: sitting exactly on SIZE.elong = 1.8 means any future lengthening of the cord starts the elongation cap shrinking the whole object, cap included.
  • Label legibility: better than round 1, still this candidate's real cost. The sticker is no longer foreshortened — it lies flat on the grip face at its true 68 × 46 with 13.5-unit type (round 1 could only afford 12 on a 68 × 36 parallelogram), which is a genuine ~15% gain and makes it legible in the hero at a glance. At pile scale it is still a ~23px desktop / ~18px phone label-shaped smudge: it says "this thing is labelled", not what the label says. It only becomes words on hover-lift or in the pick zoom (--pick-scale: 1.6). 8a/8b/8e still print their label bigger relative to the object; if legibility in the pile is a requirement, 8d loses on it, and it should not be rescued by shrinking the cord.
  • Silhouette and rotation — plan view fixed the worst of this. The outline is now one moulded body, a keyhole: circle plus tapered bar, drawn as a single dark union so nothing reads as a separate standing part. Two consequences. (1) Rotation is free. Round 1's warning — avoid ±70°…110° because it stands the coil on end — is void: from straight above, every rotation is just the paddle lying at a different angle on the floor, so the scatter can use its full range like any other item. (2) Z-order still matters more here than for the round candidates: the coil is thin ink across the right third of the box, so a neighbour dropped on it degrades the object from "buzzer with a cord" to "a knob". The first-visit z-boost from §1 earns its keep on 8d.
  • Nothing is baked into the art. No <script>, no on*, no foreignObject, no external refs, no filters, and no SMIL either — SMIL is allowed by the brief but it cannot be silenced by prefers-reduced-motion, and the idle tell is exactly the thing that must be silenceable. Motion is CSS on classed groups (.pd-cord .pd-body .pd-cap .pd-lit/.pd-ring/.pd-glint) with transform-box: view-box, so origins are stated in viewBox units and survive the pile's rotation, scale and drag transforms untouched. Under reduced motion the press does not just stop animating, it becomes a held pose — cap parked at 0.9 down the shaft, lamp on — so the travel still reads for a visitor who has switched motion off. The two press-only layers carry a real opacity="0" attribute, so the file opened on its own — or dropped into the pile before any CSS lands — shows the resting, unlit buzzer instead of a permanently glowing one.
  • Watch on implementation. The press animation lives on a wrapper, not on .jd-item itself — the pile already owns that element's transform for drag (--dx/--dy) and its filter for the lift shadow, and a competing keyframe on either would fight the gesture. Wrap, don't reuse. The JD_haptic tick should fire on the same frame as the cap light-up, not on modal open.
