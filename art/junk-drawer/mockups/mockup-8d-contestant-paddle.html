Notes for the pick, and for whoever implements it

  • What I committed to. Cobalt plastic grip, chrome bezel, fat red cap, charcoal rubber coil. The coil is severed by the right edge of the viewBox rather than tidily terminated — that clipped cord is the whole "plugged into something under the clutter" story, and it costs nothing: the root SVG clips to its own viewBox, so the cut is clean at any scale. Rendered ink in the 0 0 360 200 box leaves L 0.8% · T 1.3% · R 0% · B 1.3% of dead margin, so it can go into items/ already passing check-svg-ink.sh with no viewBox normalisation.
  • Tap target at drawer scale. On the shared ruler (m = box 15.5) the artwork's 1.8 aspect gives --w = 15.5 × √1.8 ≈ 20.8 — about 112 × 62 px of ink in a desktop-sized well and 90 × 50 px in a 375px portrait well after production's ×1.2 small-well band. The live figures for this viewport are printed under the swatch above. The whole painted silhouette is the hit target, so the item clears the 44px floor on both axes; the red cap alone is ~41px desktop / ~33px phone and the blue housing ~55 / 44px. If the owner wants the red cap itself to be the 44px target, file it m with sizeScale: 1.15. One trap: the aspect sits exactly on SIZE.elong = 1.8, so any future lengthening of the cord makes the elongation cap start shrinking the whole object, button included.
  • Label legibility is this candidate's real cost. The sticker is two lines because one line of TAKE A TURN across a 68-unit sticker is unreadable at any drawer size. Even so, at pile scale the sticker is a ~21px label-shaped smudge: it says "this thing is labelled", not what the label says. It only becomes words in the hero, on hover-lift, or in the pick zoom (--pick-scale: 1.6). 8a/8b/8e all print their label bigger relative to the object; if legibility in the pile is a requirement, 8d loses on it and should not be rescued by shrinking the cord.
  • Z-order and rotation are riskier here than for the round candidates. The coil is wide, thin ink at the far right of the box — a neighbour dropped on the right third swallows it, and the object degrades from "buzzer with a cord" to "a knob". Two implications: the first-visit z-boost from §1 matters more for 8d than for any other candidate, and the scatter's rotation range should avoid roughly ±70°…110°, which stands the coil on end and reads as a spring, not a cord.
  • Nothing is baked into the art. No <script>, no on*, no foreignObject, no external refs, and no SMIL either — SMIL is allowed by the brief but it cannot be silenced by prefers-reduced-motion, and the idle tell is exactly the thing that must be silenceable. Motion is CSS on four classed groups (.pd-cord .pd-cap .pd-head .pd-lit/.pd-ring/.pd-glint) with transform-box: view-box, so origins are stated in viewBox units and survive the pile's rotation, scale and drag transforms untouched. The two press-only layers carry a real opacity="0" attribute, so the file opened on its own — or dropped into the pile before any CSS lands — shows the resting, unlit buzzer instead of a permanently glowing one.
  • Watch on implementation. The press animation lives on a wrapper, not on .jd-item itself — the pile already owns that element's transform for drag (--dx/--dy) and its filter for the lift shadow, and a competing keyframe on either would fight the gesture. Wrap, don't reuse. The JD_haptic tick should fire on the same frame as the cap light-up, not on modal open.
