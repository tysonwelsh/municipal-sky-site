Junk Drawer · Mockup 15A — “The Requisition”

The whole turn is one municipal document: FORM JD-1 — REQUISITION FOR SPECIMEN. The seven cards are its seven chapters. One sheet of manila moves through six stations of a bureau, and each station marks it — the routing strip ticks, the docket fills in, and the stamps land. The blank form carries a dashed empty ring reserved “for department use”; the last card lands the ACCESSIONED seal in exactly that spot.

Design notes — 15A “The Requisition”

1 · The fiction, and its rules

The turn is not a wizard with seven screens. It is one sheet of paper — FORM JD-1 — moving through six stations of a bureau. Every card is the same sheet at a different desk. Three things make that legible, and they are on every card in identical geometry: the form band (form number, department, revision), the docket line, and the routing strip.

The four hands (the one hard rule)

  • The printer — serif small caps in faded ink. The blank form: form number, section heads, line numbers (4.1, 4.2…), boilerplate. It exists before anyone touches the paper.
  • The machines — typewriter mono at full ink. Everything filled in: the requisition number, timestamps, the subject line (the visitor’s own words, typed onto the form), the status column, the attribution at the unveil.
  • The bureau — red rubber stamps, and nothing else. A stamp is applied by the department at a station. The visitor can never cause a stamp directly; a stamp is always something that happened to the form.
  • The visitor — graphite cursive. A pencil tick in the line-number gutter when a scale is answered; a pencilled ring around the determination stop they chose; the determination written out in hand under the scale. The visitor writes; the visitor never stamps.

What each stamp means

  • Round seal = a determination. Two exist: RECEIVED (the requisition is now real and has a number — the darkroom) and ACCESSIONED (the specimen has entered the collection — the unveil). Round seals carry the department ring and a date.
  • Box stamp = routine handling. EXHIBITS ATTACHED (reveal), UNDER EXAMINATION (the bench’s pinned plate), OVER QUOTA (held at intake), RETURNED TO SENDER and NOT FILED (the failure ends). Box stamps sit inline in a section head, or lie across the section when the news is bad enough to interrupt reading.
  • The dashed empty ring is not a stamp — it is the space reserved for one. The Brief carries it captioned “space reserved for department use · do not write”; the Unveil lands ACCESSIONED in exactly that spot, same size, same rotation. The tie card re-uses it (“seal withheld pending election”), which is why a tie feels unfinished until the visitor elects.
  • Every stamp is masked with a turbulence speckle and set to mix-blend-mode: multiply, so the ink breaks up over the paper grain the way a stamp pad does, and never reads as a UI badge.

2 · How progress reads

Three independent readings, all saying “this document is filling in”:

  1. The routing strip — six station boxes, always all six, always in the same place. Cleared stations take a red ✓; the current station is red-outlined with a 3 px inset bar (never colour alone); stations a returned form will never reach are struck through in red. A visitor who glances at nothing else can see “3 of 6, and it moved.”
  2. The docket — “req. no.” and “received” start as italic dotted blanks and are typed in the moment the words are sent. By the unveil the third slot has become “acc. no.”, which is the number the specimen keeps.
  3. The stamps — blank form → RECEIVED → ATTACHED → (the visitor’s pencil) → ACCESSIONED. Note the deliberate gap: the examination and the call carry no new stamp, because nothing bureaucratic happens there. Those two cards are the only ones where the visitor’s own hand is the mark, and the ticks on the rail and in the number gutters are what fills them in.

The narration line under the strip (“Station 4 of 6 — examination. Exhibit A complete; exhibit B on the bench.”) is the phone’s primary progress read, since the strip compresses to numerals under 600 px.

3 · Two rails, and why they can’t be confused

There are two horizontal step-like things, and keeping them apart was the main layout problem.

  • The routing strip reports and never navigates: it lives in the masthead, is ~22 px tall, is set in serif small caps, and is an <ol>, not buttons.
  • The bench rail navigates and is the only control of its kind: it lives in the body, is 44 px tall, has a two-line button face, and is labelled with the form’s own section numbers — 4·A, 4·B, 5. That last move is the payoff of the fiction: the rail isn’t “steps in a wizard”, it is which section of this form you are writing in, which is exactly what the live code does (the third step IS the call).

4 · Functional parity checklist

  • Brief — textarea (500 max) with the count seated inside the field’s lower-right as a printed counter box; SEND IT (brass); the third-party disclosure as “Notice to requester” boilerplate under a rule. The disclosure is quiet but present — small print on a form is the most natural home it has ever had. The honeypot input keeps its off-screen position (not shown).
  • Darkroom — per-slot pending / ok / failed as a three-column status table (demo 2 and 2b cover all three states), plus the slow-turn notice as a clipped “Notice of delay” slip.
  • Reveal — both plates, blind, with the withholding stated as “Rule 4 — blind examination”, and the grade button.
  • Bench — clickable rail (44 px, disabled steps still visible), sticky pinned plate, four native <select>s (phone OS pickers intact), ⓘ popovers in flow spanning the row grid so they push the sheet down and never cover a control, the broken/offensive flag with its folded note, back/next.
  • Call — the 5-stop likert rail spanning A-left to B-right, unchanged in structure; the stops are printed graduations and the pick adds a pencilled ring on top of the red dot (selection is never colour alone: dot fill + ring + bolded caption + the written determination line).
  • Unveil — attribution table, disposition per exhibit, the accession stub, DONE + TAKE ANOTHER TURN; the tie election survives as the pill row (its only remaining use).
  • Apology — generation failure, filing failure, and over-quota, each with its retry path.

5 · Integration watch-outs

  • The masthead is outside the scroller. Live, everything lives in .jd-turn-scroll. Here .rq-mast is a fixed flex child and .rq-body is the only scroller. This is deliberate — the document’s identity must not scroll away — but it costs ~78 px of card height on desktop and ~70 on a phone, and it is the number to re-measure first if the bench runs long. If it has to go, drop the narration line before the routing strip.
  • Two sticky layers on the bench. .rq-pin is position: sticky; top: 0 inside .rq-body, which is correct because the masthead is outside that scroller. If the masthead is ever moved back inside, the pin needs a top offset or it will slide under it.
  • Stamps use mix-blend-mode: multiply plus a mask. Both are cheap, but a stamp positioned over a position: sticky element will composite oddly during scroll — keep every stamp inside .rq-secbody and out of .rq-pin (the pin’s “under examination” mark is a plain box stamp with no blend-critical overlap).
  • Line numbers are generated, not authored. 4.1 is the overall grade, 4.2… follow liveAxes() in taxonomy order, and 4.9 is reserved for the report. If an axis is added or retired the numbers shift — that is fine and correct for a form, but it means nothing may ever reference “4.3” in copy.
  • The rate-limit path does not reach the apology. settleSlot clears the turn and returns to prompt with a notice, so OVER QUOTA had to be designed onto sec. 1 (demo 7c), not onto the disposition card. Designing it as an apology would have been a fiction that contradicts the code.
  • The degraded one-survivor turn has no rail at all in the live code (one panel, then file). In this design that means sec. 4 renders without the rail and its action reads “File the grades”; the routing strip still shows station 5 as cleared-by-default, since no determination is possible.
  • min-width: 0 on the card is load-bearing. The card is a flex item of the scrim, so its default min-width: auto lets any nowrap descendant — a stamp, the form number, a scale title — push it wider than a 375 px viewport. The live .jd-turn has the same latent exposure and gains new nowrap children with this design.
  • Escaped text discipline holds. The only visitor content on the paper is the subject line and the report note, both plain text nodes; every stamp, corner, ring, tick and rule is CSS or inline SVG.
  • Fit. The card is width: min(680px, 100%), max-height: 100% of the banner-reserved padded scrim, box-sizing: border-box throughout — unchanged from the live contract. Only .rq-body scrolls, and only when the content genuinely exceeds the viewport (the bench at 375 px is the case that does).

6 · Deliberate copy decisions

The buttons keep the drawer’s plain speech — Send it, Grade the drawings, File the grades, Done — rather than becoming “Submit form JD-1”. The fiction lives in the paper, not in the voice; a bureau that also talked like a bureau would be a joke that overstays. The one place the fiction takes over the words is where the form is genuinely speaking about itself: section heads, the routing strip, the notice slips, and the stamps.

7 · Reading this file

Each demo is a 100 vh fake viewport; open #d1…#d12 to isolate one. The 375 px gallery at the end is this same file loaded in 375-wide iframes — the only way to make the real media queries fire — and its srcs are assigned after the parent’s load event, one every 120 ms, because twelve frames requested during load will stall the document. That script is the only JavaScript here; no card state is scripted.

“Response A/B” becomes Exhibit A/B throughout, because an exhibit is a thing attached to a document, and that is the whole conceit. This is the one wholesale rename the design asks for.
