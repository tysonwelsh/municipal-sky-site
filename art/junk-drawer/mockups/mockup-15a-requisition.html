Design notes — 15A “The Requisition”, rev. 2

Rev. 1 was rejected with the round: too cluttered and full of text — designed for the designer, not the end user. This pass is the cut. The fiction survives; almost all of its narration does not.

1 · What was cut, and why

  • The routing strip / station map. Six labelled boxes ticking through a bureau the visitor doesn’t work for. It told the designer the fiction was coherent; it told the visitor nothing they could act on. Gone, with the “station 4 of 6 —” narration line under it.
  • The docket. Req. no. / received / requester, filling in blank by blank. A progress display dressed as paperwork. Gone.
  • The section heads. Every card had a heading in the masthead and a “SEC. 4 · EXAMINATION” rule under it. One heading is enough; the section number now rides the form-number badge as a two-character §4.
  • The explanatory paragraphs. “Everything here is optional — sec. 5 is not. Titles only; press an i…”, “No names yet. Who drew which is withheld until your grades are filed.”, “Two machines get your words, verbatim…”. At most one short line per card survives, and only where the visitor can’t act without it: the Brief, the Darkroom, the Reveal and the Unveil each keep one. The Bench and the Call keep none — the two cards where the visitor is working are the two with the least to read.
  • The level-by-level definitions. The ⓘ popover listed every value with a sentence each — twelve lines to explain a four-option select whose options are already words. The ⓘ stays; its popover is now the axis’s one-line definition and nothing else.
  • The reserved dashed seal ring (“space reserved for department use · do not write”), the subject echo of the prompt on later cards, the machine assignment table (“exhibit / assigned to / status” with both rows reading “withheld”), the written-out determination line (“Determination: exhibit B, narrowly · signed visitor 8F2C”) and the accession stub. Each was a paragraph of set dressing where a word or nothing would do.

2 · What survives — the fiction, whispering

  • One sheet. Same manila, same double-ruled border, same badge in the same corner on all seven cards. Nothing else is needed to say “still the same form”.
  • Stamps as states. A stamp carries the state a paragraph used to, which is the entire point of a stamp. Round seals are determinations: RECEIVED at the darkroom, ACCESSIONED at the unveil. Rectangular box stamps are handling: ATTACHED, OVER QUOTA, RETURNED TO SENDER, NOT FILED. If a state can be stamped, it is never also written out.
  • Two hands, never swapped. The bureau stamps in red rubber; the visitor writes in graphite — the pencilled ring around the likert stop, the pencil tick on a finished rail step, the tie election’s tick. The visitor never stamps and the bureau never writes in pencil. That rule is what lets a red mark mean “the system did this” without a legend.
  • The busiest card gets the least decoration. The Bench has no stamp at all: the visitor is working there, and the artwork is the only thing that should compete with the controls.

3 · How progress reads now

Three signals, all of them free: the heading changes (describe → drawing → came back → grade → the call → who drew what); the section number on the badge climbs §1 → §6; and the stamps accumulate — blank sheet at the Brief, RECEIVED while it works, ACCESSIONED at the end. Inside the grading, the rail is the only step display, and it is a real control: three targets, ticked in pencil as they are finished, disabled until reached.

4 · Card by card — what earns its square inches

  • The Brief. Heading, one line, field, count, SEND IT, fine print. The disclosure keeps the canonical JD_CONSENT.text wording at 0.55rem — quiet, present, never a wall.
  • The Darkroom. One line (“leave this open”), two slot rows, the RECEIVED seal. The slow-turn notice appears as a clipped slip; per-slot ok / failed shows in the row itself (2b).
  • The Reveal. Both plates, one line, one button. The small ATTACHED stamp is the only ornament.
  • The Bench. Rail, pinned plate, four native selects with ⓘ, the broken/offensive flag (its note appears only once ticked), back/next. Nothing else.
  • The Call. Rail, both plates at reference size, one question line with a REQUIRED tag, the five-stop rail, back / file the grades.
  • The Unveil. Two attribution lines, one line of outcome, the buttons, the seal. The tie variant swaps the line for the keep-chooser and withholds the seal until the election is made.
  • The Apology. One line and a stamp across it. Filing-failed and over-quota are the same shape with a different stamp; over-quota rides the Brief, because that is where the live flow returns.

5 · Integration watch-outs

  • The masthead sits outside the scroller. .rq-head is a flex item of the card and .rq-body is the only scrolling region — production’s .jd-turn-scroll currently holds everything, so the heading needs lifting out of the painted body when this lands.
  • The heading is the focus target. head() in junk-drawer.js focuses the title on every state change; keep tabindex="-1" on .rq-title and keep it first in the DOM after the ✕.
  • The ⓘ popover is in flow (grid-column: 1 / -1) and must stay so: a floating popover on a phone covers the select it explains.
  • Selects stay native and 16px under 768px, or iOS zooms the fixed layer half off-screen.
  • Escaped text only. The prompt and the flag note render as text; every stamp, corner, ring and rule is CSS or inline SVG.
  • The card is a flex item with min-width: 0 — without it a nowrap stamp pushes the whole card past a 375px viewport.
  • Stamp mask. All stamps share the --tspeck alpha mask and mix-blend-mode: multiply; keep stitchTiles="stitch" or Safari re-tiles the noise per element.
