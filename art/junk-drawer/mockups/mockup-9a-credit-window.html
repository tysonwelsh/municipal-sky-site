Junk Drawer · Mockup 9A · The Credit Window

9A — THE CREDIT WINDOW. The drawer’s only light source: a rectangular backlit credit button borrowed straight off a coin door, its translucent amber lens lit from behind so the printed TAKE A TURN reads as a dark silhouette in the glow — the label IS the lens, and the halo it spills onto the wood is what separates it from every unlit thing in the pile.

1 · Hero — press it

click / tap, or focus and hit Enter / Space
idle: watch the backlight breathe every ~8 s

 

the press — the lens travels down a visible step into the housing (it shrinks with a slight squash and the dark well mouth widens around it), beats at the bottom of the stroke, springs back with a small overshoot · the backlight flares white-hot and the halo surges with it · under reduced motion the same press is an instant depressed pose with no keyframes at all, and the idle breathing stops

2 · In the drawer — bottom-left corner, real floor, real neighbours

the separation test — floor, craquelure, frame, wall shade, varnish and vignette are the production values copied out of junk-drawer.css; the paperclip (xs × 0.8), brass key (s) and three of hearts (m) are the real items/ SVGs, sized by the real tier math in cqmin of the well · the button sits where production screws it down: bottom-left corner, 3.5% inset, 0°, over the scatter (production z 99) · medium × 1.15 like the shipping doorbell — on a 375 px phone that is a ~58 × 72 px touch target · it presses here too: rendered right now at —

the read test — at 58 px the TURN line is ~12 px of silhouette capitals and still legible; below that the two bars + stacked dark blocks read as “printed lens” texture · but the affordance was never the type — it is the GLOW: the lens is the one lit thing in the drawer, and light says pressable before any word does

3 · Lens colours — the coin-door row, lit in the dark

cheap variants — the reference photo’s four-lens row; a variant is a host class overriding the classed gradient stops (cw-g0..3 lens, cw-h0..3 halo, cw-c0..2 core) plus the lens rim and bloom strokes, exactly the db-b0..4 pattern from turn-object.svg · the housing and the printed ink never change · amber is the recommendation: warmest against the wood, and it is the colour the wood’s own lamplight already speaks

Notes · what I committed to, what implementation must watch

1 · The glow is the whole thesis, and it lives in the asset. The halo is a radial gradient drawn UNDER the housing, fading to fully transparent near the viewBox edge — nothing else in the drawer emits light, so the amber pool on the wood is what breaks the camouflage the doorbell suffered from. Because the halo is real ink, the box is honest: the solid housing spans 83% of the viewBox on both axes, so the drawer’s area-normalized size math and the corner reservation are measuring hardware, not empty light. Worst-side fully transparent margin is <1% (halo reaches x 2..238 of 240).

2 · Fixed hardware, not a pile item. Per the 2026-08-10 revision this page seats the button exactly as production seats the doorbell: bottom-left corner at inset 0.035 plus half-plate, dead straight at 0°, above every fresh scatter (z 99). Same viewBox aspect (240×300) and same tier (m × 1.15) as the shipping asset, so JD_turnObject.GEOM, the corner reservation, and the measured 58 × 72 px / 375 px-phone numbers all carry over unchanged — this candidate is a drop-in re-skin, not a re-seat.

3 · The label is printed ON the lens, silhouette-style. Dark ink over a lit ground survives small sizes far better than lit type on dark ground (the backlight does the contrast work), and the two thick bars above the text are the reference lens’s own art — at sizes where TAKE A TURN goes sub-legible, bars + text-blocks still read as “printed credit window.” The type is anchored with textLength + lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs" so the fit inside the lens is exact on every platform’s condensed-sans fallback chain, not dependent on font metrics; a darker offset echo under the ink gives the “screened-onto-plastic” impression.

4 · The idle tell is CSS, not SMIL. Nothing animated is baked into the SVG — the ~8s breathing and the press are page-level keyframes targeting .cw-glowcore, .cw-halo, .cw-bloom, .cw-lens, .cw-flash (no script, no on*, no foreignObject, no external refs in the asset), which keeps prefers-reduced-motion in charge: the reduced state is the BASE state (static glow, instant depressed pose) and motion is added back only inside no-preference; the checkbox above simulates the setting. The flash carries opacity="0" as an attribute so the asset is a correct standalone still — a lit, unpressed button.

5 · The halo and the pile’s drop-shadow. .jd-item’s wrapper drop-shadow sees the halo’s alpha and re-casts it as a faint warm shadow offset down-right. At the halo’s low opacities it is invisible in practice (verify on the stage above), but if implementation ever deepens the item shadow, exempt #jd-turn-object from the filter or move the shadow into the asset — a light source with a dark aura is the one read this design cannot afford.

6 · One host, one asset. The interactive element is the wrapper div (role="button", tabindex="0", aria-label, Enter/Space), itself pointer-events:none with visiblePainted on the ink — identical to production’s turn-object wiring, so the candidate drops into the existing module by swapping the artwork string. The template in this page’s source is the candidate production asset verbatim; the page only namespaces its cw_ ids per copy (classes use cw- with a hyphen precisely so the re-prefixing can never touch them).
