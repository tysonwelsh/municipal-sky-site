The Ruler on the Plate’s Edge

mockup 43 · replay & scrub for the drawings · 2026-09-16

The replay control becomes a ruler printed along the photograph’s bottom edge, in the graph paper’s own blue, inside the margin band the artwork already leaves clear — so nothing sits over the drawing and the plate’s footprint does not change. A red-pencil caret marks the moment; beside it, in the pencilled hand, a count of which element is being drawn (“14 of 35”) and the elapsed seconds, and a row of hairline marks above the scale shows where every element starts — dark once drawn, faint while pending, so the crowded stretches are visible before you scrub into them. Press and drag anywhere on the scale to scrub, click to jump, ←/→ to step one element at a time (Home/End for the ends, space to play); the small tab at the ruler’s left end plays and pauses, its glyph a drawn open triangle that closes into two bars. The margin furniture moves up one ruler-height rather than sharing a line: MODEL/DATE keep their lower-left seat, DOWNLOAD SVG and the file number keep their lower-right stack, and the old REPLAY button simply leaves the row — the ruler runs under all of them along the very edge, where a scale on a lab print belongs. Trade-offs: the band is fixed at 20% of the plate, so below roughly a 320px plate the ruler drops to a compact cut (26px, numbered every 20) and the stack gets tight; the instrument’s plate has no margin band today and must yield its bottom 17% to get one.

Notes for the build

  • jd-core.js — the engine needs one addition: a way to HOLD a run. Today JD_drawOn strips its inline animations (secs + 0.4)s after it starts; the ruler defeats that here by bumping svg.__jdDrawSeq right after the call, then drives every CSSAnimation from svg.getAnimations({subtree:true}) itself — all paused, currentTime set from its own clock (a requestAnimationFrame loop while playing). Ship it as JD_drawOn(svg, {force:true, hold:true}) returning {secs, anims, starts}, plus an exported JD_drawOff(svg) (the private strip) so a re-arm can clear the held styles first — without that, a second JD_drawOn reads the held dasharray as “pre-dashed” and demotes every stroke to a fade. Note anim.play() is NOT usable for resume: a finished element’s animation auto-rewinds to 0 on play(), so mid-run resume must stay clock-driven.
  • jd-record.js — plateBtnsHTML drops the REPLAY button (the word REDRAW on the enlargement goes with it, or the enlargement gets the same ruler); cardHTML / zoomHTML append rulerHTML() to the plate; the plate’s click/key handlers exempt .jd-ruler from the zoom as they do .rc-plate-btns (it carries rc-zoom-keep); the open / response-flip draw-on runs go through the ruler so it owns the clock; a response flip re-arms on the new svg. The swipe-to-flip gesture on the plate must ignore pointerdowns inside the ruler.
  • jd-turn.js — plate() mounts the ruler where it mounts .jd-turn-draw today (opts.replay); the delegated data-act="replay" handler becomes the ruler’s. The bench’s pinned plate and the pod prints (15px corners) want the compact cut.
  • junk-drawer.css — one shared .jd-ruler block (the tokens --rl-ink / --rl-red / --rl-paper / --rl-h, the tab, the track, the SVG classes, the blueprint re-tint) beside the draw-on keyframes; .rc-notes, .rc-plate-btns, .rc-note-no take their new bottoms in terms of --rl-h; .jd-turn-art-in goes to inset: 7% 7% 17% when a ruler is mounted (the bench plate without one keeps 7%). The .rc-draw rules retire.
  • Open questions — (1) should the ruler read 0–100 (scale-free, the same print on every plate, as here) or seconds (true to the clock, a different ruler per drawing)? (2) DOWNLOAD SVG could become the ruler’s RIGHT end cap (a ⤓ tab), which frees the lower-right stack entirely and puts the file number back on its own line — against the standing call that the word stays on the card. (3) The element marks: keep, or reserve for the enlargement, where there is room to hover them for an element’s tag name? (4) On open, the card still draws itself once (the caret rides along and parks at 100); under reduced motion it parks at 100 without playing and the ruler is the explicit way in.
