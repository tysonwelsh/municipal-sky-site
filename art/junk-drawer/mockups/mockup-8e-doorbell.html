4 · Notes

  • What I committed to. Premise-fit over spectacle, exactly as the candidate promises. The whole object is four shapes a child would draw — plate, four screws, one fat pearl, one taped note — at a 5px cartoon ink line, saturated brass and a candy-gloss diagonal sweep across the plate. No starburst, no motion lines, nothing leaving the viewBox. The label does the work the other four candidates give to noise: it is the only thing on the drawer floor with instructions typed on it, and that is 8e's whole discoverability argument.
  • The sink is real mechanism, not a wobble. Bezel, then a dark well (db_well), then the pearl drawn on top of it: when .db-pearl drops 10 user units the well shows above the button, so the eye reads "that went into a hole". The plate's buzz starts with a scale jolt (3.5% squash) before the side-to-side shake, because at drawer scale a 4-unit shake is under a pixel while a 3.5% squash of the whole silhouette still reads. Implementation should keep that ordering if it re-times the keyframes.
  • Tap target at drawer scale. The live readout above states it. Filed medium, the plate measures 73 × 91px on this page's 1200px-wide stage (it grows toward ~95px on a taller well, since cqmin follows the well's short side) and 47 × 58px on a 375px phone under production's ×1.2 small-well bump — i.e. three pixels above the 44px touch floor, the tightest of the five, because a portrait plate spends its footprint on height. Ink hit-testing helps here: the plate is a solid rounded rectangle, so essentially the entire box is live (unlike a thin-rimmed or L-shaped object). If the owner picks 8e, my recommendation is to file it "m" with sizeScale around 1.15, or simply "l" × 0.8 — do not ship it at a bare medium.
  • Label legibility — the honest bit. On the hero the label is a joke you can read. In the drawer the cap height of TAKE A TURN is 5.3px (7px at the largest wells) and 3.4px on a phone — the readout above states it live. That is texture, not text — "there is typing on that thing", which still says this object has a purpose and is most of what the label needs to buy in the pile. The words themselves land on the picked zoom and in the aria-label. If the owner wants them legible on the floor, the answer is a shorter placeholder (PRESS, or the eventual name) rather than a bigger tier — TAKE A TURN is 11 characters across a 191-unit paper.
  • Z-order, the tape hinge, and the one thing that breaks. The doorbell sits at the top of the pile z-order (the §1 first-visit boost) and under the varnish + vignette, both pointer-events: none, exactly as production stacks them. The label is drawn first and the tape strips after it, so the paper flaps under its own tape — that is why the hinge is transform-origin: 50% 3% on a fill-box. Watch the mechanism there: the tilt is a transform ATTRIBUTE on the static seat group and the CSS animates the child, because a CSS transform replaces an attribute rather than composing with it. Flatten those two groups and the label snaps upright the moment it is pressed.
  • Idle tell is page CSS, not SMIL, and it is a whisper. One gleam sweeping the pearl for ~1s every 8s, clipped to the button so it reads as light on a curved surface. Under prefers-reduced-motion it is off entirely and the press becomes a discrete held state (pearl down, rim flash on) for ~320ms: motion reduced, acknowledgement kept. Doubt for the owner: a 17px-wide pearl gleaming once every 8s may be too polite to do the job §1 asks of it. The cheap escalation is the bakelite variant — a red button is a pre-attentive colour pop against brown wood in a way a pearl is not.
  • The real risk is camouflage, and it cuts both ways. Brass on warm wood, aged paper next to manila specimen tags — 8e is the candidate most likely to be too at home. The pearl and the cartoon ink line are what save it (a bright near-white disc is the only one on the floor), but the owner should judge section 2 on a phone before deciding; if it disappears there, the nickel variant is the answer that keeps the object and drops the problem. Compared to 8a/8b this is the quiet one on purpose — the question is whether "quiet" and "findable in clutter" can both be true.
