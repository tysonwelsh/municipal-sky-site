The Filmstrip

mockup 46b · the thumbnails ARE the track · the ruler overprinted · 2026-09-16

Mockup 45 stacked two things that said the same thing twice — a row of frames and, under it, a bar per mark — for 60–70px of the card. So the graph is cut and the frames become the track: one container, the thumbnails butted across it, the red caret scrubbing over the top of the pictures, and ruling to keep it reading as a timeline. One row, 34–48px by frame count. The axis is still the mark — element order, first to last — and dragging anywhere on the strip seeks; each cell shows the drawing at the end of its own stretch, so cell k and the stretch under it are the same span of the run and the last cell is the finished drawing.

Rev b settles two of 46’s open questions. The ruling is now A and B merged: the ruled cells keep their container and their frame divisions, and B’s ruler is overprinted on the pictures — the ticks alone, majors at the frame boundaries and three minors between, standing 9px off the strip’s floor, with no paper band and no baseline under them. It reads as a timeline the way B did and costs the zero extra height A did. And the readout drops the word MARK, and the word of, for a printed fraction — 14 over 31, set diagonally across a slanted solidus, unboxed — kept at the right of the strip where 45’s block was, but 24px wide against its ~48 and 16px tall against the square form’s 23, at a larger type size than either. What is still unsettled is what carries how far along: A and C wash the not-yet-drawn stretch of picture in the card’s own paper, so the strip develops left to right as it plays; B is A exactly, unwashed, with the elapsed run inked along the floor instead. Notes for the build

  1. What changed in rev b. Three things, all cheap. (a) The ruler is overprinted: one .mk-grid::after, two repeating gradients keyed off --tb-period, absolutely positioned on the strip’s floor and painted ABOVE the wash so it reads continuously while the pictures develop underneath. B’s 12px band and its border-top baseline are gone; the strip’s height is the cells’ own in every variant now. (b) The readout stays where 45 put it — at the right of the strip — but is set as a fraction, the mark over the count with a border-top between: no label, no “of”, and aria-hidden, because the strip’s aria-valuetext already says “mark n of M” in full. The two numbers are thrown off a slanted solidus — the mark up and left, the count down and right, each 1.25em off centre and overlapping vertically by .18em — with nothing boxing them in. The throw has to clear a two-digit numeral (1.2em in this mono) or the stroke lands on a glyph; 1.25em is the tightest that still leaves a clean lane. It comes to 24px × 16px at .62rem, against 45’s ~48 × 23 at .56rem — bigger type in a smaller footprint. The strip gains roughly 24px of track on a 380px plate for nothing, and at 16px tall it is well under the play button’s 28px — the row’s height floor is the button, and only the strip can make the control taller. A four-digit count (M > 999) is the one case that widens the column.
  2. What changed from 45. barsSVG() and the .mk-prof pair are deleted outright; the frames stop being buttons and become the track’s own content (pointer-events stay on the strip, so a press anywhere scrubs continuously rather than jumping to a frame’s end — strictly more control, one less gesture to explain). Everything else — arming, the per-mark start/end walk, the cumulative Ti axis, play-only-what-is-ahead, the frame builder — is 45’s code unchanged.
  3. Height. The strip’s height is the cells’ own: the plate’s 84:74 at one nth of the track’s width, so nothing is squashed and the number of frames is the height dial. Under a 420px plate: 12 frames → 23px, 8 → 34px, 6 → 46px, 4 → 68px. Twelve is the owner’s pick (2026-09-16): the most stages the strip will show, and the shortest row — 23px against 45’s 62px stack. The dial at the top of this sheet rebuilds all four exhibits live; try 4 against 12 to see what twelve costs in thumbnail size.
  4. The wash (A, C). .mk-veil is the card’s own --tb-paper at .58, clipped to the stretch ahead of the caret — a continuous reading laid over discrete cells, so progress is smooth even though the pictures step. It is the bar graph’s --done clip, moved onto the photographs. It costs one absolutely-positioned div and no extra height. Open question: on the blueprint paper the wash is a blue veil over blue line-work — check it before committing (the paper button on each exhibit switches).
  5. The ruler (all three). Two repeating gradients keyed off --nf, which the control writes onto .mk-bar as an inline custom property. Majors at the frame boundaries mean the ruler and the filmstrip agree by construction at any frame count — including in C, whose 2px paper gaps widen the period to calc((100% + 2px) / var(--nf)) so the majors land in the gaps rather than drifting into the pictures. The three minors are decoration, not a unit — a mark is not divisible, so no tick can honestly stand for a quarter of one. If that bothers us, the minors go and the majors stay. Weights are --tb-tick / --tb-tick-2, which default to the card’s own rule colours; if the ticks read thin over dark line-work those are the two knobs.
  6. The red mark. The current cell keeps 45’s red outline at the thinned weight (1px, ~52%, inset so it never shifts the row). With the caret standing on the strip it is arguably redundant — but it is the thing that survives when the caret is parked on a boundary, and the owner asked to keep it.
  7. Cost, unchanged. n × (elements + 1–2 CSSAnimations each), built in place and never moved (re-inserting an element restarts its animations from zero and the frame loses its moment). Build time prints to the console as [mk46]. Fewer frames than 45 by default, so the phone spike on a 600-mark drawing gets smaller, not bigger.
  8. Still open. (0) A or B — the wash on the photographs, or the inked line on the floor. They are now identical in everything else, so this is the whole remaining call. (a) Does the last cell — always the finished drawing, always identical to the plate above it — earn its width, or should the strip run 1…n−1 and let the plate be its own last frame? (b) A hover preview on the strip (show that moment on the plate without moving the caret) is nearly free here and would make the strip a browser as well as a scrubber. (c) On a phone the strip is the plate’s width minus 38px; at 6 frames that is ~46px tall — readable, but the cells are ~52px wide, which is about the smallest a thumbnail of this line-work survives.
