Junk Drawer · Mockup 8B · The Arcade Dome

8B — THE ARCADE DOME. A jumbo candy-translucent arcade cabinet button in a chrome bezel, printed label ring reading TAKE A TURN, faint internal LED attract-pulse on idle, mechanical clunk-depress plus a glow flash on press — the most machine of the five, tied to the site’s arcade lineage.

Notes · what I committed to, what implementation must watch

1 · The label ring repeats three times, on purpose. A pile object scatters at a random angle and the visitor can rotate it, so a single arced TAKE A TURN would spend most of its life upside-down. Three repetitions at 120° mean some instance is always within 60° of upright, and a gold pip separates them. Glyphs are placed individually (each its own rotated <text>) rather than on a textPath, so ring fit is exact on every font stack instead of depending on metrics that vary per platform.

2 · Tap target at drawer scale. The painted disc is the whole object — 100% of the box width, no dead margin — so the hit area equals the rendered size: ~46 px on a 375 px phone well, ~90 px on desktop. That clears the 44 px minimum, but only just, and only because the artwork is a full-bleed circle. If implementation ever tightens the medium tier or adds the id-hashed jitter downward (−9% linear → ~42 px), the object needs a sizeScale nudge above 1 rather than a bigger transparent box — transparent padding buys nothing under silhouette hit-testing.

3 · Label legibility is a hero-scale luxury, not the affordance. At 46 px the ring text is ~3 px tall and reads as machined texture, which is fine: the thing that says "press me" is the silhouette — chrome annulus, fat glossy dome, specular hot-spot upper-left. Treat the ring as ornament that rewards the zoom, and do not chase legibility by enlarging the type; bigger type eats the dome, and the dome is the button.

4 · The idle tell is CSS, not SMIL. Nothing animated is baked into the SVG — the attract pulse and the press are page-level keyframes targeting .ad-led, .ad-cap, .ad-flash, .ad-ring. That keeps the asset sanitizer-clean (no script, no on*, no foreignObject, no external refs — the template below was lifted out and run through api/jd-svg-sanitizer.php, which returns ok) and, more importantly, keeps prefers-reduced-motion in charge: CSS cannot switch SMIL off. The reduced state here is the BASE state — a static faint glow and an instant depressed pose — and motion is added back only inside no-preference; the checkbox above simulates the setting.

5 · Z-order and the glow. The press flash lives inside the object's own box (the impact ring peaks at r=93 of a 100 half-box), so it never paints outside the item and never needs to escape .jd-pile's stacking context — no z-index fight with the wall shade, varnish or vignette above it. Consequence to accept: the varnish and vignette tint the flash slightly, and an item dragged on top of the dome will cover the pulse. That is the intended bargain from §1 — the visitor may bury it.

6 · One host, one asset. The interactive element is the wrapper div (role="button", tabindex="0", aria-label, Enter/Space), itself pointer-events: none with visiblePainted on the ink — identical to .jd-item, so the object drops into the existing gesture wiring without special-casing, and the square viewBox's transparent corners keep passing scroll through to the wood. The template in this page's source is the candidate production asset verbatim; the page only namespaces its ids per copy.
