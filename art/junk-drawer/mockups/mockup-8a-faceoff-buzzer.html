4 · Notes

  • What I committed to. A near-elevation 3/4 read, not plan view. The drawer's hardware is strict plan view, but its specimens are not (the toilet, the rat, the urn are all drawn as objects), and 8a only works if you can see the dome stand proud of the base and read the nameplate on the base's front. The mismatch is the camouflage the proposal asks for.
  • Chunk over realism. Dome half-width 147 against a base half-width 120 — the dome is 1.23× its base, which is the cartoon tell that makes it look slappable. The dome geometry is deliberately seated so its lower bulge always hangs below the base's top ellipse: no background ever shows through the joint at any x. If the base is widened past ~120 in implementation, that gap opens up.
  • Tap target and label legibility at drawer scale. The live readout above the variants states both. At a desktop well (~1060 stage, 64px walls) the buzzer lands near 90px wide — comfortably past the 44px floor, and the dome alone is ~42px tall. On a 375px phone the same ruler gives ~57–63px wide and a ~30px-tall dome: still a legal hit box because the whole silhouette (dome + base) is the target, but the dome by itself is not. Production hit-testing is ink-only (pointer-events: visiblePainted), so the base's ink counts — do not shrink the base to a sliver. The nameplate survives further down than I expected: at ~90px it is readable if you look at it, and only around ~60px does it degrade into a lettered brass strip. That is why the plate is 208 of 300 viewBox units wide with textLength pinned — do not narrow it for prettiness, and do not swap in a lighter weight.
  • The burst leaves the viewBox by ~8%. Rays flare past the box top and sides on purpose (the resting art fills the box edge-to-edge so the ink-bounds check passes; the transient flare could not also fit). Implementation must give the turn item's svg overflow: visible — production .jd-item svg does not set it, and the UA default clips. If it's forgotten the press still works and only the flare gets trimmed, so this degrades safely. The burst group carries pointer-events="none" in the asset because an opacity: 0 shape still hit-tests.
  • Idle tell is page CSS, not SMIL. The glint is a static bright slash clipped to the dome; the 8s sweep lives in a keyframe so prefers-reduced-motion can switch it off — SMIL baked into the asset could not be. Under reduced motion the press also drops to a discrete held state (dome down, base bulged, rays on) rather than a spring: motion reduced, acknowledgement kept. Honest doubt: at ~60px in a busy pile, an 0.8s glint every 8s may be too quiet to do the discoverability job §1 asks of it. Worth watching on-device before we trust it as the only tell.
  • Z-order and the pile. The buzzer sits at the top of the z-order here (the first-visit boost) and under the varnish + vignette layers, exactly as production stacks them — those are pointer-events: none, so they tint it without stealing the press. Note the vignette does dull the cap noticeably in the drawer's lower-left corner, which is precisely where §1 wants the initial scatter; the amber variant is the cheap answer if the red reads too muddy down there.
