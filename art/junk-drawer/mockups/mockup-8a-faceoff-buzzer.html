4 · Notes

  • Round 2: redrawn in plan view. Round 1 filed this candidate in ¾ elevation and the owner rejected the viewpoint; §2 now makes plan view binding, and the object above is a fresh top-down drawing, not the old one tipped over. Everything that carried depth by standing up now carries it by radius: bakelite body (r 147) → knurled grip band (r 119.5) → brass collar (r 112) → the dark well (r 97) → the dome (r 92), all concentric on 150,150, all shaded from one upper-left key. The only cast shadow is the pool the host's drop-shadow lays around the footprint. The design language survived intact — same reds, same brass and bakelite, same engraved plate, same squash-and-flare personality; what changed is that you are now looking down at it.
  • The press is travel, not squash. In elevation the dome sank and squashed. From above a sinking dome has exactly one honest reading: it gets smaller and the dark well ring around it gets wider — 5 viewBox units of ring at rest, 12 at the bottom of the stroke (dome scale 0.925). The specular tightens with it (.bz-gloss scales further, to 0.82) because a gloss surface moving away from the light gives a smaller, harder highlight. The base ring bulges 2% to catch the impact and the starburst — now radial, twelve rays and four sparks all the way round — flares from the whole footprint. No group translates: a translate from directly above would read as the buzzer skating across the wood, which is the one thing it must not do.
  • Where the label went, and what it cost. Plan view removes the base's front face, which is where the nameplate lived. Two options: bend the words around the ring (8b's answer) or lay a plate flat on the base. I took the plate — a moulded bakelite flange at the lower edge carrying the brass nameplate — because straight horizontal type stays readable about a third smaller than arc type does, and because it gives the silhouette a "front", which a bare disc has none of. It also keeps 8a visibly distinct from 8b at pile scale, where both are otherwise a red circle in a metal ring. The cost is that the footprint is no longer a clean circle (aspect 300 × 340, so the pile ruler files it a touch narrower) and that the flange is the one place the object could be read as "a thing with a handle" rather than "a buzzer".
  • Tap target and legibility at drawer scale. The live readout under the drawer states both, and plan view helps here: the whole silhouette is now one fat, near-round, entirely-painted target instead of a wide-but-short elevation, and the dome alone — the part that looks pressable — is 184 of 300 viewBox units across rather than a 30px-tall sliver. At a desktop well the buzzer lands near 80px wide with a ~50px dome; on a 375px phone it is ~55–60px wide with a ~34px dome, and the dome alone is close to the 44px floor while the full ink clears it easily. Production hit-testing is ink-only (pointer-events: visiblePainted), so the base ring and the flange count as target — do not thin them. The nameplate is 184 of 300 viewBox units wide with textLength pinned at 152: readable if you look at it around 80px, degrading to a lettered brass strip near 55px. Do not narrow it for prettiness and do not swap in a lighter weight. And note the plate rotates with the item, exactly like the pencil's paint and the card's pips — text lying flat on the drawer floor is the drawer's own convention, not a compromise.
  • The burst leaves the viewBox by ~13%. Radial rays flare past all four sides on purpose (the resting art fills the box edge-to-edge — the body disc reaches within 1% of the left, right and top edges and the flange within 0.6% of the bottom — so the ink-bounds check passes; the transient flare could not also fit). Implementation must give the turn item's svg overflow: visible — production .jd-item svg does not set it, and the UA default clips. If it's forgotten the press still works and only the flare gets trimmed, so this degrades safely. The burst group carries pointer-events="none" in the asset because an opacity: 0 shape still hit-tests.
  • Idle tell is page CSS, not SMIL. The glint is a static bright slash clipped to the dome circle; the 8s sweep lives in a keyframe so prefers-reduced-motion can switch it off — SMIL baked into the asset could not be. Under reduced motion the press drops to a discrete held state (dome shrunk into the well, ring widened, base bulged, rays on) rather than a spring: motion reduced, acknowledgement kept. Honest doubt, unchanged by the redraw: at ~55px in a busy pile, an 0.8s glint every 8s may be too quiet to do the discoverability job §1 asks of it. Worth watching on-device before we trust it as the only tell. Plan view does buy a little of that back — the red disc is a rounder, higher-contrast blob against the wood than the old elevation silhouette was.
  • Z-order and the pile. The buzzer sits at the top of the z-order here (the first-visit boost) and under the varnish + vignette layers, exactly as production stacks them — those are pointer-events: none, so they tint it without stealing the press. Note the vignette does dull the cap noticeably in the drawer's lower-left corner, which is precisely where §1 wants the initial scatter; the amber variant is the cheap answer if the red reads too muddy down there. Plan view also fixes a round-1 stacking oddity: an elevation object in a top-down pile had to be either in front of or behind its neighbours with no honest answer for overlap. Lying flat, it overlaps the pencil and the key exactly the way two flat things do.
