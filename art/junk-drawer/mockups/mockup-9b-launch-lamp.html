Junk Drawer · Mockup 9B · The Launch Lamp

9B — THE LAUNCH LAMP. A jumbo illuminated pinball launch button: translucent cherry-red lens in a chunky matte-black bezel, the label printed in dark ink on the lens itself so the backlight cuts it into silhouettes. The only thing in the drawer that emits light — and the fattest, most slappable target of the three candidates.

1 · Hero — press it

click / tap, or focus and hit Enter / Space
idle: pinball attract mode — a double-blink every ~6.5 s

 

the press — the lens travels down into the bezel (it shrinks; the dark seam ring around it widens from a hairline to a moat), beats at the bottom of the stroke, springs back with a small overshoot · the backlight flashes white-hot, the halo pops, and an impact ring snaps off the bezel · under reduced motion the same press is an instant sunk pose with no keyframes at all, and the idle blink is gone

3 · Lens variants — one custom-property swap

cheap variants — seven custom properties (--lamp-core/hot/mid/deep/rim/halo/spill) recolor lens, backlight, halo, rim-light and impact ring together; the black bezel, the seam and the dark label ink never change · exactly the db-b0..4 pattern: stops carry classes, a variant is a few stop-color overrides on a host class · red is the recommendation — it is the reference part, and red light on warm brown wood is the loudest possible "this is not junk"

Notes · what I committed to, what implementation must watch

1 · The glow is the affordance, not the label. Every previous candidate fought the camouflage problem with shape or story; this one breaks the physics of the drawer instead — nothing else in the pile emits light. The halo (a radial gradient inside the SVG, fading to fully transparent before the viewBox edge) spills colored light past the black bezel onto the wood, and the rim-light on the bezel's inner edge (.la-rimlight) sells the lens as the source. At 52 px the arched text is texture, and that is fine: a lit red disc in a black ring on brown wood needs no caption.

2 · The arched label is a textPath, and that is safe here only because the button never rotates. 8b placed glyphs one at a time precisely because a pile item scatters at random angles; the 2026-08-10 revision fixed the trigger at 0° in the bottom-left corner, so a single upright arch is now guaranteed upright forever. The two arc paths (la_arcTop, upper semicircle running left→right over the top; la_arcBot, lower semicircle with sweep 0 so TURN renders upright, tops toward center) are ids and get re-prefixed per copy like every other id. Glyph metrics vary per font stack, so both arcs carry ~30% slack past the centered word; the words are silhouettes, so a few px of drift never breaks the read.

3 · Ink bounds vs. size math. The halo counts as ink and reaches r=99 of the 100 half-box (0.5% worst-side margin), but the BEZEL disc reaches r=86 — the solid object is 86% of the box, so the area-normalized tier math is not fooled into rendering a mostly-empty box. The hit area is deliberately the bezel only: the halo, shadow, flash and ring carry pointer-events="none" in the asset (advisory) and an enforcing page rule (binding, same arrangement as turn-object.svg's .db-flash), so light never steals a drag meant for the wood beside the button. 86% of 52 px ≈ 45 px on a phone — over the 44 px floor, with production's unconditional min-width: 44px backstop still behind it.

4 · The idle tell is a blink, not a breath. Real pinball attract behavior: the lens snaps bright and the halo swells, twice in quick succession, every ~6.5 s — insistent, because this candidate's whole thesis is refusing to camouflage. It is CSS, not SMIL (la-blink-core / la-blink-halo against .la-glowcore / .la-halo), so prefers-reduced-motion stays in charge: the BASE state is the reduced state (steady glow, instant sunk press pose), motion is added back only inside no-preference, and the checkbox above simulates the setting. The asset itself is sanitizer-clean: no script, no on*, no foreignObject, no external refs, no SMIL.

5 · The press is travel. The lens group scales down inside the fixed bezel and the dark seam ring widens from a hairline to a moat — the bezel visibly overlaps more of the lens, which is what makes it read as a plunge rather than a wobble — then a white-hot flash, an expanding impact ring off the bezel, and a springy overshoot home. Everything paints inside the object's own box (the ring peaks at r≈97), so nothing escapes .jd-pile's stacking context; the varnish and vignette tint the glow slightly, and junk dragged on top can cover it for the session — the accepted §1 bargain. One watch-item: wrapper drop-shadow works off the alpha channel, so the semi-transparent halo casts a faint ghost; this page ships the lamp a gentler shadow than .jd-item's default, and implementation should carry that override.

6 · One host, one asset. The interactive element is the wrapper div (role="button", tabindex="0", aria-label, Enter/Space), itself pointer-events: none with visiblePainted on the ink — identical to .jd-item. The template in this page's source is the candidate production asset verbatim; the page only namespaces its la_ ids per copy (l1_, l2_, …), and since every class uses the la- hyphen prefix, the id rewrite can never touch a class the stylesheet depends on.
