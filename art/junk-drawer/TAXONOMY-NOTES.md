# Taxonomy notes — presenting and sharing the rubric

The owner's working notes for explaining the Junk Drawer's grading taxonomy
to other people: exemplar responses that make an axis legible, framings that
land, caveats worth saying out loud. Repo-only (deploy excludes `.md`); this
is a notebook, not servable data. Add entries at the top of their section
with a date.

## Exemplars, by axis

### Boundary rulings (2026-10-02, taxonomy v35)

The owner's rulings when Paintwork arrived — each issue axis is named by the
edit that fixes it (redraw / move on x-y / restack or erase / repaint in
place), and these two cases are the ones worth showing a reader:

- **Every cast shadow is Paintwork's.** A shadow is light, not a thing and
  not a setting — so a shadow that contradicts the light, and the pooled
  shadow beneath the subject that every model was told to leave out, are
  both repainting jobs. The one exception: a shadow that has grown into
  ground the subject stands on is a setting, and goes to Layering. Good
  exemplar: a drawing that is right in every other way but sits on a soft
  grey ellipse — Paintwork Minor (faint) or Major (heavy), nothing else
  docked.
- **Setting or thing?** The test for anything nobody asked for. A
  *setting* — ground, sky, water, horizon, a backdrop, a vignette, a frame —
  is Layering's (erase it). A *thing* — an extra object, an accessory,
  decoration, sparkles, a floating label or caption — is Understanding
  Assignment's, wherever it sits; even one that fits makes the drawing
  Mostly Understands at best. (The owner rejected an earlier "can you erase
  it and leave the subject whole?" test that put floating labels and
  decoration on Layering.) Good exemplar pair: the same prompt drawn once on
  a grassy patch (Layering) and once with a stray butterfly beside it
  (Understanding Assignment).

### Layering

- **2026-08-30 — GPT-5.1's oil-painted eyeball (googly-eyeball rerun) is a
  great example of a CRITICAL failure on the Layering axis.** An otherwise
  great SVG that is poorly layered — and the filed ratings say exactly that:
  the owner's blind scores were top marks on every other axis
  (Understanding Assignment 4/4, Structural Coherence 3/3 — the v1,
  3-point axis, now defunct — Je ne sais quoi 3/3, overall Select) against
  Layering 1/3, big problems. Useful when
  presenting because it shows the axes are genuinely independent: a drawing
  can grasp the brief, hold together structurally, and still fail purely on
  stacking order — which is why Layering earns its own axis instead of
  folding into coherence. (Item `2026-08-01-googly-eyeball`, rerun
